# Voice-over

- **Voice-over (picked in a spike 2026-10-24; the game side: [Localization and voice](../design/localization-and-voice.md), what's left in [`planned/voice.md`](../planned/voice.md)):** **Qwen3-TTS** (Apache-2.0, code and weights): `Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-VoiceDesign` makes a character's anchor takes from its description, `Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-Base` clones every line from the picked anchor, one anchor per character and language (about 25 s per line on a 16 GB GPU). Picked by ear over six rounds (Brannik and Nell, English and German, about 140 takes rated blind with half stars and notes): Qwen3-TTS cloned best in both languages (5 of 5 in English, 4½ and 5 in German); VoxCPM2 (Apache-2.0) designed natural English voices but cloned worse and its German voices failed; MOSS-VoiceGenerator (Apache-2.0) had accents and a low quality; Chatterbox multilingual (MIT) cloned second best, a bit fast. Cloning German from an English anchor kept a heavy English accent with every model, converting a native German take to the English anchor's timbre (Chatterbox voice conversion) lowered the quality, and Kartoffelbox (a German Chatterbox fine-tune) was the weakest: so German gets its own anchor. Ruled out before listening: non-commercial weights (XTTS-v2, F5, Higgs Audio v3, OmniVoice, Echo-TTS, Voxtral TTS, Breeze TTS 2) and Fish Audio Drama 3 (a revenue cap and a "Built with Fish Audio" duty, and too big for a 16 GB GPU). **Setup:** a second portable ComfyUI next to the image one (its own Python packages, so neither breaks the other) with the TTS Audio Suite custom nodes, started with `PYTHONUTF8=1` (the suite logs emoji) on port 8189 (env var `VOICE_COMFYUI_URL`); Qwen3-TTS runs in the suite's "shared runtime" (Transformers 4), which its `install.py` fails to build under the portable Python (its `._pth` makes `utils` resolve to ComfyUI's own): build it with `install_runtime(get_runtime_profile('vibevoice_transformers4_shared'))` from `utils.runtimes`, the suite's folder first on `sys.path`. VoxCPM2 runs in its own Python environment (env var `VOXCPM_PYTHON`). The suite's bundled example voices imitate real people and are never used. **Tools** (`tools/voice/`): `characters.py` (the descriptions and test lines), `round1.py` (voice-design takes; a character's `description_de` replaces the English description for German takes where it names an accent), `round2.py` (anchors cloned onto a character's lines, joined per anchor and model), `convert.py` (voice conversion), `rate.py` (the listening page on port 8191: half stars, notes, takes anonymous until rated, a Best view across rounds, duels between tied favorites, keyboard rating, each character's takes grouped by language; a Settled view plays each voiced NPC's anchor and every rendered line per language, as the game has them). Takes and ratings stay out of git in `local/voice/` (the SQLite database `voice.db` records what made each take). Generated voice lines count as pre-generated AI content for Steam's disclosure. **Voices without real people:** no recordings of real people as references (also no public-domain readings: their readers never agreed to being cloned); a voice comes from a model's designed or built-in voices, and where a model needs a reference clip, it's another model's synthetic voice. **Writing a description** (what the ratings taught): age, voice (pitch, texture), pace and temper, and the scene ("talking to a crew member across the counter"); "plain and conversational rather than narrating" (without it, voices read like a narrator); "native English speaker with a neutral accent" (without it, some came out with an accent); for female voices, warm but not breathy or seductive. Several seeds per description, picked by ear; voices that fit someone else are kept as spares. A German voice to match an English one: measure the English anchor's pitch and describe it ("a light tenor, not a baritone"), since a plain description gave Dace a baritone in German; cloning the English anchor by its speaker embedding alone (x-vector) came closest, with a light accent left.
- **Voice lines (in the game):** `tools/voice/anchor.py <npc id> <language> <round>/<character>/<file>` makes a rated take an NPC's anchor: `assets/voice/source/<npc id>/anchor_<language>.flac` (ignored by Godot) and what made it in `voice.json` there (model, seed, description, the text it speaks, which take). `tools/voice.sh [npc id ...] [--prune] [--dry-run]` (env vars `VOICE_PYTHON`, a Python with numpy and soundfile, and `VOICE_COMFYUI_URL`) renders every line of every NPC with anchors, in English and from `locale/<language>.po`; which lines an NPC says, the game tells (`VoiceLines.lines_by_speaker`, written by `tools/voice_lines.gd` run headless, so the voice tools need `GODOT4_EDITOR` too): every resource of a type in `VoiceLines.SPEAKING` under `game/` gives its lines by who says them (`spoken_lines()`: an `NpcProfile` its prompts, the greeting's, the topics' with their guest lines and the ones for elsewhere, its tour's, reactions' and remarks), so a line in another file (a quest's hand-in) counts for the NPC who says it; each is cloned from the language's anchor (Qwen3-TTS Base, seed 1), trimmed, leveled (-20 dBFS RMS, peaks under -1 dBFS) and written as Ogg Vorbis to `assets/voice/<npc id>/<language>/<hash>.ogg` (Git LFS), the hash being the first 16 hex digits of the SHA-256 of the English line, so `VoiceLines` finds a line's file from its text without an index. `manifest.json` next to the anchors records what made each file (the text spoken, the anchor's hash, the model, the seed): a changed line, a new anchor or a new model renders it again, and files of lines that are gone are listed (`--prune` deletes them). A line that came out badly gets another seed: `tools/voice/reroll.py <npc id> <language> "<English line>" [seed ...]` renders candidates onto the listening page (round `reroll-<npc id>-<language>`, seed 1 being the one in the game), and the picked one goes into `voice.json` (`"reroll": {"<hash>": <seed>}` under its language). About 16 s per line on a 16 GB GPU; run it after changing an NPC's lines, then let Godot import the files before the tests (`tests/npcs/test_npc_voice.gd` fails on a line without its recording or a recording without its line). A new voice language goes into `VoiceLines.LANGUAGES` once its lines are rendered. **What's missing or left over:** `tools/voice_status.sh [npc id ...] [--prune] [--unvoiced]` (Python's standard library only, no GPU) lists per voiced NPC and language the lines without a recording (missing), the recordings made from something else than now (outdated: text, translation, anchor, model or seed, named), lines without a translation, and what's stale: recordings whose line changed or is gone (with the text they were made from), `.import` files without their recording and manifest entries of gone lines; `--prune` deletes the stale ones. It also lists the NPCs without anchors with their line counts (`--unvoiced`: the lines too) and exits with 1 while anything is missing, outdated, untranslated or stale. `tools/voice/lines.py` is the shared part (which lines an NPC has, from the game, the hashes, the manifest) that `render.py`, `status.py`, `rate.py` and `reroll.py` use; `tests/npcs/test_npc_voice.gd` checks the recordings against the same lines.
