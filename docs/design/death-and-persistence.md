# Death and persistence

- Health shows on the suit ring's left arc (see [UI](ui.md)) and, for teammates, on a light band around the collar: green, amber below half, red and blinking below a quarter, flashing white on damage. Greybox: 100 HP, **TBD (playtest)**.
- On death: the body drops where it stands, **everything carried this run is lost**, and the player respawns in the medical bay with full health.
- When every player is dead, the mission is lost: the countdown turns grey and says LOST, and after a few seconds (greybox: 3 s, **TBD (playtest)**) everyone respawns aboard and the ramp goes up. The next run starts with a newly picked mission.
- Co-op: the mission ends when every player is either back aboard or dead, as long as at least one made it back. The dead lose what they carried and respawn aboard when it ends (planned with co-op: going down first and being revived by a teammate standing close, and respawning after 30 s instead of waiting, see [`planned/coop-revive.md`](../planned/coop-revive.md)).
- When every player dies, the miners on the planet are lost too (see [Miners](mining.md)).
- **Not too punishing:** losses should hurt, but not set players back for long.
- Planned skills soften it (see [`planned/skills.md`](../planned/skills.md)): the **rescue drone** revives a player where they fell once per run, in solo and in co-op, so playing alone isn't unfair without anyone to revive you; the **hardened canister** keeps part of the carried ore on death.
- Back aboard (before the storm gets you): carried resources go into the hold and are spent at the upgrade stations. Every player has their own hold, in their own save (also when they play on someone else's ship), and every run's whole haul goes into each player's hold (see [Co-op progression](progression.md#co-op-progression)). The hold keeps growing over runs. It shows in the cargo bay as silo clusters (greybox until the cargo bay's art pass): an industrial silo per ore of the game in a fixed spot, ordered by rarity, with a window strip where the ore glows up to its fill level (full at 3000, 1500 and 600 units for common, mid and signature ores, **TBD (playtest)**) and above it, at the top of the silo's body, a display with the exact amount in big digits in the ore's color (the ore's name small below), readable from the normal camera; it sits high enough to show over the silo in front of it, and amounts of more than four digits get smaller to fit. Each band is a cluster around a pump hub with pipes to the silos' tops: the common and mid clusters (2 × 2 big silos each, about 2 m and 1.6 m wide, 5 m and 4 m tall) behind the main lane, the commons by the ramp; the signature cluster (2 × 3 smaller silos) in front of the lane, set back from the lane and the ramp so the way in from the ramp stays open. A cluster's far row is shifted half a step sideways, so from the isometric camera no silo hides another's window. The main lane (at least 4 m), the way between the clusters, the way to the lift, the ramp button and the floor signs (moved to fit) stay clear; players can't walk through the silos. Rarer ores get smaller silos. An ore never brought aboard shows as a dark silo with a grey "?" and no name (it stays known once found, even when spent), so the hold doesn't give away what's still to find. The silos are used directly: `interact` next to one opens the hold as a station with it selected; the camera follows the selected silo, and the card shows the ore's name, its band, how much the hold has, the open regions where it's found (in more than four, the planets and moons instead, so the card keeps its size with every planet open) and, for an ore harder than a first drill mines at full speed, the drill level that does ("Full speed with drill MK3."). `tab_prev`/`tab_next` step through the silos in reading order (rows from the back, each from left to right) the directions to the nearest silo, furnace or rack shelf on screen. The ramp's countdown hides meanwhile, since it hangs over the silos by the ramp. The silos show the hold of the player using them, else the one of the player the camera follows (each player sees their own). The refinery stands in the cargo bay's front corner by the door to the lower deck (see [Resources](resources.md)); the cargo bay's floor sign moved in front of it.
- **Health carries over** between runs (and is saved with the player): a player keeps what they came back with. The four **med beds** in the medical bay heal, for free: `interact` at a bed (its foot or its sides) lies the player down on it (the weapon put away), and health fills up at a rate that heals from nothing to full in 3 s (**TBD (playtest)**); its card shows the health filling up ("Healing: 62 / 100", a bar, then "Full health") and "Get up". Any player can use any bed, one player per bed. The first time the player stands at the ramp button hurt (with a mission picked), a bubble above it says "Not at full health: the medical bay is at the nose." (once per player, saved with the player: in co-op every player sees it once, whoever's ship it is). Dying still means respawning in the medical bay with full health. The medic (Ilse) explains it, standing between the beds.
- **Saving:** automatic, no save button. Progress (the player's hold, the heat shield and where the ship is, the owned weapons, the loadout, the upgrade levels) is saved whenever it changes aboard; what changes during a mission is saved once it's over, so quitting mid-mission loses that run like dying (the game starts aboard in space again). There is one save. **Reset progress** on the settings terminal wipes it: it asks three times, each time saying that all progress is lost for good, and each confirm button waits a moment (greybox: 1 s, **TBD (playtest)**) so quick repeated presses can't get through. Only while the player is alone: with others in the session the button is disabled with a hint, and everyone keeps their progress. The settings (language) and the statistics stay.
- **Statistics:** the game keeps statistics of what each player does on their own machine (kills per weapon and strain, damage dealt and taken with its causes, deaths, reloads and their timing presses, ore collected, hauled and lost, miners, unlocks, upgrades and skills, runs and their outcomes, time aboard and outside), over all sessions and as a log per session, for playtesting and tuning (see [`technical/saving.md`](../technical/saving.md#statistics)). They're local files; nothing is sent anywhere. Planned: a live view for development, a statistics station aboard and achievements on top (see [`planned/stats.md`](../planned/stats.md)).
