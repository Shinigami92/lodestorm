# Characters and people

- **Characters:** a character `.glb` carries its skeleton and animations. The player (`assets/player/player.glb`) has a skeleton with Godot's humanoid bone names (`SkeletonProfileHumanoid`, no fingers, T-pose rest), so its clips could be retargeted later, and one skinned mesh, generated (see Generated models in [`assets.md`](assets.md)) in an A-pose and fitted by the build script: the skeleton's joints and bone lengths are the suit's (a joint table measured on the mesh), every vertex belongs to the bone nearest to it (the distance to the bone's segment minus the bone's radius; above the hips, what's behind the back only to the torso), blended with the neighbor bone within a band across each joint, faces joining parts that move apart (forearm and hips, arm and flank) are cut, and the mesh is moved into the T rest pose by the inverse of the skinning (the feet only turned, so the soles stay on the ground). The collar's glowing ring becomes `SuitLight`: a thin band following the collar (radius and height per direction around the neck), weighted like the suit under it, with the ring painted over in the texture; the other glowing cyan of the base color (lamps, the backpack's panel) becomes the emission map. The bake keeps the generated UV layout. `CargoRack` and `HosePort` are placed on the backpack measured on the mesh. The clips are keyframed by the build script, not by hand: every pose is a set of world-space rotations per bone plus two-bone IK for arms and legs (feet planted on the ground, hands on Harl's grips), sampled every frame at 30 fps, one Blender action and NLA track per clip. The posing and the clips everyone has (`Idle`, the run directions, `Walk`, `Die`) are shared with the NPCs in `tools/blender/humanoid.py` (with `sit()`, the suit's `Sit` for the colonists: upright on a seat behind the feet, the hands on the thighs): a `Body` is a skeleton's bones at rest plus the few measures the clips stand it on (the feet's and hanging wrists' places); rotations are relative to each limb's rest direction and the side its joint bends towards, so a body with another rest pose plays the same clips. The aim and melee poses stay in the player's script. Melee poses place a weapon by its grip: `hold()` turns the right hand so the `WeaponGrip` marker lands on a spot with the weapon pointing a given way (`fist()` for Clout's gauntlet: the fist along the punch), `left_on()` puts the left fist on a point of the weapon given in its scene's axes; a shield (`WeaponType.Hold.SHIELD`) hangs on the `ShieldGrip` marker on the left forearm instead (the player's `ShieldMount`); `player_build.py clips` renders workbench contact sheets of the melee clips with rough stand-in weapons on the grip. Loops are set in the game (`PlayerAnimator` builds its `AnimationTree` in code), so the `.glb.import` stays default. Markers the game uses are empties on bones (Godot imports them under a `BoneAttachment3D`), and the scene's nodes follow them through a `RemoteTransform3D`, so their paths stay stable. The model faces glTF +Z (Blender -Y), the player's `Body` turns it by 180°. Aliens (`assets/aliens/<strain>/<strain>.glb`) are built by one script per strain (`assets/aliens/<strain>/source/<strain>_build.py` and `_bake.py`) on the shared creature kit `tools/blender/creature.py` (the skeleton from a bone table, the organic mesh builder weighting to the two nearest bones, body profiles and plates hugging them, the hive's materials Sinew, Chitin, Bone and Mouth, posing and clip baking), so every strain reads as one species; strains with extra channels (the spitter's sac scales) bake their own clips. The swarmer is generated instead (see Generated models): its build script takes the skeleton's joints from a table measured on the mesh, in the mesh's own standing pose (the rest pose, so nothing is moved), weights every vertex to its nearest bone like the player's suit, cuts the lower jaw from the skull (except at the hinge), and puts the `Eyes` where the texture's eyes glowed; its blades hang from the `Neck`. They have their own skeleton: `Body` (moves the whole trunk), `Hips` with the tail and the hind legs, `Spine`, `Chest` with the front legs and the scythes, `Neck`, `Head`, `Jaw`; legs are `<Front|Mid|Hind><Left|Right><UpperLeg|LowerLeg|Foot>` (the swarmer, warden, gnawer, brute and shrieker have four: front elbows bend back, hind knees forward, the hind `Foot` is the long metatarsal; mite, spitter, burrower, bloater and tender have six, the middle ones under `Spine`, every knee bent out at rest), plus strain bones (the mandibles, a `Sac` with scale keys on the spitter, the shrieker (under `Neck3`, its neck has three bones) and the bloater, the tender's `Tendril<n>A/B/C/Tip`). Meshes: `Creature` (the baked body), `Eyes` and `Glands` (constant emissive, both flare during a wind-up). Their organic mesh is lofted along the bones and weighted to the two nearest bones; plates, teeth, claws and blades are rigid. The clips (`Idle`, `Run`, `Attack`, `Die`) only move the trunk, head, tail and blades; the legs come from `AlienLegs` (`game/aliens/alien_legs.gd`), a `SkeletonModifier3D` added under the model's `Skeleton3D` at runtime: every foot stays planted until the spot under its hip (plus half a step's travel) is too far away, then steps there in an arc, in two sets taking turns (four legs: the diagonal pairs, a trot; six: tripods, front and hind of one side with the middle of the other; the strain's `stride` scales the steps); a ray finds the ground under each new step (other characters skipped), and two-bone IK bends the upper and lower leg so the ankle sits above the foot, the foot keeping its animated angle. It works with the interpolated transform, so feet stay put between physics ticks. On death it switches off and `Die` folds the legs. `AlienAnimator` builds the alien's `AnimationTree` in code (idle/run blend by speed, attack one-shot, death). `AlienPose` moves the model's parent (`Body`) on top of the clips until the art pass brings clips for them: the crouch of a wind-up, the arc of a leap (the legs switch off in the air and plant again on landing), rearing up when staggered, a jolt per hit.
- **People:** an NPC is a MakeHuman body wearing a generated outfit. The body comes from MPFB, MakeHuman's Blender extension (installed from Blender's extension platform, version **2.0.18**), and its asset packs (installed in MPFB's "Apply assets" → "Library settings" → "Install asset pack"; used: the MakeHuman system assets, skins01, eyebrows01 and eyelashes01, all CC0, so the exported models are ours; a CC-BY asset would be credited in `assets/CREDITS.md`): it gives the face, the skeleton and its weights. What they wear (down to gloves and boots) and their hair are generated (see Generated models in [`assets.md`](assets.md)): TRELLIS.2 on an A-pose concept of the person, made from their rated concept. A person's build script (`assets/npcs/<name>/source/<name>_build.py [wip <tag>|final]`) describes the body as data (MPFB's macro sliders: gender, age, muscle, weight, proportions, height, race; a skin; eyes, brows and lashes) and how the outfit is fitted over it (a `dressing.Fit`: the generated model's file and what differs from the defaults, e.g. a skirt, the gloves, bare arms, its own head), then `tools/blender/dressing.py` makes the person (`make()`: the steps below, the renders and the export); `tools/blender/people.py` builds the body (`build()`: the sliders baked into the mesh), rigs it with MPFB's `game_engine` skeleton renamed to Godot's humanoid bone names (fingers included; MPFB's A-pose is the rest pose) and gives its parts export materials (`export_material()`: the texture retinted, alpha cut for brows and lashes, at most 1024, the skin 2048). The outfit is fitted over it (`load_outfit()`: scaled to the person's height with the hair, centered on the torso): its face and the generated skin around it are cut off (faces whose texture looks like the generated face's skin, from the chin up), the skin left (forearms, neck) is moved to the body's skin tone, its hair is scaled over the skull, the body's arms and legs are posed into the outfit's pose (`limb_directions()`, `pose_limbs()`: each elbow and wrist on the outfit's arm, the bones' lengths apart), the outfit's hands and its gloves' cuffs are cut at the body's wrists (the body's hands stay: their fingers have bones; Nell's fingerless gloves are painted on them), the outfit takes the weights of the posed body under it (`transfer_weights()`: the nearest four vertices; loose cloth below the waist, an apron, blends from the hips into both thighs by how far it hangs from the body, `skirt_weights()`), goes back into the rest pose with the body (`unskin()`), and the body's skin it covers is deleted (all but the head, the neck above the collar and the hands from inside the cuffs). People's poses take care of what their skeleton lacks: the forearm rolls with the hand (60 % of its twist; no twist bones, the wrist's skin would twist alone), a hand bends at most 45° from its forearm, and the fingers curl (`Pose.curl()`: loose when standing, closed around what's held). `body_of()` measures the `humanoid.Body` (where the feet stand, where the hanging hands are, which way the palms face), so the shared clips bake for every person: `Idle` and `Walk` (the other clips too when a person needs them). An empty `HeadTop` on the head marks the top of the hair (`NpcBody` puts the name plate and the bubbles above it). What a person holds while working is its own model (e.g. `assets/props/bar/`: a glass, a bottle, a rag, made by `source/bar_props_build.py`, each standing on the frame a hand holds it by: its origin where it's held, its up along the thumb's side): the person's skeleton has a bone per prop under a hand (`prop_grip()`: "Prop<Name>" with the empty "<Name>Grip"), the game puts the prop's model on the marker (`NpcProfile.props`) and the clips show it by the bone's scale (hidden in all others). A person's work at their place is clips of their own, built from key poses (`tools/blender/key_poses.py`, used by e.g. `assets/npcs/nell/source/nell_clips.py`: the lean and turn of the upper body, a crouch, where the head looks and per arm the wrist's target, the elbow's side, the hand's direction and its palm, blended between keys, starting and ending standing, with the places of the counter and the shelf); `NpcProfile.activities` names them and `NpcAnimator` plays one now and then (a one-shot over the standing). The crew share four of them (`tools/blender/fidgets.py`, merged into each crew member's `clips(body)` and rendered as checks by `work(body)`): `LookAround`, `Stretch` (hands behind the head), `CheckWrist` and `Tap` (at a console whose top is `CONSOLE_TOP` high, its edge `CONSOLE_EDGE` in front of the feet). `final` writes `assets/npcs/<name>/<name>.glb` and `source/<name>.blend`; renders go to `tmp/assets/npcs/<name>/` (front, side, back in rest, idle and walk; hands in rest and idle; face, forearm and waist close-ups). The game gets the model through the profile (`NpcProfile.model`, a path that `NpcBody` loads, so loading a profile, e.g. for its name, doesn't load the model); `NpcAnimator` plays its `Idle` and `Walk`. A person can also keep the generated head (`Fit.own_head`, Kade's: a face much closer to the concept than MakeHuman's, with its hair and beard): nothing is cut there, the head takes the Head bone's weight (blending into the neck just above its joint) and the body's head, eyes, brows and lashes go, the body's skin (its hands) takes the generated face's tone; such a face never blinks or moves its lips. A generated model may come with a dent (a pit or a tunnel into the surface, black in the render): `Fit.dents` names a point in front of or behind it and its radius, and the build cuts the dent's faces, moves the opening's edge onto the surface around it and caps it in the cloth's color there (Selin's coat; another TRELLIS seed is the other way out, but it changes the face too). Generated hands that splay far from the arm's line (Rook's) need a wider reach for the cut (`Fit.hand_reach`, 22 cm instead of 16), or pieces of their fingers stay and stretch when the body moves; hands hanging lower than the waist in the concept (Brannik's) need the arm's line found below it too (`Fit.arms_below`, 30 cm), or the body's wrist lands off the outfit's arm and most of the generated hand stays. Bare hands out of a bare forearm (Brannik, Dace, Hollis and Fenna, `gloved=BARE`): nothing dark is cut above the wrist (`cuff=0`), the forearm's cut end moves onto the wrist (`center_cuffs`) and narrows onto it over 6 cm (`taper_cuffs`: a wider open end shows its dark inside around the hand); a cap's shade makes the generated face's tone too light, so Fenna's hands take her forearms' tone instead (`forearm_tone`, their warm texels only). Made so far: Nell, Kade, Selin, Rook and the eight crew members (all but Nell with their own heads; see [`planned/people.md`](../planned/people.md) for the rest). **Making the next person** (about an hour of machine time): (1) re-render their rated concept in an A-pose with Z-Image (`tools/concepts/gen.py`, 6 seeds; the prompt describes the concept's look in detail plus the player's A-pose clause: arms out with a clear gap, empty open hands, a plain dark background, no holographic panels; Nell's is in `assets/npcs/nell/source/trellis/trellis.json`), pick the seed closest to the concept; (2) `tools/trellis.py <concept> <out.glb> --faces 80000 --texture 4096` and keep the concept, the model and a `trellis.json` in `assets/npcs/<name>/source/trellis/`; (3) copy `nell_build.py` or `kade_build.py` (the sliders, the skin, the export looks and the `Fit`) and, for work at their place, a clips file like `nell_clips.py`; (4) check the renders (`wip`: hands in every pose, the face, the waist), then `final`, wait for the import and give the profile its `model`, `activities` and `props`. Kade's whole gloves: the generated gauntlets stay (`cuff=0`: only the hands are cut), the glove is painted over every finger (`gloved=FULL`) and 5 cm up the forearm under the gauntlet (`glove_up`: no bare wrist shows when the hand bends); small pieces of the generated hands left beyond the cut (splayed fingers, within 30 cm of a wrist) are dropped; the gauntlets' ends move across the arm (fading in over 15 cm, `center_cuffs`) so the hand comes out of their middle (the bulky sleeves pulled the outfit's arm line, and so the body's wrist, to the top of the opening); without bare skin below the collar the tone shift stays off (`bare_arms=False`: it bleached his pale jacket); over his bulky outfit his hands hang wider (`Person.wrist_out`, 12 cm outside the shoulders instead of 5, clear of the belt's pouches). Pitfalls met with Nell: generated hands are fused and spread (cut them, keep the body's), the outfit's shoulders are wider than the body's (the elbow and the wrist go onto the outfit's arm, not just its direction), weights must be normalized before `unskin()` (or vertices stretch), loose-cloth weights must leave the hands out (they hang below the waist), the skin tone may only move on the arms and the neck (the apron's stains look like skin), and the generated back is a guess from the front view.
