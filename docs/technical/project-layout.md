# Project layout

The game lives in `game/`, one folder per feature: its scenes, scripts and resources together, so a feature is found (and moved, or worked on in parallel) in one place. Imported art stays in `assets/` (the Blender pipeline writes there), tests in `tests/`, mirroring `game/`.

Rules: a folder is named after a game concept, never `utils`/`common`/`core` (a shared helper goes to the feature it serves, or gets its own named folder like `models/`). A scene's tuning `.tres` sits next to it (e.g. `game/player/player_settings.tres`); a feature's content (one resource per ore, weapon, skill, ...) goes in a subfolder (`types/`, `tree/`, `crew/`). Later features (VR, co-op, platform services) get their own folders (`vr/`, `net/`, `platform/`). Developer shell scripts live in `tools/` (ignored by Godot via `.gdignore`).

**The ship** (`game/ship/ship.tscn`) is put together from parts, so the hull and each room can be changed (and later modeled) on their own:

- `hull.tscn`: the greybox hull (floors, walls with doors and windows, the upper deck's slab, roof, the nose's and the legs' collision shapes), generated by `game/ship/hull_build.py` (`python game/ship/hull_build.py`). Don't edit it by hand. The nose rooms' slanted walls are turned boxes, their floors, slab and roof CSG polygons with a convex collision shape; outer windows sit in niches (side walls, a lid, a floor piece).
- `Exterior`: the hull's model around the box (`assets/ship/ship_exterior.glb`, a blockout so far, built by `assets/ship/source/ship_exterior_build.py`: `wip <tag> [views] [legs]` renders previews to `tmp/ship_exterior/`, `final` exports the `.glb`) with `ShipExterior` (`game/ship/ship_exterior.gd`): it only casts shadows while the viewer is aboard and moves the six legs (`Leg1`..`Leg6`) down for the landing and up for flight.
- **The shell** (the art pass of the inside, the whole ship, `hull_layout.SHELL_REGIONS`): the greybox pieces keep only their collision shapes (but the lower deck's floor slab, the ship's outline on the holo table), and the kit's model of each cut-away group (`assets/ship/shell/<group>.glb`) sits under the group's node with `ShipShell` (`game/ship/ship_shell.gd`), which swaps its placeholder materials (`ShellPaint`, `ShellDark`, `ShellPlate`, `ShellGrating`, `ShellHazard`, `ShellMarking`, `ShellAccent`, `ShellLamp`) for the shell's (`game/ship/shell/*.tres`), puts it on the `RenderLayers.INTERIOR` layer (the cool rim light leaves it out: it casts no shadows, so it would shine through the cut-away roof) and gives a room's floor (an object named after the room) its `RoomLook`'s floor tint and the room's accents ("<room>Accent") its accent color as instance shader parameters (`game/ship/room_looks.tres`, by room name; `RoomLooks.get_look` drops a trailing number, so the cabins share one). The floor and its accents are objects of their own on purpose: instance shader parameters of different names share slots within one object, so the floor took the accent's color. `assets/ship/source/ship_shell_build.py` (headless Blender: `wip <tag>` renders the iso and an eye-level view with the game's textures to `tmp/ship_shell/`, `final` exports) imports `hull_build.py` for its pieces and rooms and dresses each piece in its own frame (the cargo bay's walls count as one room through both decks, the cockpit's under its lower ceiling, floors, slabs and niche lids stay plain cores): a painted core; on every face seen a 45° chamfer up to the ceiling with a light line in it and a slanted foot (`profile`); a round rim along the top of each wall the camera cuts, its top at the wall's (`rim`; heavy on the outer walls, slim between rooms; the stubs get one with a lamp line); heavy frames with rounded corners around doors (from the lintels) and niche openings (`rounded_frame`), hazard thresholds; walls cut away for the camera stay plain cores; the rooms' floors from `ROOMS` (one plate with amber edge markings, the nose rooms' following their outline, a grating strip down a corridor, an accent strip along an outer wall); rounded room corners (`corners`: fillets in each room's corners, the ones not on the back outer wall in the cut groups); the drive core's conduit along the lower deck's back wall (`conduit`); a lamp housing in the ceiling over each lamp (`fixtures`, with the ceiling's group). `assets/shaders/ship_shell.gdshader` projects a tileable set in the model's space (which is the ship's: floors from above, walls from the side they face) with hex tiling and a large brightness noise as the terrain's (`hex_tiling`, `macro_strength`; off for the grating), a tint, grime low on the walls (from the deck a point is on), optional hazard stripes, the room's tint, and the metalness from the albedo's alpha (`metal_from_texture`); `ship_accent.gdshader` is the accent's glow. The sets (`assets/ship/textures/<set>_albedo.png` with the metalness in A, `_nra.png`) are clean satin paint (3 m), a glossy epoxy floor (3 m) and grating (1 m), made by `assets/ship/source/ship_textures.py` (`tools/blender.sh ... [set ...] [preview]`: procedural Cycles materials baked the terrain's way, reusing `terrain_textures.py`; the grating with numpy). `hull_build.py` also writes the rooms' doors and lights from the layout: a `ShipDoor` in every doorway with a lintel (`doors()`; not the ramp's or the lifts' gates, `NOT_DOORS`), in the lintel's group, and per room (`room_lights()`) lamps in the ceiling about `LAMP_STEP` apart (the middle one the key light, with shadows in rooms of at least `SHADOW_AREA` m²), spots washing the back wall and a box-projected `ReflectionProbe` with the rooms' own ambient; the upper deck's in its group (off while the viewer is below). The rooms' scenes have no floor or lights of their own. With the map's screen-space reflections the floors mirror the lamps. Frame time aboard (4K desktop, development PC): about 9 ms (110 fps), the reflections about 0.2 ms of it; VR's budget is checked with M6.
- `neon_sign.tscn` (`NeonSign`, a tool script): a sign like neon tubes on a dark backing that grows with the translated text (`get_width`, `get_height`; lines split by line breaks) from its `anchor` (its center, or its left or right edge for a sign beside a door), in the Tilt Neon font (`assets/fonts/tilt_neon/`, SIL OFL; its license is on the credits page and exported through the presets' include filter), with an optional arrow (left of the text when it points left), a faint light on the wall and an optional flicker. Its parts are an ordinary child without an owner (not saved), so the ship's cut-away hides an upper-deck sign while the viewer is below. Every room's name (`Sign` in its scene) and every guide is one. `ship_door.tscn` (`ShipDoor`): two leaves sliding apart into the wall while one of the `ShipDoor.OPENERS` (a group: players, and colonists walking a route; people at their posts aren't in it) is within `reach` of the opening's bottom middle (in `open_time`, with `mission/door`), closing `close_delay` after the last one left; no collision. The hull builds a door into every room's doorway (see the shell above).
- `rooms/` (`lower/`, `upper/`, `cargo_bay.tscn`): a scene per room with its sign, props, stations and NPCs, edited by hand (its floor and lights are the hull's). `ship.tscn` places each at its floor center under `Rooms/Lower`, `Rooms/Upper` or `Rooms`, so node paths like `Rooms/Lower/Armory/Locker1` stay the same.
- `materials/`: materials used by more than one of these scenes (props, beds, lockers, the station's floor guides).
- `ship.tscn` itself keeps what belongs to the whole ship: the lifts, the guide signs between rooms (`Rooms/Lower/Guides`, `Rooms/Upper/Guides`), the ramp and its button, the boarding and respawn markers, the hold, the countdown and the flight.

The layout (deck heights, each room's position and size, the nose rooms' outlines, the window niches, the ramp door, the legs) and the skin of the hull's model (its section, the nose's taper: `skin_section`, `inside_skin`) are defined in `game/ship/hull_layout.py`, shared by `hull_build.py` and the exterior's build script: moving or resizing a room means changing it there, rebuilding the hull and the exterior (its windows), and moving the room's scene (and resizing its floor) to match. `hull_build.py` fails when a piece comes closer than 0.2 m to the skin's outer surface (the skin is 0.3 m thick). The `Interior` area (who is aboard) covers the box with its niches and, with a second shape, the nose rooms.

**Greybox builders** (`tools/greybox.py`, shared by `hull_build.py` and the stations' builders): a `Greybox` collects walls along x or z (with doors, a `front` part for walls in front of a corridor), turned walls between two points (`segment`), convex polygon prisms (`prism`), boxes and outer walls with windows (optionally in niches) (glass in `CutAway.HIDE_GROUP`), each in a group mapped to a parent node; walls in a cut-away group get a stub in an always-shown one. `write` keeps the scene's and its references' UIDs and fails with `--check` when the file differs. `CutAway.show` (`game/camera/cut_away.gd`) is the runtime half: a cut-away group only casts shadows, its lights and hidden nodes (glass, holograms) turn off.

**Stations** (`game/space_station/`): a station's map is a `SpaceStation` scene generated whole by its builder (`ostra/ostra_build.py` writes `ostra/ostra.tscn`: `Walls` with its `Cut` child, `Rooms` with floors, lights, signs and floor guides, `Clamps`, `Interior`), standing in ship-local axes like a location. Its `CelestialBody.map` makes the station dockable; the voyage offers a `MissionBrief` with `station` set (no region) and places the map as the location when the flight's docking starts (`ShipFlight.reached_location`); the station follows `ShipFlight.station_offset` and `clamp_amount` while docking and undocking. Rooms get hand-placed scenes (NPCs, the ore exchange) once they have more than greybox.
