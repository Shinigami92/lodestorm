# Localization and voice

- **Languages:** English and German first, more later. All player-facing text goes through translations from the start, also the text in the world (3D menus, labels, the ship's signs, the station's room names painted on its floors). German runs about a third longer than English, so panels and labels leave room for it. The game follows the system language; the settings terminal in cabin 1 picks another one and saves it (resetting keeps it). How it works: [`technical/localization.md`](../technical/localization.md).
- **Translations by contributors:** the project is on GitHub, so new languages and fixes come in as pull requests (one translation file per language, readable in a diff; the CLA applies).
- **Subtitles for every spoken line**, in every language, shown in the world, not on the screen: a speech bubble above the speaking NPC, radio lines next to the player. They work the same in VR. Names in them (people, the star and its planets, moons, stations and regions, also by a short name like "Ostra", weapons, ores and alloys) stand out in pale gold, so players notice what they can ask about or look up; cards keep them plain. Alien strains aren't highlighted: their names are common words (German "Heiler", "Wächter").
- **Voice-over:** generated ahead of time with a free AI text-to-speech model running locally (Qwen3-TTS, picked by ear, see [`technical/voice.md`](../technical/voice.md)), one audio file per line and voice language, with voices designed from a written description of each character, never cloned from real people. Every line of a character is cloned from one recording of their voice (the anchor), so they always sound like themselves; each voice language has its own anchor, made from the same description. Voiced so far: Brannik, Nell, Kade, Selin, Rook, Tamsin, Okoro, Ilse, Dace, Wren and Hollis, in English and German, every line; Fenna every line in English (so in German too, until her German voice comes). The NPC speaks each line from their head as its bubble shows; their prompt is spoken when a talk starts without the greeting. **Skipping is always allowed:** the voice never holds a player back, so moving on (`interact`, "Continue") cuts the line off at once and the next one starts, and leaving the topic or the talk stops it; players who read faster than the voice speaks go at their own pace, and nobody waits for a line to finish. A line without a recording just shows its bubble. **Talks can continue by themselves** ("Dialogue: Continue by itself" in the settings, off by default, each player's own; values **TBD (playtest)**): a voiced line moves on 1 s after its voice ends, a line without a voice after its reading time (2 s plus 0.06 s per letter of the shown text, so a five-row line stays about 17 s), and after a topic's last line the list comes back. Meanwhile an amber bar fills along the bottom of the card's "Continue"; pressing it still moves on at once, and "Back" leaves the topic. A square button right of "Continue" pauses it (a pause icon; F, or X on the gamepad): the bar and the voice stop in the middle of the line, the icon turns to play, and pressing it again goes on from where both stopped. "Continue" still moves on at once while paused, and the next line plays normally again (so do "Back" and the next talk). Colonists' remarks aren't affected (they stay until the player walks off), and radio lines always go on by themselves. **The voice language** is its own setting next to the texts' ("Voices": English, Deutsch or Off): until one is picked, the voices follow the texts' language when it has voices, else they speak English; a line missing in the picked language is spoken in English. Still to come (see [`planned/voice.md`](../planned/voice.md)): the rest of the cast. Steam asks to disclose AI-generated content on the store page.
- **AI-driven NPCs** (idea for later, an optional experiment): a local language model writing NPC dialogue on the fly. Open: it shares the GPU with rendering (VR needs 90 FPS), it must stay consistent with the quests, it needs content guardrails (Steam asks for them with live-generated content), and its lines would need live voice. Scripted quest dialogue stays the backbone.

## Writing lines

Rules for every line an NPC says (talks, transmissions, colonists' remarks, quest text):

- **Full, plain sentences**, not clipped ones: a speech bubble fits about five rows. The German reads naturally (not word for word), stays gender-neutral towards the player and uses the game's German terms (Mission, Miner, Spind, Schießstand, Ersatzmagazine); the crew says "du", or "ihr" to several players.
- **Never name locked content** (a weapon not built yet, a place not reached): gate such topics instead (`Topic.requires_weapon`, story flags). Keep lines consistent with what earlier lines already set (the crew knows the player; the people's about-lines in `game/npcs/crew/` and `game/npcs/ostra/`).
- **True without caveats:** a line holds in every case it can play (reloading needs a spare magazine or ore). Say "lowest quality", not "most common"; no abstract units ("per unit"), say "from the same amount".
- **In-world knowledge only:** NPCs know their world (devices, the holo table, consoles, lights on gear, the silos), never the interface around it: the timer, settings, saving, topics, markers, the suit ring, holo cards or their buttons ("press Set course"). They say it themselves ("set it as our destination"). Host and guest get their own lines (`guest_lines`), not "only the host can".
- **Where they stand:** an NPC knows their room (`Topic.away_lines` elsewhere) but not the player's deck, so no "come up" or "on the lower deck". Prefer one line that fits in their room and elsewhere alike ("one of my med beds") over a pair; keep a pair only when the home line really points at the room.
- **Transmissions** are heard wherever the player stands (no "come to the mess"), and lines heard by everyone have solo and co-op wordings (`CrewReaction.players`).
- **Messages** (and a station's calls) are left for the ship and its crew: they speak to all of them ("you", German "ihr"), true whether one player or four hear them, and a call doesn't say why when it can be about different things (news or a hand-in).
- **Names** of weapons and alien strains may still change before the release: a line that names one hardcodes it, so keep such lines few.
