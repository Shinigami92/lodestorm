# Spaceship (hub)

- Multiple rooms with NPCs. The ship is big: the whole hub is its interior.
- **No loading screens** (but the boot screen at the start, see [UI](ui.md)): the ship and the planet outside are one world. Players walk from the hub down the ramp straight into the mission. That's also why the weather outside matters: while the ship flies and lands, a storm around it hides the next location being loaded, so it acts as the loading screen.
- **Landing gear:** the landed ship stands on six legs along its sides (the hull 1.5 m above the ground, **TBD (playtest)**); nobody walks under it. The legs pull up into their housings in space and in flight, and come down in the touchdown's last 2.5 s. The ramp is 10 m wide and 5 m long and reaches the ground at 30°.
- **The ramp always leads out to the right** (screen right): players walk from the inside to the right into the mission, and come back from the right.
- **Ramp button:** the ramp is closed by default. A button on the inner wall next to the door lowers it, once a mission is picked at the holo table; this also locks the mission and resets its timer. Pressing it again before anyone stepped out raises the ramp again. Once everyone is back aboard, the ramp goes up and the mission is done: the next run needs a newly picked mission.
- **No weapons inside the ship** (nor in a station): weapons are holstered aboard, except in the shooting range, where players try them without a mission. They only work inside the range (not in its doorway), and a force field in the doorway stops every shot, so nothing leaves the range. Targets stand at its far end. Reloads there are free (the ship's stock), so trying weapons and practicing the timing reload costs nothing. Idea for later: the targets show damage numbers on hit.
- **Camera inside:** while a player is aboard, the roof and the walls facing the camera are cut away (they still cast shadows), so the camera can see inside. Inner walls between the camera and the corridor keep a low stub, so the rooms stay outlined. On the lower deck, the whole upper deck is cut away too; on the upper deck, the lower deck is hidden under its floor. Some rooms frame themselves: in the shooting range the camera looks at the whole room instead of the player, so the targets stay on screen from the firing line.
- **Two decks**, nose on the left, tail with the ramp on the right (like a cargo plane's rear ramp). A corridor runs along the middle of each deck; lifts join the decks at both ends, one by the bridge and one in the double-height cargo bay, so a run is a loop: ramp → cargo bay → lower deck (upgrades, armory) → lift → bridge (pick a mission) → upper deck → cargo bay lift → ramp.
  - **Upper deck (command and living):** cockpit at the nose, the bridge next to it, then the player cabins, the crew cabins and the mess hall.
  - **Lower deck (work):** the specimen lab in the nose (empty so far: its tanks for the strains found and the statistics station are planned, see [`planned/ship-interior.md`](../planned/ship-interior.md)), medical bay (respawn point, four med beds) and engine room behind it, the shooting range, the armory (loadout) and one upgrade room per vendor (weapons, armor, skills, miners), each with its station (see [Upgrade stations](progression.md#upgrade-stations)).
- **Armory:** four lockers along the back wall, one per player (numbered, with a strip in the player's color; planned colors: see [`planned/art.md`](../planned/art.md)), where they pick their loadout (see [UI](ui.md)). In each, under an amber "Loadout" sign, are two cradles, slots 1 and 2, holding the weapons that go along on the next mission (the card says so too). Every weapon of the game hangs once on the **weapon wall**, a free-standing wall in the middle of the armory, in rows by origin (colony arms on top, then the converted mining tools, then the expedition's prototypes; each row named at its start, in the order of the campaign; an origin without weapons yet gets no row, and the rows grow with the weapons), the ones the player doesn't own yet see-through ("Locked"), which hints at what's still to find; under each weapon in their loadout its slot's number shows in the player's color. Using their locker, the player selects a weapon in a cradle or on the wall, and the camera follows it there and back; on the wall the card floats above the wall over the weapon, so it hides no other weapon (its card: name, how it plays, ranged or melee, the MK pips of its parts, a button per slot). With the keyboard or a gamepad the highlight moves over the wall as a grid, up from its top row into the cradles and down from there back to the wall; `tab_prev`/`tab_next` jump to the first weapon of the previous or next row. The selected slot (its cradle's strip lights up, its number turns amber) is the cradle selected last, or an empty one, so the next weapon fills it; `interact` (or the card's slot buttons, or the keys 1 and 2 straight away) puts the weapon into that slot (if it's in the other slot, the two swap) or takes it out again; a slot may stay empty, but one weapon always goes along (the last one can't be taken out). Only the player a locker belongs to can open it; lockers of players who aren't in the session stay shut. The loadout is locked from the ramp going down until the run is won or lost: the doors stay shut meanwhile. The wall shows the weapons of the player using a locker, else of the local player. Using the wall itself (from either side, anywhere along it) opens the player's own locker with the weapon of the top row nearest to them selected. Greybox: a dark panel standing on the floor, weapons 1.3 m apart, rows 0.6 m apart (**TBD (playtest)**).
- **Lifts:** a platform for up to four players in a shaft beside the corridor. Stepping on it sends it to the other deck (greybox: after 0.6 s, 2 s ride, **TBD (playtest)**); riders who came along have to step off before it leaves again. Waiting at the gate on the other deck calls it. Gates close the shaft on every deck the platform isn't at.
- **Walking the ship:** the bridge sits at the nose, far from the ramp. Setting course in the cockpit starts the flight, and players walk aft and gear up while the ship flies and lands, so the walk isn't dead time (flight time **TBD (playtest)**).
- **Bridge:** missions are selected here. A holo table in the middle is the navigator (see [Mission selection](missions.md#mission-selection)); its hologram of the mission location (a small copy of the location, the ship's spot highlighted) doesn't turn: it lies the way the ship will land, the ramp side to the right like aboard, so what lies beside the ship in the hologram lies beside it outside. Players gather around it to see where they're going. Planned beside it: the quest board (see [`planned/quests.md`](../planned/quests.md)). The **comms station** on the bridge invites friends (see [UI](ui.md)).
- **Cockpit:** the flight console sets course for the mission picked at the holo table and launches the flight.
- **Engine room:** the drive core with the ship's heat shield around it, the station that opens the planets (see Opening planets in [Missions](missions.md)). Greybox until the ship's art pass: a dark core with a ring of plates stacked around it, one per planet inwards (MK2 at the bottom); installed plates glow, the next one fills up from left to right over its front like a silo's gauge as it's paid, later ones show as dim outlines, so the length of the way stays visible. The camera frames the core, each plate is selectable (the next one first), and its card says what it opens ("Heat shield MK2", "Opens Vehra"), its cost per ore with how much is paid so far and what the hold has (red where the hold can't cover the rest), "Make it the ship's project" and "Pay the rest from the hold"; once it's the project, the card says so. Installed plates say so, later ones which level they need first. Locked during a mission, like the other stations.
- **Cabins:** one per player (up to 4, cabin 1 closest to the bridge for solo play) with a bed and a locker, and in cabin 1 the settings terminal; the game starts with the player in cabin 1 (after the title view); eight crew cabins for the NPCs with a bed each. The bed in one's own cabin is where a player quits ("Sleep"). The cabin locker is furniture: there's no inventory (quest items belong to their quest, see [Quests](missions.md#quests)).
- **Windows:** every room along the outer walls has windows on its deck (one per ~9 m of wall), each in a niche reaching out towards the hull (about 1 m deep, the specimen lab's flat, the belly rises under them), and the cockpit looks out through its front wall under the canopy. From inside, they show the outside (light falls in through them); from outside, they show into the ship. Windows in walls cut away for the camera go with their wall.
- **The shell** (the art pass of the inside; the rooms' key objects and dressing are next, see [`planned/ship-interior.md`](../planned/ship-interior.md)): a ship's cross-section rather than a hall's: the walls meet the ceiling in a 45° chamfer with a light line in it and the floor in a slanted foot, a heavy round rim runs along the top of every wall the camera cuts (the hull's thickness; the corridor's stubs too, with a thin lamp line), doors and window niches sit in heavy frames with rounded corners, the rooms' corners are rounded, and the drive core's conduits run along the lower deck's back wall from the engine room to the cargo bay. Clean light-grey satin paint on the walls, a glossy grey epoxy floor that mirrors the lamps (reflection probes and screen-space reflections), amber edge markings, a grating strip down the corridor's middle, hazard-striped thresholds in the doors. Each room has its own floor tint and accent color (a lamp strip along its outer wall; values **TBD (playtest)**), shown in the palette viewer. Rooms are lit only by their own lamps (the cool rim light outside leaves the shell out), all near neutral white: lamps in the ceiling about 7 m apart (the middle one with shadows in big rooms) and spots washing the back wall. Every room's door slides open when someone walking comes within 5 m (players, later NPCs on their way; 0.4 s, with a short hiss, so it's open before they get there) and closes 1.5 s after the last one left; it never blocks anyone (no collision).
- **Finding the way:** signs like neon tubes on the walls, never paint on the floor; the rooms themselves should read by what stands in them. Every room's name is a sign in its accent color: on its back wall (rooms behind the corridor, the bridge, the mess hall, the cargo bay over its window; lower-deck names under the drive core's conduits, the armory's over its lockers), on the slanted back wall of the cockpit and the specimen lab, and for the rooms in front of the corridor (the vendors, cabins 2 and 4, crew 5–8, the engine room) standing on the corridor's stub beside their door, facing the camera (the weapons room's and the armor room's left of the door, their stations stand right of it). Amber guide signs with arrows point the way: on the corridors' stubs (to the cargo bay and the ramp, to the lift, the medical bay and the engine room, to the cabins, the mess hall and the cargo lift, back to the bridge), on the lifts' shaft walls (which deck the lift goes to, and what's there), on the bridge's and the mess hall's back walls and in the cargo bay. A sign grows with its translation, away from its door; the upper deck's signs hide with the deck while a player is below.
- **Greybox layout** (top view, camera at the bottom; about 96 × 30 m, plus the nose rooms reaching 18 m (cockpit) and 9 m (specimen lab) into the nose, their outer walls slanting in with its taper; rooms 4 m high on the lower deck and 5.5 m on the upper, the cockpit 4 m under the nose's sloping top; sizes **TBD (playtest)**; the hull's model wraps it, and the greybox builder checks that nothing pokes through it):

          \---------+---------------+------+----+----+----+----+----+----+-------+  CARGO  |
  LOWER    /--------+  Medical bay  | LIFT | Shooting range   |  Armory          |   BAY   |

  C = player cabins, N = crew cabins. Each room has its own floor tint and light.

- **Ship states:**
  - *In space:* black space with stars all around and the planet's glow far below; windows show space; the ramp cannot be opened. The game starts here.
  - *Flight:* setting course flies the ship there in three phases (greybox about 24 s, about the walk from the cockpit to the cargo bay, all **TBD (playtest)**): the departure in space (6 s, stars streak past), the descent through thick clouds (14 s; the clouds rush up past the hull and the camera shakes, less while it frames a menu) and the touchdown (4 s, the clouds clear and the ground shows). The ship itself never moves, the world changes around it. The location is generated in the background from the moment its mission is picked at the holo table (no freeze) and goes under the ship in the clouds; if it isn't ready yet, the descent lasts until it is. From a location, the ship first takes off (10 s, climbing through the clouds into space, where the location is left behind). To another planet or moon the departure takes longer (greybox 14 s), with the destination growing behind the hull, seen through the windows (lit per body with the art pass, see [`planned/art.md`](../planned/art.md)); within the same body only the take-off and the landing happen.
  - *Docking, docked, undocking:* at a station, see [`station.md`](station.md).
  - *Landed, no mission started:* windows/ramp area show the local environment (e.g. storm). Weather per biome comes with the art pass (see [`planned/biomes.md`](../planned/biomes.md)).
  - The player **cannot go outside without a selected mission**; the ramp button only works with one and while landed (docked at a station, it always works). The **first player stepping out of the ship starts the mission** and its timer.
  - After a run (won or lost), once the ramp is up and everyone is alive aboard, the ship takes off into space on its own after a few seconds (greybox 5 s, **TBD (playtest)**), unless a new course was set before.
