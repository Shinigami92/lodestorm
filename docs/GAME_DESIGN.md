# Lodestorm — Game Design Document

> Status: draft / pre-production. Values marked **TBD (playtest)** are intentionally left open and will be tuned once the systems are playable.
>
> This document describes the game as it is built, with its vision and rules. Designs that are decided but not built yet are in [`planned/`](planned/README.md); the order of the work is in [`PLAN.md`](PLAN.md).

## 1. Pitch

A 3D isometric action/mining game with a dark, realistic sci-fi look. The player lives on a spaceship that acts as the hub. From the ship they pick a mission, the ship flies there, and the player heads out to place miners on resource nodes, fight off aliens, collect the mined resources via a hose, and get back to the ship before the storm hits. Resources are spent on upgrades in the ship. A story campaign leads from planet to planet: better ores open the way to the next planet, where rarer ores wait.

### Story and setting (draft)

- **Setup:** a colony in a newly opened star system runs short of supplies. The players are a prospecting crew supplying it, next to other crews, stations and outposts.
- **The mystery:** a first expedition went missing. Following its trail reveals bit by bit what the players don't know at the start: the storms come from the system's star, which is becoming unstable, and they keep growing stronger. The rare ores only form where its radiation is strongest.
- **The ending:** the colony has to leave the system before a big flare, which needs something built from the rarest ores near the star: a flare shield for the colony's convoy, to slingshot around the star and out (the players' heat shield is its small prototype). So the campaign moves inward, planet by planet. The six acts, the expedition's fate, the people and the post-game: see [`planned/story.md`](planned/story.md).
- **How it's told:** NPCs and their quests drive the main story, in conversations aboard that the players start themselves. During runs, NPCs speak over the radio (quest updates, warnings, short story bits). Players in co-op may talk over it (e.g. on voice chat), so nothing important is only said over the radio: radio lines stay short and can be listened to again aboard (a comms log). The expedition's logs, found as handcrafted pieces on the planets, are optional lore for players who want to dig deeper; the main story never depends on reading them. Cutscenes are unlikely (costly); key moments can use the camera transitions that exist anyway (e.g. the ship taking off and landing).
- **One star system** for the campaign: six planets from the outside in, one per signature ore (the fourth a gas giant whose two moons carry its ore), four moons, stations and outposts; built so more systems can follow later (see [Missions](#6-missions), Planets, moons and regions).
- **Length:** about 40 h to beat the game; play can go on afterwards (post-game, e.g. higher difficulties with rarer ores, open).

### Architecture implications

- **Multiplayer-ready from day one:** game state changes go through host-authoritative code paths, solo = local host. Co-op is enabled later without rewriting gameplay.
- **Camera abstraction:** desktop uses an isometric camera; VR puts the player's head where the desktop camera is, looking down onto the character from the same direction, free to turn and tilt (planned, see [`planned/vr-view.md`](planned/vr-view.md)). Same gameplay, swappable camera rig. The rig also supports camera transitions (see below).
- **Input abstraction:** all gameplay reads Godot input actions only, so keyboard/mouse, gamepad and VR controllers map onto the same actions.

### Localization and voice

- **Languages:** English and German first, more later. All player-facing text goes through translations from the start, also the text in the world (3D menus, labels, the room names painted on the floors). German runs about a third longer than English, so panels and labels leave room for it. The game follows the system language; the settings terminal in cabin 1 picks another one and saves it (resetting keeps it). How it works: [`TECHNICAL.md`](TECHNICAL.md#localization).
- **Translations by contributors:** the project is on GitHub, so new languages and fixes come in as pull requests (one translation file per language, readable in a diff; the CLA applies).
- **Subtitles for every spoken line**, in every language, shown in the world, not on the screen: a speech bubble above the speaking NPC, radio lines next to the player. They work the same in VR.
- **Voice-over** (wanted, English only is fine): generated with a free AI text-to-speech model that runs locally, like the concept images. Lines are generated ahead of time (per scripted line, an audio file), not while playing. The model needs good English (German is a bonus), a license that allows selling the game, and distinct voices per NPC that aren't cloned from real people without their consent. Planned after the art pass, starting with a spike that picks the model (see [`planned/voice.md`](planned/voice.md)); sound effects come earlier (see [`planned/sfx.md`](planned/sfx.md)). Steam asks to disclose AI-generated content on the store page.
- **AI-driven NPCs** (idea for later, an optional experiment): a local language model writing NPC dialogue on the fly. Open: it shares the GPU with rendering (VR needs 90 FPS), it must stay consistent with the quests, it needs content guardrails (Steam asks for them with live-generated content), and its lines would need live voice. Scripted quest dialogue stays the backbone.

## 3. Perspective & art direction

- 3D, isometric camera.
- **The core loop is played in the isometric view** (desktop) / **from the same viewpoint with a free head** (VR). Camera transitions away from it are allowed for specific moments, e.g. moving closer to a terminal for a 3D menu, the ship taking off and landing, or the ramp opening at mission start. They always return to the main view. In VR, transitions must stay comfortable (e.g. fades or moving the viewpoint, never turning the player's head); details **TBD (playtest)**.
- **Desktop camera:** fixed angle looking along the map's diagonal, following the player. Projection (orthographic vs. perspective), angle, distance and follow speed are **TBD (playtest)**; they live in `game/camera/iso_camera_settings.tres` (current: perspective, chosen over orthographic after a first comparison).
- **Controls (twin-stick):** movement is relative to the screen ("up" walks up the screen, whatever the camera angle). The player faces the aim direction: the point on the ground under the mouse cursor, or the right stick. Without aim input (gamepad, stick released) the player faces the movement direction. Whichever of mouse and right stick was used last wins.
- **Target lock:** an alien under the mouse cursor (within 4% of the screen height, let go beyond 7%), or the nearest one within 12° of the right stick and 25 m, gets locked on: corner brackets in the player's color frame it on the ground, the player turns to it and shots fly straight at it, so aliens up or down a slope can be hit too (`game/player/player_settings.tres`, **TBD (playtest)**). Without a lock, shots fly level. The mouse aims at the ground under the cursor, also on hills.
- Readability over detail: ore types, aliens and miners must be recognizable at a glance from the isometric distance (and in VR).
- **Seen behind cliffs:** what matters stays visible behind cliffs, plants and walls as a see-through silhouette (`OccludedSilhouette`, `assets/shaders/occluded_silhouette.gdshader`): players in orange (each co-op player in their own color with M7), aliens in red, miners in pale cyan, ore crystals faintly in their ore's color and unopened crates in amber, but only within 30 m of a player, so finding them stays exploring (**TBD (playtest)**). It's only a depth test, so it works from any camera (VR's head tracking too).
- **Seeing into the passes:** the generated ground keeps the rock on the camera's side low instead of cutting anything away at runtime (see [Missions](#6-missions), Generated outside world): the camera sees over it into every area and pass.

### Art direction

Found by rating four rounds of concept images (generated locally, not part of the repo); the art pass (M5) refines it per asset.

- **Realistic, dark sci-fi:** PBR materials (painted metal, rubber, glass) with wear: scratches, rust, grime and dust. Real proportions; toy-like, chunky, pastel and cartoon looks are out.
- **Rim light for readability:** players, aliens and machines get a strong rim light (fresnel on their materials), so their silhouettes stay readable on dark ground and from the isometric distance. It's the main tool that keeps the realistic look from turning into mush.
- **Muted world, glowing accents:** ground, rocks, plants and hulls stay desaturated (greys, browns, olive). Saturated color is kept for what the player has to read: glowing ore in its type color, alien eyes and glands, lamps, the hose, the suit's collar band and other status lights. These emissive accents also carry most of the diegetic UI.
- **Human tech vs. the hive:** human gear (ship, miners, suits, weapons) is industrial and weathered; the aliens and their nests are biopunk and organic (chitin, sinew, wet surfaces, glowing glands), see [Alien look](#alien-look-and-behavior-direction-for-the-art-pass).
- **Per asset** (from the concept rounds):
  - **Miner:** a big machine, about twice the player's height, with the glowing glass column and the lamp on top. First model in the game (`assets/miners/miner_glow.glb`): a column on four mechanical legs with two equipment boxes, 3.6 m tall, legs standing around the ore crystals; its body (collision) has a 1.5 m radius, **TBD (playtest)**. When placed, it turns its front toward the camera's view (25° past straight on), so the fill column is never hidden behind its equipment boxes.
  - **Player:** a bulky, dark armored suit; the collar band and the ore canisters on the back glow. First model in the game (`assets/player/player.glb`): helmet with a dark visor, pauldrons, gauntlets and greaves over a padded undersuit, a backpack frame the cargo canisters hang from, a blue chest light; 1.86 m. Animated: idle, running in four directions relative to where the player faces (blended by the velocity, played faster or slower with the speed), the upper body holding the blaster at the hip while armed (relaxed arms aboard the ship, where the weapon is holstered), and falling face down on death.
  - **Ore nodes:** crystal clusters in many shapes and sizes, also growing sideways out of rock walls; each ore type has its own crystal shape besides its color. Modeled per ore in `assets/ores/<ore>.glb` (shapes: see [Resources](#9-resources)): two ground variants (on a cracked rock pad of the deposit's size, crystals at most 1 m tall so they fit between the miner's legs) and one wall cluster for dressing cliffs later. The crystals glow in the ore's color and its glow pattern (see [Resources](#9-resources)), strong enough to bloom from the camera distance without blowing out to white.
  - **Ground:** textured per biome with three tileable sets made from real geometry in Blender: the open ground (*Dust flats*: sandy regolith with patches of cracked crust and sparse pebbles; *Overgrowth*: dark damp soil under mats of moss, with roots, stones and pale fungus buds), scree (loose stones at the foot of cliffs and in patches) and rock on the cliffs (*Dust flats*: sandstone strata; *Overgrowth*: dark blocky rock with moss in the joints). Small stones are scattered on top as 3D models, so the ground has real silhouettes up close. Details in [`TECHNICAL.md`](TECHNICAL.md#4-asset-pipeline) (Terrain textures).
  - **Rocks:** jagged and cracked, never smooth. Modeled in `assets/rocks/rocks.glb` (boulders, rubble, outcrops, and cliff chunks for dressing cliffs later), the plants and debris of both biomes in `assets/plants/plants.glb`.
  - **Ship:** stands on landing skids (no wheels); the ramp is at the rear between the thrusters.
  - **Weapons:** glowing parts show their state (the rifle's ammo counter, the power core in the hammer's head). The blaster is modeled as that rifle (`assets/weapons/blaster.glb`), its six counter segments show the ammo (see [Ammo and reloading](#ammo-and-reloading)). The shotgun (glowing shell strip on its side, one shell per round) and the hammer (glowing core in its head) are greybox meshes so far.
  - **Swarmer:** the first alien model (`assets/aliens/swarmer/swarmer.glb`), close to the concept: a lean runner on four clawed legs, back 0.72 m high (about the player's hip), nose to rump 1.5 m plus a long whip tail; a long head with a toothed jaw and glowing yellow-green eye slits, dark crimson chitin plates over sinew, and two bone scythes rising from its shoulders that arch forward over the head (1.5 m up). Its legs step on their own over the ground, in a trot (the diagonal pairs together); the body breathes while it waits, runs with the head low and the tail streaming, rears back and snaps forward with the jaw open and the blades striking when it attacks, and collapses onto its side when it dies. Guards, hunters and raiders all use it for now; raiders are shaded darker.
  - **Alien nest:** a mound of chitin plates and sinew with tendrils spreading over a slime layer and a glowing mouth on top (`assets/aliens/nest/nest.glb`); the mouth and the glands pulse with the hive's escalation.
- **Cost and performance:** realistic PBR is the most expensive look to build and to render. Texture sizes and LODs are planned per asset type in the art pass, with the VR budget (90 FPS, two views) in mind; **TBD (profiling in M5/M6)**.
- Planned: each body's own light (weak and cool far out, harsh inwards), how dark it may get, the storm's light steps, and four player colors paired with numbers (see [`planned/art.md`](planned/art.md)); the sky per body and a far ring of land beyond the map (see [`planned/vr-view.md`](planned/vr-view.md)).

### UI: in the world, not on the screen

- **No 2D HUD** where it can be avoided. Game information is shown in the world or on the player (diegetic UI). This also keeps desktop and VR the same: a screen-space HUD doesn't work in VR anyway.
- **Where things show:** what a player reads in a fight on the suit ring around their feet (health, ammo, spare magazines, the dash, where hits come from, the pointers), carried resources on the player's back (and ore arriving above them), a miner's fill level on the miner itself, mission time above the door and by the storm building up; exact amounts aboard (the silos in the cargo bay). Nothing needs to be looked up during a run. The suit's own lights (the collar band mirroring the health, the weapon's counter) stay as looks and for teammates. Three layers: critical things (low health, an empty or reloading weapon, a hit) are always readable and big, situational ones (spare magazines, the dash) show when they change, details (exact amounts, MK levels) stay aboard. Every kind of marking on the ground has its own shape: the player's ring solid arcs, the target lock corner brackets, pointers arrows on the ring's edge, giving ammo a green band around the ring (alien telegraphs come as red dashed circles and lines with the aliens, see [`planned/aliens.md`](planned/aliens.md)). Planned: the other weapons' feeds on the ammo arc, the tracked quest's pointer and the miners left (see [`planned/player-display.md`](planned/player-display.md)).
- **The suit ring** (`SuitRing`, values **TBD (playtest)**): a ring the suit projects on the ground around the player (1.36 m radius, wider than the body; its lines keep at least 2 px on screen). Its halves stay fixed to the screen, not to where the player faces, and both arcs fill from the bottom up, so low values sit on the camera's side where the body never hides them; the top is left open where the body stands.
  - **Left arc, health:** green, amber below half, red and blinking below a quarter (it dims between blinks but never goes dark, so its length still reads), flashing white on damage (under steady damage like the storm's, a pulse every half second). A short red mark on the outer edge points to where a hit came from and fades within a second (up to four at once; the storm's damage comes from nowhere and has none).
  - **Right arc, ammo** (only with a ranged weapon drawn): the magazine in its ammo color (blaster cyan, shotgun amber), as a segment per round up to 16 rounds (the shotgun's shells), else one bar; amber and blinking when nearly empty (a sixth or less), the whole arc blinking red when it's empty and nothing pays a reload. A magazine reload fills it up again with the timing window marked white (red after a miss); with single rounds the round being loaded brightens, white within the window. It flashes white when a timed press finishes the reload and red when a reload can't start.
  - **Ticks inside the ammo arc:** the spare magazines, one per spare a run starts with (a partly used one dimmer, used ones only an outline); they show on a reload, when a spare is used or refilled and on switching weapons, then fade.
  - **Inner thin ring:** the dash, warm white, filling up again while it recharges, gone once it's ready.
  - **Outer band:** fills green while the player hands a teammate ammo.
  - **Quiet when nothing goes on:** each arc dims to a faint outline 3 s after its last change (a hit, healing, firing, reloading, switching weapons), with its filled share still readable; health below half and a nearly empty magazine keep it lit. Aboard (the weapon holstered) the outline hides too, so only what changes shows. It hides while the player uses a menu and once dead.
  - **Pointers on the edge:** arrows just outside the hit marks: the way back to the ship in its status color (once the door is more than 12 m away, blinking in the last minute, the countdown beyond its tip, pushed out by the text's width so it never covers the ring), and the ore scanner's smaller arrow in the ore's color.
  - **On the ground:** it tilts with the ground under the player and is drawn over the ground, grass and low plants, but cliffs and props hide it like the body (the silhouette shows the player then).
  - **Settings** (on the pause panel and the settings terminal): its size (small, medium, large: 0.8, 1 and 1.25 times) and "Always show" (a checkbox), which keeps the arcs and the spare ticks lit instead of dimming. While the pause panel is open the ring stays shown and lit beside the card, so a new size shows right away. Health never relies on color alone: it blinks below a quarter, and the arc's length shows the share.
  - **Teammates' rings** show only the health arc inside a thin outline in their player color (1 orange, 2 sky blue, 3 yellow, 4 reddish purple), with no ammo, ticks, dash, hit marks or pointers, so four players don't cover the ground. Only the player a machine plays reads its input; until co-op (M7), the debug panel adds teammates that stand still.
- **Target lock on the ground:** four corner brackets in the player's color frame the locked-on alien, sized to its body and kept square to the screen like the ring; drawn over the ground the same way.
- **Ore arriving:** while a hose delivers, a line above the player shows each arriving ore's badge with the amount ("+180", several ores side by side), counting up; once the ore stops coming (slower than 5 units a second) it rises and fades over 2 s. A slow trickle (a miner filling while the player stands by) shows once it adds up to 10 units. Spending ore (ammo) shows nothing.
- **The object is the menu.** Where something is chosen or bought, the player uses the thing itself, not a screen next to it: the weapon on a workbench, their own suit in a fitting frame, a miner on its maintenance pad, the star map on the holo table, the skill graph. The camera frames it, its parts become selectable (a weapon's barrel for its damage, its magazine, a miner's drill), and a small **holo card** beside the selected part shows what it is, its level pips (the same pips as on the weapons and the helmet), what the next level does, its cost and the action. This holds any number of weapons, upgrades and skills (there may be 50–100 skills), gives the rooms things to stand at (they fill with assets and NPCs later), and German only has to fit one kind of card. Flat **terminals** stay for system things only: the settings in cabin 1 and the comms station on the bridge.
- **Using one** (values **TBD (playtest)**): a player walks up and uses it (`interact`). They stand still, and their camera moves in to frame it (keeping the isometric angle); nobody standing in front of it hides it. Leaving moves the camera back. One player uses it at a time.
  - **Mouse:** the cursor points at the parts and the card's buttons, like a 2D menu.
  - **Gamepad and keyboard:** movement (left stick, WASD, also the d-pad and arrow keys) moves the highlight to the nearest part (on a screen: button) in that direction, `interact` presses the card's action; `tab_prev`/`tab_next` (LB/RB, Q/R; E is `interact`) jump between groups (the next weapon on the rack, the next skill cluster); `pause` (Escape, Menu) or B steps back and closes.
  - **VR (later):** a controller points like the mouse does.
  - Cards and screens lean back to face the camera and glow in the dark rooms (cyan text and frames, the highlight in amber like the floor markings). **Text size:** body text is at least about 2% of the framed view's height (about 22 px at 1080p). Every card and screen is tested to fit in every language (German runs about a third longer).
- **A title, not a main menu:** with one save there's nothing to choose. The game opens on a **title view in the world**: the ship drifting in space, seen from outside (its hull closed, the view drifting slowly around it), the game's name as a hologram above it and "Press any button" below. Any button (a key, a mouse or gamepad button; moving the mouse or a stick doesn't count) ends it, and the camera glides into the ship to the player in cabin 1. After a while without input (greybox: 30 s, **TBD (playtest)**) the credits scroll there instead of the prompt; any input brings the prompt back.
- **The pause panel:** `pause` (Escape, Menu/Options) with no menu open shows a holo card beside the player, facing the camera, which moves in like at a station; the player stands still. It holds **Resume**, **Settings** and **Quit**; `pause` again or "Resume" closes it, and on the other pages `pause` steps back, focusing the button that opened the page. Since the card keeps to the bottom of its screen, a page's "Back" sits where the first page's "Quit" appears: after a page changes, a mouse click only counts once the pointer has moved a little. **Solo, the world stops** while it's open (aliens, the timer, the storm, the ship's flight); it also opens by itself when the game's window loses focus. **In co-op the game goes on**: the card's title says "Menu" instead of "Paused" with the note "The game goes on", and the player can still be attacked, like at any station.
- **Settings in one place, reachable everywhere:** the same settings show on the pause panel's settings page and on the settings terminal in cabin 1: the language and the suit ring's size and "Always show" (the sound volumes join them with their feature). The terminal also keeps the one dangerous thing, **Reset progress** (see [Death & persistence](#11-death--persistence)). Both have a **Credits** page: who made the game, the tools, and the third-party licenses (Godot's own and those of the components it's built from); the sound sources join them with the sound effects.
- **Quitting:** "Quit" on the pause panel works like the bed: aboard it saves and quits to the desktop at once, fading to black; during a run it asks first, since quitting loses the run (one confirm, "Cancel" focused). The bed in one's own cabin (cabin N for player N) stays as the in-world way: a station whose card offers "Sleep", which saves the progress one last time, fades to black (the camera stays on the bed) and quits. No confirmation, since nothing is lost aboard; during a run the card warns that sleeping loses it (what changes during a run is only saved once it's over). Other players' beds can't be used. **Inviting friends** (with co-op, M7): at a comms station on the bridge, which opens the platform's invite (the Steam overlay is the platform's, not a HUD); friends come aboard through the cargo bay. The comms station comes with co-op, together with the comms log (radio lines to listen to again). Planned: leaving and ending a co-op session from the pause panel, joining a friend from the title view, and both in VR (see [`planned/menu-pause.md`](planned/menu-pause.md)).
- **Exceptions:** developer tools (debug overlay for tuning) are allowed in 2D.

## 4. Core loop

```
Ship hub ──► select mission ──► ship flies to location ──► first player steps out = mission starts (timer)
   ▲                                                                    │
   │                                                                    ▼
   │                                                  place miners on resource nodes
   │                                                  fight aliens
   │                                                  collect from miners via hose
   │                                                                    │
   ├── everyone back aboard ──► resources saved ──► spend on upgrades ┘
   │
   └── die ──► lose everything carried this run ──► respawn in medical bay
```

## 5. Spaceship (hub)

- Multiple rooms with NPCs. The ship is big: the whole hub is its interior.
- **No loading screens:** the ship and the planet outside are one world. Players walk from the hub down the ramp straight into the mission. That's also why the weather outside matters: while the ship flies and lands, a storm around it hides the next location being loaded, so it acts as the loading screen.
- **Landing gear:** the landed ship stands on six legs along its sides (greybox: the hull 1.5 m above the ground, **TBD (playtest)**); nobody walks under it. The ramp is 5 m long and reaches the ground at 30°.
- **The ramp always leads out to the right** (screen right): players walk from the inside to the right into the mission, and come back from the right.
- **Ramp button:** the ramp is closed by default. A button on the inner wall next to the door lowers it, once a mission is picked at the holo table; this also locks the mission and resets its timer. Pressing it again before anyone stepped out raises the ramp again. Once everyone is back aboard, the ramp goes up and the mission is done: the next run needs a newly picked mission.
- **No weapons inside the ship:** weapons are holstered aboard, except in the shooting range, where players try them without a mission. They only work inside the range (not in its doorway), and a force field in the doorway stops every shot, so nothing leaves the range. Targets stand at its far end. Reloads there are free (the ship's stock), so trying weapons and practicing the timing reload costs nothing. Idea for later: the targets show damage numbers on hit.
- **Camera inside:** while a player is aboard, the roof and the walls facing the camera are cut away (they still cast shadows), so the camera can see inside. Inner walls between the camera and the corridor keep a low stub, so the rooms stay outlined. On the lower deck, the whole upper deck is cut away too; on the upper deck, the lower deck is hidden under its floor. Some rooms frame themselves: in the shooting range the camera looks at the whole room instead of the player, so the targets stay on screen from the firing line.
- **Two decks**, nose on the left, tail with the ramp on the right (like a cargo plane's rear ramp). A corridor runs along the middle of each deck; lifts join the decks at both ends, one by the bridge and one in the double-height cargo bay, so a run is a loop: ramp → cargo bay → lower deck (upgrades, armory) → lift → bridge (pick a mission) → upper deck → cargo bay lift → ramp.
  - **Upper deck (command and living):** cockpit at the nose, the bridge next to it, then the player cabins, the crew cabins and the mess hall.
  - **Lower deck (work):** medical bay (respawn point) and engine room at the nose, the shooting range, the armory (loadout) and one upgrade room per vendor (weapons, armor, skills, miners), each with its station (see [Upgrade stations](#upgrade-stations)).
- **Armory:** four lockers along the back wall, one per player (numbered, with a strip in the player's color; planned colors: see [`planned/art.md`](planned/art.md)), where they pick their loadout (see [UI](#ui-in-the-world-not-on-the-screen)). Every weapon of the game hangs in it, the ones the player doesn't own yet see-through ("Locked"), which hints at what's still to find; below, under an amber "Loadout" sign, are two cradles, slots 1 and 2, holding the weapons that go along on the next mission (the card says so too). The player selects a weapon (its card: name, how it plays, ranged or melee, the MK pips of its damage and magazine, a button per slot), `tab_prev`/`tab_next` pick the slot (its cradle's strip lights up, its number turns amber), and `interact` (or the card's slot buttons, or the keys 1 and 2 straight away) puts the weapon into that slot (if it's in the other slot, the two swap) or takes it out again; a slot may stay empty, but one weapon always goes along (the last one can't be taken out). Only the player a locker belongs to can open it; lockers of players who aren't in the session stay shut. The loadout is locked from the ramp going down until the run is won or lost: the doors stay shut meanwhile. Planned: a weapon wall across the armory for the 18 planned weapons (see [`planned/weapons.md`](planned/weapons.md)).
- **Lifts:** a platform for up to four players in a shaft beside the corridor. Stepping on it sends it to the other deck (greybox: after 0.6 s, 2 s ride, **TBD (playtest)**); riders who came along have to step off before it leaves again. Waiting at the gate on the other deck calls it. Gates close the shaft on every deck the platform isn't at.
- **Walking the ship:** the bridge sits at the nose, far from the ramp. Setting course in the cockpit starts the flight, and players walk aft and gear up while the ship flies and lands, so the walk isn't dead time (flight time **TBD (playtest)**).
- **Bridge:** missions are selected here. ## 6. Missions

- A mission is defined by: **location**, **timer/duration**, **difficulty**, and the quests that can be done there.
- Target duration: about 15–30 min per mission; exact timers **TBD (playtest)**.
- Planets farther in are more dangerous and have rarer resources.
- **Generated outside world:** the world outside the ship is generated for every run from a seed and a **biome** (`game/locations/generated_location.tscn`, biomes in `game/locations/biomes/`). The picked mission sets the biome, the seed, the size (by the region's length) and (by its difficulty) the number of nests; the world the ship stands in before the first mission is picked has a random biome. Greybox, all values **TBD (playtest)**:
  - **Layout:** open **areas** joined by winding **passes**, with high rock nobody can climb everywhere else (`LocationLayout`, a graph laid out per seed). The passes form a tree with a few **loops** (about one per five areas), so there are ways around and **dead ends**; they never cross, and every area can be reached. The landing yard in front of the ramp is the first area; the ship stands on a flat landing site.
  - **Size:** a standard mission has 11 areas, a region's length scales it (`MissionSettings.location_areas`); the areas spread over a square that grows with their number (11 areas: about 420 m with the rock around it; the farthest area 250–450 m along the passes, 40–75 s on foot). Every outline wobbles, so no area is a circle and no pass a tube.
  - **Kinds of areas** (where they lie in the graph decides): the farthest dead ends are **ore pockets** (small, with the rarest ore), the next ones **nest hollows** (sunken, with two nests); where three or more passes meet is a **crossroads**; in the far half some areas are **plateaus** (a mesa with a level top and a natural rock ramp up its side) and **craters** (a bowl with a rim); the rest are **basins**. Pockets and hollows grow with the layout (about one each per eight areas), plateaus and craters too (one each per ten). Areas can hold a point of interest or a set piece (see below); the landing yard and the ore pockets stay free of the generic ones.
  - **Points of interest and set pieces** (map parts, `game/locations/parts/`, greybox until the art pass): a part fills one area, standing on ground flattened under it, with its props (which block players, aliens and shots) in the middle and a walkable ring around them, so it never blocks a pass. It turns its front towards the way in. Its markers say where its ore nodes (the region's commonest, a middle or its rarest ore), nests, crates and quest sites go; ore and nests go there first, the usual placement fills in the rest. Each part says which kinds of areas it fits; its entry in a pool or in a region's set pieces (`PartPlacement`) says where it goes (near the ship, in the middle, far out, in a dead end, or anywhere) and how often it's picked. About one generic point of interest per four areas, picked from the biome's pool (`LocationBiome.part_pool`); a region's **set pieces** (`Region.set_pieces`) are always placed, in the biggest area that fits (a crater set piece turns a basin into a crater if there is no crater). First parts: a **wreck** (a broken hull half sunk in the ground, a crate), an **abandoned outpost** (prefab modules and a landing pad, a crate), a **rich vein** (a wall of crystals in a dead end, the rarest ore in front of it), a **meteor crater** (a meteorite in a crater, ore around it; later the hook for the late deposit) and an **expedition camp** (a survival pod and a blinking beacon, a quest site; only looks for now). Every region has one set piece, e.g. Redscar Flats the outpost, Hollow Mire the expedition camp, Cinder Steps a big crater, Pale Basin a wreck, Glare Fields a rich vein (`game/world/star_system.tres`). Points of interest show in the landmark color in the location hologram, each with a thicker beam so it reads at the hologram's scale (crates don't show, so finding them stays a discovery). Generic points of interest come up about once per four areas, the least used ones first and each at most twice per location; a dead end for a rich vein lies in the far half (rarest ore next to the ship would skip the way out). The ground under a part blends back over 3–8 m, the shortest that keeps every step as it was (a crater's bowl needs the long one); a part's props may stand where a pass's lane runs into the middle of an area, as few as possible, since the walkable ring keeps the way through open.
  - **Crates** (in parts only, never scattered): `interact` opens one (a press, no hold), its lid swings open and what it holds flies to the player; an unopened crate has an amber light band around it that goes dark once it's open, and shows behind props and cliffs within 30 m like ore. Once per run. What it holds is decided, not rolled: a fragment an accepted quest put in this region (see [Quests](#quests)) if one is still to find, otherwise a **supply cache** that refills the opener's spare magazines (every ranged weapon of the loadout; an opened crate is spent even when they were full). No ore.
  - **Ground:** gentle swells on the open ground; the rock rises in a steep cliff (8 m in the Dust flats, 7 m in the Overgrowth, over 4–5 m) and higher in flat layers behind it. **On the camera's side** of the open ground (the desktop camera never turns, and the mission tells the location where it looks) the rock starts as a 3.5 m ledge and rises behind it in terraces no steeper than the camera looks down, so it never hides the floor beyond its foot; the cliffs the camera looks at keep their height. Only low plants and rocks (up to 1 m) grow on that rock. The first step onto the rock is at least 3 m everywhere. Nobody gets stuck: every spot players can walk to leads back to the ship (a drop off a mesa lands in its basin), and nothing can be climbed onto the rock. Steep ground shows rock, scree collects at the foot of cliffs and in patches, the rock's top is a bit lighter (see [Art direction](#art-direction), Ground). Invisible walls stand at the edges, behind a higher rim of rock.
  - Plants and rocks per biome, scattered in patches. Big ones (trees, boulders) block players, aliens and shots; small ones (bushes, grass) are only looks and fade out in the distance. A lane along every pass (up to the middle of the areas it joins) stays free of blocking ones, ore nodes and nests. Ground and plant colors stay muted so ores and aliens stand out.
  - **Ore and nests by the way there:** ore fields of 1–3 nodes of one type. The first two lie 25–55 m from the ramp, so they're found right away, and have the region's commonest ore; every ore pocket has the rarest; the other fields spread over the basins, crossroads, plateaus and craters, rarer the farther they lie along the passes. Nests (about 0.9 per area, scaled by the difficulty) lie at least 80 m from the landing site along the passes (a bit closer through the rock), two in every nest hollow first, then spread over the other areas, the ones every way out goes through last. Ore fields and nests only appear where players can walk to from the ship.
  - **Biomes**, on a gradient from the cold outside to the scorched inside (each planet has 2–3): *Frost fields* (outer planets and moons), *Dust flats* (outer and middle), *Overgrowth* (middle), *Salt pans* (middle and moons), *Ash fields* (volcanic, inner), *Glass plains* (ground vitrified by the star's flares, innermost). *Dust flats* (sandy with a cracked crust, high plateaus with layered sandstone cliffs, boulders, rock outcrops, rubble, small stones, thorny spire plants, dry shrubs, pebbles and bones) and *Overgrowth* (dark soil under olive moss, gentler hills, fleshy tube plants, mushrooms, puffball fungi, fronds, a few boulders and stones) have their own look. The other four are greybox so far, with their own layout and ground values, the existing ground textures tinted and the existing rocks (planned: each biome its own geography, see [`planned/biomes.md`](planned/biomes.md)): *Frost fields* only rocks on gentle ground, *Salt pans* wide flat areas with low cliffs, pebbles and bones, *Ash fields* dark, rough ground under high rock, *Glass plains* flat and sparse.
  - Generating takes about 1–1.5 s on a worker thread, started when the mission is picked at the holo table; putting the world under the ship costs a short hitch (about 0.2 s) in the clouds during the descent, and its navigation mesh bakes in the background (see [Spaceship](#5-spaceship-hub), Ship states).
  - The tests use the small hand-built test location (`game/locations/test_location.tscn`) instead: fixed spots, quick to set up. The location viewer (`game/debug/location_viewer.tscn`, see [`TECHNICAL.md`](TECHNICAL.md#engine-settings)) draws generated layouts from above, with the map parts.
- **Planets, moons and regions:** each planet and moon has fixed **regions** (like countries), each with its biome and its handcrafted set pieces (landmarks, quest sites, the lost expedition's sites). A visit generates a new section of the region every time (a new seed), with the region's set pieces placed in it. So story missions use the same generated worlds with handcrafted pieces placed in them.
  - **Six planets** from the outside in, one per signature ore, with 3–4 regions each and at least one hard one; the first planet's three regions go from easy to hard for getting started. The fourth is a **gas giant** with no ground to land on: its two moons share its signature ore, so that step of the campaign is a pair of moons instead of a globe. About 25 regions in all (with about 120 runs in a 40 h campaign, about 5 visits each).
  - **Moons** are small (1–2 regions, shorter timers) and have a biome their planet doesn't, so the ores of other biomes are found early; they have no signature ore of their own (except the gas giant's).
  - **Tiers:** each body has a tier (1 for the outermost planet, one more per planet inwards; a moon has its planet's). It shows on the navigator's card; a region's danger (easy, normal, hard) works within it. The heat shield needs that MK level to reach the body, and the body's highest MK level is its tier + 2 (`CelestialBody.max_mk`, see Upgrade stations in [Progression](#12-progression--upgrades)).
    - **Farther in is harder** (`game/mission/mission_settings.tres`, Tiers; all **TBD (playtest)**): per tier above 1, aliens have +30 % health and do +20 % damage, and a location has +10 % nests. That keeps pace with one damage and one armor MK level per planet (+25 % each), so each new planet feels a bit harder until the players have upgraded. Planned: two new strains per tier (see [`planned/aliens.md`](planned/aliens.md)).
    - **The star's influence** (the star is getting unstable, see [Story](#story-and-setting-draft)): per tier above 1, the storm's warning starts 10 s earlier (60 s on Torvane, 110 s on Zairen) and its damage grows 25 % faster (2 → 4.5 HP/s per second); the starting damage and the timer stay. With more armor inwards, the time to get aboard stays about the same, but the warning gets longer and the last seconds harsher. No other hazards from the star; the biomes have their own (planned, see [`planned/biomes.md`](planned/biomes.md)).
  - **Opening planets:** the next planet inwards opens with the ship's **heat shield** at the drive core in the engine room, one MK level per planet, paid mostly with the current planet's signature ore, so better ores open the way. MK1 is the bare core (Torvane and Lisk); MK2 opens Vehra, MK3 Cassor and Mirr, MK4 Gaulm's moons Orrel and Iska, MK5 Skelde, MK6 Zairen: a body is open when its tier is at most the shield's MK level (moons open with their planet), and every region of a reached body is open. The story wraps it later but never adds a second gate.
    - **Costs** (all **TBD (playtest)**), ores only, never alloys: MK2 300 Meridine and 400 Vyrel, MK3 300 Emerin and 400 Halcyne, MK4 300 Indral and 400 Ressa, MK5 300 Selyx and 400 Duskine, MK6 300 Noctile and 500 Duskine (about 3–4 good runs of the signature ore, less than a full silo).
    - **Paid from the hauls:** the host makes the next plate the ship's project; from then on every run that comes back pays into it first, with exactly the ores it still needs, before the rest goes into the hold (see [Co-op progression](#co-op-progression)). Example: the plate needs 300 Meridine; a run brings home 200, the plate takes all 200; the next run brings 150, the plate takes the last 100 and the hold gets 50. Ore the plate took still counts as found. The host can also pay what's missing from the hold any time, as far as the hold has it. The plate is installed as soon as it's paid (at once, not timed, so opening a planet never waits), the project ends with it, and the next planet's regions offer missions right away. Only the host works on it (the ship and the open bodies are the host's save); a guest sees the card with a hint.
    - **The navigator** shows bodies out of reach dimmed, with "?" for their signature ore; their card, and their regions', say what they need ("Needs heat shield MK2 (engine room)", with the plate's ores as badges).
    - **Each plate also raises the highest MK level** of the upgrades by one: MK3 with the bare core (Torvane), MK4 with Vehra, up to MK8 with Zairen, so the upgrades grow with the campaign instead of all being bought on the first planet (see Upgrade stations in [Progression](#12-progression--upgrades)).
  - **Flights between bodies** cost nothing (no fuel).
- **Stations and outposts** are fully handcrafted maps with NPCs and quests, and no enemies (see [Spaceship](#5-spaceship-hub), Stations and outposts).

### Quests

- NPCs hand out quests: the main (story) quest and several side quests can be active at once, e.g. destroy a nest, recover something, extract an amount of one ore, find an expedition log.
- A quest is tied to a planet, location or kind of mission, so it can't be done in just any run.
- **Quests can't be missed:** a run that fails one or skips it leaves it open, and it's available again in a later run.
- Optional bonus goals pay off for staying longer (see below).
- Rewards: ores, weapon blueprints, and reputation with the NPC.
- **Planned in detail** (quests as data, eight kinds of goals, blueprint fragments in crates, requests, the main story's acts, the quest board on the bridge, co-op): see [`planned/quests.md`](planned/quests.md).
- **Teaching the mechanics:** crew NPCs explain mechanics when they become relevant, in their conversations and first quests, not in tutorial pop-ups (no 2D HUD). For example, whoever runs the armory explains that the suit makes ammo from carried ore, that the spare magazines come from the ship's stock and aren't rebuilt during a run (so ore is only spent on rounds that are fired), what the dark cells on the backpack mean, and the timing reload.

### Reasons to move and to stay (risk vs. reward)

Standing at one spot until the timer runs low must not pay off.

- **Nodes run dry** (planned): each holds a limited amount, a few miner fills, so the players have to move on (see [`planned/risk-reward.md`](planned/risk-reward.md)).
- **A mining site draws aliens:** each working miner raises its nearest nests' raider rate (planned, see [`planned/aliens.md`](planned/aliens.md)).
- **Richer and rarer ore** lies farther from the ship; planned: rich fields between the nests of every nest hollow (see [`planned/risk-reward.md`](planned/risk-reward.md)).
- **A late deposit** (planned): a meteor bringing the region's rarest ore late in the run, far out and guarded (see [`planned/risk-reward.md`](planned/risk-reward.md)).
- **Quest goals** elsewhere on the map, and optional bonus goals.
- **No carry limit:** the risk is dying with everything on the back (see [Hose transfer](#8-hose-transfer)). Planned: a cargo hatch at the ramp to bank ore without ending the mission (see [`planned/risk-reward.md`](planned/risk-reward.md)).
- Not planned: the storm closing in from the edges before the timer runs out.
- **Ideas for later** (from the first playtest of the passes, where finding the way back in time became part of the run):
  - More things to place from the miners' room besides miners and the sentries: e.g. beacons that help find the way back to the ship.
  - **Barriers** in a location (e.g. a rockfall or a wall of alien growth closing a pass) that only an unlocked tool or upgrade removes, so the far, richer parts of some regions (their signature ore) open up with progress. Never the only way to an ore a quest needs before that unlock.

### Timer and return

- Greybox: a mission has a 15 min timer (finding ore along the passes takes a while) (`game/mission/mission_settings.tres`), **TBD (playtest)**.
- The mission is **over when every player is back aboard**, in time or after the timer ran out. Dead players don't count: once everyone alive is aboard and at least one player made it, the mission ends (see [Death & persistence](#11-death--persistence)).
- The time shows in the world: a countdown hangs above the door, in a status color that is green, turns amber after half the time, red in the warning time (the last minute on a tier 1 body, longer farther in, see Tiers in [Missions](#6-missions); **TBD (playtest)**) and stays red once the storm hits.
- Outside, when the door is farther away (greybox: 12 m, **TBD (playtest)**), an arrow on the suit ring's edge points to it in the status color (blinking in the last minute), with the countdown beyond its tip, so the way back, the urgency and the exact time are visible while the ship is off-screen.

### Timer expiry

- When the timer runs out, the storm becomes lethal: everyone outside the ship takes damage that grows every second. Greybox: 2 HP/s, +2 HP/s per second on a tier 1 body (faster farther in, see Tiers in [Missions](#6-missions)), so an unhurt player (100 HP) lasts about 9 s (`game/mission/mission_settings.tres`, **TBD (playtest)**). Aboard is safe. There's no grace period beyond the rising damage: the countdown and the warning say enough.
- The storm shows in the world: in the warning time the light dims and a reddish-brown haze builds up in the distance; when the timer runs out it gets thick within a few seconds. Inside the ship the haze fades out (it's an inner room), only the dim light outside still shows the storm. It stays while the players are aboard and clears for the next run.

## 7. Miners

- Placed by the player on ore / mineral / resource nodes.
- One miner per node. The player places it on the nearest free node in range (`place_miner`); a ring on the ground marks that node.
- Fill slowly over time; the player needs to place several for a good haul. Target: an empty miner takes about 2–5 min to fill (so a few fills per mission); exact values **TBD (playtest)**.
- **Stop when full.** Capacity and speed can be upgraded (see [Upgrade stations](#upgrade-stations)) / better miner types.
- Fill level is shown on the miner itself: a glass column fills up in the ore color, and a lamp on top lights up when it's full. Aliens attack it from its surface: their reach counts from the edge of its body, not its center.
- Art pass: the miner model gets a small display showing the fill counter (amount / capacity) and a timer (time until full).
- **Attacked by aliens** (raiders, see [Combat & aliens](#10-combat--aliens)): a miner has health (greybox 100); the frame flashes white when hit and turns rusty with damage. At zero it **breaks down**: it stops mining and the hose disconnects, so its ore can't be taken out until it's repaired, but it keeps the ore. The frame blinks red while broken.
- **Repair:** a player standing next to a miner (greybox 2.5 m) repairs it automatically, 25 health/s; a broken miner works again only once it's repaired completely (4 s from broken). Damaged miners that still work get repaired too. Values **TBD (playtest)**.
- Defense options like shields and sentry turrets are possible later.
- **Unlimited for now:** a player places a miner on every free node they like, instantly, and doesn't pick it up again; the miner upgrades apply to every miner the player places. Planned: crafted miners that are kept and upgraded one by one, picked up again, up to 6 per run with free standard-issue ones filling up, and drills by ore hardness (see [`planned/refinery.md`](planned/refinery.md)).
- **Rarer ores mine slower** (per ore, greybox: mid ores 85 %, signature ores 70 % of the fill rate, **TBD (playtest)**). `OreType.hardness` is prepared in the data but unused: every miner mines every ore.

## 8. Hose transfer

- When the player is within a miner's radius, a hose automatically connects the miner to the player and transfers resources.
- Player can move and shoot while connected.
- Leaving the radius disconnects the hose and stops transfer; re-entering reconnects.
- Each miner has its own hose, so several miners in range transfer at once. In co-op a miner connects to the nearest player in range.
- The hose sags between the miner and the player's back, rests on the ground when it's long, and has the ore color.
- Radius and transfer speed are miner values (upgradable with the miner): greybox 5 m and 30 units/s, so a full miner (180 units) empties in 6 s. **TBD (playtest)**.
- **No carry limit:** everything is carried until returning to the ship; a soft limit (a full load slowing the player down) was dropped, since it would fight the "one more trip" decision. The risk is dying: everything carried is lost.
- Carried resources show on the player's back as one stack of ore poured into glass vessels, like layered sand: the rarest ore at the bottom, the commonest on top, each ore a band in its glow. The stack fills the backpack's two tubes first (left, then right), then canisters strapped on beside the backpack (two stacked per side, a third farther out; at most six, beyond that they all show full), so a big haul makes the silhouette bulkier, also for teammates. Ammo takes the commonest ore first, so it drains the top of the stack and rare ore stays safe at the bottom. Greybox: a tube and a canister each hold one full miner (180 units), **TBD (playtest)**. Exact amounts show aboard, at the silos once the ore is in the hold.

## 9. Resources

- **14 ore types** in three bands, with invented, mineral-like names (each starting with its own letter, so they read apart in lists; listed in `game/ores/all_ores.tres`, commonest first):
  - **Common** (found in several biomes, on many planets): Kyrite (cyan, long hexagonal prisms, steady), Olvane (green, chunky blocks, steady), Pallin (pale ice blue, frost needle sprays, slow pulse), Tessar (teal, stacked plates, wave).
  - **Mid** (fewer biomes, farther out): Vyrel (violet, blade shards from broken geodes, slow pulse), Duskine (deep blue, stretched double pyramids, sparkle), Halcyne (sky blue, basalt-like column bundles, flicker), Ressa (pink, round grape clusters, breathing).
  - **Signature** (one per planet, outermost to innermost; the rarest near the star): Meridine (magenta, twisted spires, heartbeat), Emerin (emerald, crossed double-pointed spindles, sparkle), Indral (indigo, faceted gem balls, fast pulse), Selyx (pale lavender, fiber tufts, wave), Noctile (dark body with mint veins, broken-open nodules, breathing), Aethrine (iridescent, a crown of gem prisms, shifting).
  - The number comes from the plan of about six planets (some with moons): four common and four mid ores for the biomes, a signature ore per planet. More planets bring more signature ores; the catalog, the hold, the cards and the tests work for any number.
- **Each ore is identifiable at a glance** by its color, its crystal shape and its glow pattern, so telling them apart never depends on color alone. The patterns: **steady**; **slow pulse** (about 0.4 Hz, between about 55% and full); **fast pulse** (about 2.5 Hz, shallower); **flicker** (irregular like a candle or a loose contact, with occasional dips); **wave** (bands of light rising up the crystals); **sparkle** (single facets glint at random times); **breathing** (slow in and out, about 0.25 Hz, with a brighter core); **heartbeat** (a double beat about once a second, then rest); **shifting** (the hue slowly turns, about once in 10 s, and changes with the viewing angle, iridescent). One ore has a **dark body** instead: near-black glossy crystals with glowing veins in its color, the veins in its glow pattern. Neighboring deposits don't glow in sync. Patterns stay calm and readable from the camera: nothing brightens more than 3 times a second (photosensitivity). Rates and depths are **TBD (playtest)**. The same glow shows on everything filled with the ore (the miner's column, the hose, the canisters) and on its badge on holo cards (its icon in its color, glowing in its pattern, with its name).
- **Spread over the planets:** biomes carry the common and mid ores, each ore in 2–3 biomes (*Frost fields*: Kyrite, Pallin, Halcyne; *Dust flats*: Kyrite, Pallin, Vyrel, Halcyne; *Overgrowth*: Olvane, Tessar, Duskine, Ressa; *Salt pans*: Olvane, Tessar, Vyrel, Ressa; *Ash fields*: Kyrite, Tessar, Duskine, Halcyne; *Glass plains*: Olvane, Pallin, Vyrel, Ressa; not every biome is on every planet), each planet adds its signature ore, so a region has 3–6 types (see [Missions](#6-missions)) and a single run about 3–5. Rarer ores lie farther from the ship, make more ammo and mine slower. Prepared for later: harder ores needing better drills (see [Miners](#7-miners)).
- **Refinery** (planned): combines 2–3 ores into alloys in real time, so early ores stay useful (see [`planned/refinery.md`](planned/refinery.md)). The far end of the cargo bay is kept free for it.
- **Ores are the only currency:** everything is bought with ores (later also alloys); there are no experience levels.

## 10. Combat & aliens

- Aliens outside the ship try to kill the player; player fights back with weapons.
- **Weapons:** a fixed set of unique weapons, ranged and melee, each with its own in-game name (three so far, 18 planned, see [`planned/weapons.md`](planned/weapons.md)). New ones unlock as blueprints from quests and the story. Ranged weapons use **ammo** made from carried ore (see [Ammo and reloading](#ammo-and-reloading)). The player can't carry all of them: before a mission they pick a **loadout** of a few weapons in the ship and play the mission with it.
- **Enemies:** only aliens for now, one species in several strains and roles (the swarmer so far, in three roles; ten strains and an apex planned, see [`planned/aliens.md`](planned/aliens.md)).
- **Attacking:** one attack button; the equipped weapon decides what it does (shoot, swing). Melee stays on it: a separate melee button would make the melee weapons pointless.
- **Loadout slots take any weapon type**, so a melee-only loadout is a real choice. Melee is never required to get on in a run (nothing that only a melee weapon can do).
- **Starting weapons:** a new player owns the blaster (ranged) and the hammer (melee), and both are in the loadout. The others are unlocked through the game (blueprints, M4); the first one to find is the shotgun. Owned weapons and the loadout belong to each player (see [Co-op progression](#co-op-progression)).
- **First weapons** (`game/weapons/`, values **TBD (playtest)**): the **blaster** fires fast single shots that reach far; the **shotgun** fires a fan of pellets (7 over 30°) that only reaches 12 m; the **hammer** swings at everything within 2.5 m in a 120° arc in front of the player (also several aliens at once), unless something solid is in the way, about 1.5 times a second.
- **Loadout slots:** 2 (`PlayerSettings.loadout_slots`, **TBD (playtest)**). `switch_weapon` (Q, gamepad Y / Triangle) equips the weapon in the next filled slot, also during a mission. Planned: a draw time per weapon (see [`planned/weapons.md`](planned/weapons.md)).
- The hive escalates over a run (see the nests below), and aliens are stronger on bodies farther in (see Tiers in [Missions](#6-missions)). Planned: working miners drawing raiders (see [`planned/aliens.md`](planned/aliens.md)).

### Ammo and reloading

Values in `game/weapons/*_settings.tres` and `game/player/player_settings.tres`, all **TBD (playtest)**.

- **Magazines:** each ranged weapon holds a magazine (greybox: blaster 24 rounds, shotgun 6 shells); a shot uses one round. A magazine upgrade holds more (see [Upgrade stations](#upgrade-stations)). Melee weapons need no ammo, so they're the fallback when the ore runs out; nothing in a run needs ammo.
- **Spare magazines first:** each ranged weapon in the loadout starts a run with 2 spare magazines (`PlayerSettings.spare_magazines`; the shotgun: 2 loads of shells), so with the loaded one that's 3. Reloads take them first, for free; a blaster reload with a spare still throws away the rounds left in the old magazine. More spares are a skill.
- **Then made from carried ore:** once the spares are used, a reload pays ore from the player's cargo for each round it loads, so the carried ore is the reserve. Any ore type works, so it never depends on what a location has; the commonest one is used first (lowest tier: Kyrite, then Olvane, ...), so rare ore is spent last, and rarer ores make more ammo per unit (greybox: one Kyrite unit makes 4 blaster rounds or 1 shotgun shell; Olvane and Tessar 1.25×, mid ores 1.5×, signature ores 2×). Aboard (also in the shooting range) reloads are free, from the ship's stock.
- **Full for every run:** each run starts with full magazines and spares, and so does respawning in the medical bay. Without ore, those three magazines are all there is until the miners deliver, so mining early or taking a melee weapon still matters.
- **Reloading** (`reload`): by hand at any time, and by itself when the magazine runs empty (also when pulling the trigger on an empty weapon once ore is carried again). Without ore to pay for it, nothing happens and the gauge blinks red. The player walks slower while reloading (greybox: 75 % speed).
  - **Blaster (magazine):** a reload throws away the rounds left in the magazine and loads a full one (1.6 s), paying for all of it; with too little ore it loads what the ore pays, and it doesn't start when that would be no more than what's loaded. It can't fire meanwhile.
  - **Shotgun (single shells):** loads one shell after another (0.5 s each) until it's full or the ore runs out; the loaded shells stay. Pulling the trigger stops the reload and fires what's loaded.
  - **Switching weapons** cancels a reload: the blaster's magazine stays empty (it was thrown away), the shotgun keeps the shells it loaded. The rounds of a holstered weapon stay as they are for the run.
- **Timing reload:** pressing `reload` again during a reload is a timing press. Within the window (blaster: 55–70 % of the reload; shotgun: 40–90 % of each shell, so it opens late enough to react to the next shell after a hit) the reload (the shell) finishes right away; outside it, it takes longer (blaster +0.5 s, shotgun +0.3 s per shell), and there's only one press per reload (shell). Not pressing is normal speed, so it's an optional skill, never required. Shotgun shells timed in a row load quicker (5 % each, up to 10 %); a miss or a shell without a press breaks the chain. The window may get an audio cue with the SFX (needed for VR and accessibility).
- **Shown in the world:** on the weapon, the blaster's six counter segments each light up for a sixth of the magazine, and the shotgun's strip has a glowing shell per round; the last segment (shell) blinks amber when it's nearly empty, all of them blink red when it's empty and nothing pays a reload. During a reload the counter fills up like a bar with the window's segment white (red after a miss); the shell being loaded brightens, white within the window. Since the weapon is small at camera distance, the suit ring's ammo arc shows the same (see [UI](#ui-in-the-world-not-on-the-screen)), and it flashes red when a reload can't start (not with a full magazine: then `reload` just does nothing).
- **Handing a teammate ammo** (co-op support role): holding `interact` next to a teammate (within 2 m, for 0.8 s, with no terminal or button in reach) gives them a magazine for their equipped weapon, paid from the giver's ore, by the same rules as a reload (a magazine replaces theirs, shells top it up). The suit ring's outer band fills green meanwhile. Works with local players now; over the network with co-op (M7).
- **Spares shown on the ring:** the equipped weapon's spare magazines are ticks inside the suit ring's ammo arc, in the weapon's ammo color; a partly used one (shotgun shells) is dimmer, used ones are only an outline. They show on a reload and when a spare is used, then fade.
- **Skills** (see [Progression](#12-progression--upgrades)): more spare magazines, keeping the rounds left in a magazine when reloading, reloading at full walking speed (relevant once aliens come in faster and slower variants), planned: the suit rebuilding spare magazines from carried ore during a run (see [`planned/skills.md`](planned/skills.md)). Maybe a reload speed or a wider window as an upgrade part, if playtests ask for it.
- **More feeds** (belts, heat, batteries) come with the planned weapons (see [`planned/weapons.md`](planned/weapons.md)).
- **Alien** (`game/aliens/alien_settings.tres`, all values **TBD (playtest)**): the swarmer (see [Art direction](#art-direction)); its glowing eyes show where it faces. It waits until a living player outside the ship comes within 18 m, then chases the nearest one at 4.5 m/s (slower than the player, so running away works) and strikes at it once per second for 10 damage when in reach. A player gets away beyond 20 m or by going aboard: aliens don't hunt inside the ship. 30 HP; it flashes white when hit; when it dies it collapses, lies still for a moment and sinks into the ground. The blaster deals 10 damage per shot, so three hits kill one. Shots don't hurt other players (no friendly fire).
- **Greybox nests** (`game/aliens/nest/alien_nest.tscn`, values in `alien_nest_settings.tres`, all **TBD (playtest)**): dark crimson mounds on a slimy patch with a glowing mouth. When the outside world appears, 2 guards hatch around each nest and wait there like any alien. Once the mission starts, each nest also sends out **hunters**: aliens that go for the nearest player outside however far away and don't give up because of distance (only by the player going aboard or dying). The hive escalates with the mission's progress, from the start until the storm hits: a nest hatches a hunter every 45 s at first and every 15 s at the end, with at most 1 and later 4 of its hunters out at once; after a hunter dies, the next one comes a full interval later. The glow in the mouth pulses faster as the hive escalates. Destroying nests (see [`planned/nests.md`](planned/nests.md)) and escalation by mining are planned (see [`planned/aliens.md`](planned/aliens.md)).
- **Raiders:** nests also send out raiders, a darker strain that goes for the nearest working miner however far away and ignores players on the way, attacking the miner until it breaks down and then moving on to the next one. Once no miner works, they hunt players like hunters. They only hatch while at least one miner works, the first one a full interval after that, and escalate like hunters with their own values: one every 60 s → 25 s, at most 1 → 2 per nest at once (**TBD (playtest)**). Defending miners means shooting raiders before they get there, or repairing behind them.
- **Pathfinding:** aliens walk around rocks and nests along a navigation mesh that the outside world bakes when it appears (miners placed later aren't part of it, aliens steer around them instead), and steer around each other, so swarms spread around their target instead of stacking.

### Alien look and behavior (direction for the art pass)

- **A hive of bio-organic beasts**, not soldiers or machines: insect- and reptile-like creatures with chitin plates over sinewy flesh, claws, mandibles and teeth, several legs, no tech, no clothes. They are one species in many strains, all sharing the same body language and colors, so any alien reads as "one of them" at a glance.
- **Swarms:** most aliens are small and come in numbers; they scuttle low over the ground, fast and erratic. Danger comes from many at once more than from a single one.
- **Strains:** the planned roster is in [`planned/aliens.md`](planned/aliens.md). Looks from the concept rounds:
  - **Swarmer:** a lean, dog-sized runner on four clawed legs with a long toothed jaw and scythe-like bone blades on its back. Modeled close to the concept (see Art direction above).
  - **Brute:** a four-legged rhino-beetle with heavy layered armor plates and horns. The plates are chitin and must not read as riveted metal.
  - **Spitter:** a beetle body with a swollen, glowing acid sac on its back; the acid glows in flight.
- **Nests:** they live in organic nests / hives grown into the ground, from which they spawn. The ground around a nest can be covered by a living, slimy layer, which also marks their territory. Look: a low, wide mound of chitin and sinew with a glowing opening on top and tendrils spreading over the slime.
- **Escalation and attraction:** the longer a mission runs and the more the players mine, the more aliens come and the stronger the strains get; miners draw them in (see Combat above).
- **Readability:** silhouettes with clear head, legs and claws, glowing eyes or glands that show where they face, colors that don't clash with the ore colors and stand out on the ground: every strain glows in the swarmer's yellow-green (acid included), which no ore uses; raiders and stalkers have darker chitin. Death should read clearly too (collapse, splatter).

## 11. Death & persistence

- Health shows on the suit ring's left arc (see [UI](#ui-in-the-world-not-on-the-screen)) and, for teammates, on a light band around the collar: green, amber below half, red and blinking below a quarter, flashing white on damage. Greybox: 100 HP, **TBD (playtest)**.
- On death: the body drops where it stands, **everything carried this run is lost**, and the player respawns in the medical bay with full health.
- When every player is dead, the mission is lost: the countdown turns grey and says LOST, and after a few seconds (greybox: 3 s, **TBD (playtest)**) everyone respawns aboard and the ramp goes up. The next run starts with a newly picked mission.
- Co-op: the mission ends when every player is either back aboard or dead, as long as at least one made it back. The dead lose what they carried and respawn aboard when it ends (planned with co-op: going down first and being revived by a teammate standing close, and respawning after 30 s instead of waiting, see [`planned/coop-revive.md`](planned/coop-revive.md)).
- When every player dies, the miners on the planet are lost too (see [Miners](#7-miners)).
- **Not too punishing:** losses should hurt, but not set players back for long.
- Planned skills soften it (see [`planned/skills.md`](planned/skills.md)): the **rescue drone** revives a player where they fell once per run, in solo and in co-op, so playing alone isn't unfair without anyone to revive you; the **hardened canister** keeps part of the carried ore on death.
- Back aboard (before the storm gets you): carried resources go into the ship's hold and are spent at the upgrade stations. The hold keeps growing over runs. It shows in the cargo bay as silo clusters (greybox until the cargo bay's art pass): an industrial silo per ore of the game in a fixed spot, ordered by rarity, with a window strip where the ore glows up to its fill level (full at 3000, 1500 and 600 units for common, mid and signature ores, **TBD (playtest)**) and a name plate with the exact amount. Each band is a cluster around a pump hub with pipes to the silos' tops: the common and mid clusters (2 × 2 big silos each, about 2 m and 1.6 m wide, 5 m and 4 m tall) behind the main lane, the commons by the ramp; the signature cluster (2 × 3 smaller silos) in front of the lane, set back from the lane and the ramp so the way in from the ramp stays open. A cluster's far row is shifted half a step sideways, so from the isometric camera no silo hides another's window. The main lane (at least 4 m), the way between the clusters, the way to the lift, the ramp button and the floor signs (moved to fit) stay clear; players can't walk through the silos. Rarer ores get smaller silos. An ore never brought aboard shows as a dark silo with "?" (it stays known once found, even when spent), so the hold doesn't give away what's still to find. The silos are used directly: `interact` next to one opens the hold as a station with it selected; the camera follows the selected silo, and the card shows the ore's name, its band, how much the hold has and the open regions where it's found. `tab_prev`/`tab_next` step through the silos in reading order (rows from the back, each from left to right), the directions to the nearest silo on screen. The ramp's countdown hides meanwhile, since it hangs over the silos by the ramp. The far end of the cargo bay (by the door to the lower deck) stays free for the refinery.
- Players are healed for each new run. Planned: health carries over and the medic's med bed heals (see [`planned/crew.md`](planned/crew.md)).
- **Saving:** automatic, no save button. Progress (the hold, the heat shield and where the ship is, the owned weapons, the loadout, the upgrade levels) is saved whenever it changes aboard; what changes during a mission is saved once it's over, so quitting mid-mission loses that run like dying (the game starts aboard in space again). There is one save. **Reset progress** on the settings terminal wipes it: it asks three times, each time saying that all progress is lost for good, and each confirm button waits a moment (greybox: 1 s, **TBD (playtest)**) so quick repeated presses can't get through. The settings (language) stay. Only while the player is alone: with others in the session the button is disabled with a hint, and everyone keeps their progress.

## 12. Progression / upgrades

Bought in the ship with ores, later also alloys from the refinery (no experience levels, no player classes):

- **Weapons:** each one upgraded on its own, part by part, in MK levels (MK1–MK8, one more with each opened planet); planned: damage, capacity and a part of its own per weapon (see [`planned/weapons.md`](planned/weapons.md)).
- **Armor:** the suit, in MK levels.
- **Skills:** bought with ore like the upgrades, each player their own. Each cluster is a small tree growing out from its center: a skill opens once the one before it is bought (no gates between clusters). Most are a single unlock, some have ranks (shown as the same pips as the MK levels). Skills deeper in a tree cost rarer ores: the first one of a cluster commons only, the next ones a mid ore too, from the fourth on a signature ore (`Skill.depth`; amounts **TBD (playtest)**). Everything can be maxed out completely, so the choice is only what to get first, and there's no respec. A run's role comes from the loadout (e.g. a healer loadout), not from a class.
  - **The first skills** (values **TBD (playtest)**), in four clusters:
    - **Mobility:** **dash** (`dash`: Space, gamepad B; about 4 m in the direction the player moves, else where they aim; a 2 s cooldown, no invulnerability; the suit ring's thin inner ring fills up again during the cooldown), then **reload on the move** (reloading at full walking speed).
    - **Survival:** **reinforced suit** (3 ranks, +20 health each, before the armor's multiplier), then **regeneration** (health comes back at 5 HP/s once the player took no damage for 5 s; the collar band pulses slowly meanwhile; it doesn't replace the medical bay).
    - **Ammo:** **spare magazines** (3 ranks, one more spare magazine per ranged weapon each, one more tick on the suit ring), then **careful reload** (a magazine reload keeps the rounds left in it and only tops it up).
    - **Scanning:** **ore scanner** (a small arrow on the suit ring's edge in the ore's color points to the nearest ore node within 40 m, also one with a miner on it, like the arrow back to the ship), then **damage readout** (numbers on hit aliens; a shotgun's pellets add up into one).
  - **Planned:** the rescue drone, the hardened canister, rebuilding spares and the miner radar next, then about 60 skills in six clusters (Mining and Support added) (see [`planned/skills.md`](planned/skills.md)).
- **Miners:** capacity, speed, drill types, defenses (shields, sentry turrets).
- **New planets:** better ores open the way to the next planet: the engine room's heat shield, paid with the current planet's signature ore (see Opening planets in [Missions](#6-missions)). Each opened planet also raises the highest MK level by one (see Upgrade stations below).

### Upgrade stations

- One station per upgrade room on the lower deck, each the object itself (see [UI](#ui-in-the-world-not-on-the-screen)):
  - **Weapons:** a rack along the wall with every weapon of the game (the ones not owned yet greyed out, "Locked"); picking one lays it on the workbench, where its parts (damage, magazine, later more) are the upgrades. More weapons make a longer rack, which the camera follows.
  - **Armor:** a fitting frame the player steps into; the camera frames their own suit, its parts are the upgrades (so far the whole suit, later helmet, chest, legs), and the real helmet pips light up when bought.
  - **Miners:** a maintenance pad with a miner, its drill, ore tank and hose reel are the upgrades. Planned: a bay of crafted miners (see [`planned/refinery.md`](planned/refinery.md)).
  - **Skills:** a holo graph of all skills, projected above an emitter in the skills room and facing the camera, styled like the star map: each cluster is a constellation with its name, the skills its glowing points, the links between them lit once bought. Bought skills glow fully, ones that can be bought right now pulse, ones that open later stay dim. The mouse pans (dragging) and zooms (wheel) smoothly; on a gamepad or keyboard the highlight jumps to the nearest skill in that direction (the view follows it) and `tab_prev`/`tab_next` jump between clusters. The card scales with the zoom, so it keeps its size on screen, and it sits where it covers no other skill (so the mouse can still point at them) and stays in view.
- The holo card of a selected upgrade shows the MK level as pips, how much the next level improves (compared to MK1), the next level's cost in ore and an "Upgrade" button, plus what the hold has of those ores. Costs the hold can't pay show in red and can't be bought; upgrades of weapons the player doesn't own yet are greyed out ("Locked"). Levels beyond the ship's reach show as faint pips, and at the highest level the ship allows the card says which plate brings the next one ("MK4 comes with heat shield MK2 (engine room).") while still showing its cost. Like the loadout, buying is locked while a mission is underway. The card floats beside the selected part, an amber leader line points from it to the part, and the part gets an amber outline.
- **Greybox** (all **TBD (playtest)**): the weapons rack and workbench, the fitting frame and the maintenance pad are primitives (the pad carries the miner model at three quarters of its size); the player steps to a fixed spot beside the object (into the frame for the armor), so they don't hide it.
- **Up to the ship's reach:** the highest MK level that can be bought is that of the bodies the ship's heat shield reaches (tier + 2): MK3 while only Torvane is open, one more with each plate, MK8 with Zairen. - **Paid from the ship's hold, by one rule** (`game/progression/upgrades/mk_costs.tres`, the same for every upgrade): each MK level costs a common and a mid ore, and from MK3 on also the signature ore of the planet that brought the level, so each planet's signature ore buys that planet's MK level and the way onward (the heat shield). Higher levels ask for rarer commons and mid ores and more of each. ### Co-op progression

- The **host's save** holds the ship, story, crew and quests. Each player keeps their **own weapons, skills and loadout** and brings them along; players can inspect each other's gear. No trading for now.
- Everyone gets the full haul in their own save; nothing is split. The ship's project (the heat shield's next plate) takes what it still needs from the haul before it's stored, so in co-op everyone pays the same (see Opening planets in [Missions](#6-missions)). Planned: every player's own hold in their own save, quest deliveries filling from the haul the same way, and the cargo hatch and the silos showing what the ship took (see [`planned/coop-ore.md`](planned/coop-ore.md)).
- **Drop-in:** friends can join a player's session at any time, also while they're alone on their ship; solo and co-op aren't separate modes, solo just means nobody else is in the session. Invites go out from the comms station on the bridge. Progress can only be reset then (see [Death & persistence](#11-death--persistence)).
- Each player places **their own miners**; a lost miner is lost for its owner.
- **Tier sync:** each body has a maximum MK level (its tier + 2, the same number that caps buying, see [Upgrade stations](#upgrade-stations)); planned with co-op: stronger gear counts as that level, so guests with better gear don't carry the host through. Weapon types and unlocked skills stay. Alien numbers and health scale with the player count.
