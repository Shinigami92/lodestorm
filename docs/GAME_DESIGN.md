# Lodestorm: Game Design Document

> Status: draft / pre-production. Values marked **TBD (playtest)** are intentionally left open and will be tuned once the systems are playable.
>
> This document describes the game as it is built, with its vision and rules. Designs that are decided but not built yet are in [`planned/`](planned/README.md); the order of the work is in [`PLAN.md`](PLAN.md).

## 1. Pitch

A 3D isometric action/mining game with a dark, realistic sci-fi look. The player lives on a spaceship that acts as the hub. From the ship they pick a mission, the ship flies there, and the player heads out to place miners on resource nodes, fight off aliens, collect the mined resources via a hose, and get back to the ship before the storm hits. Resources are spent on upgrades in the ship. A story campaign leads from planet to planet: better ores open the way to the next planet, where rarer ores wait.

### Story and setting (draft)

- **Setup:** a colony in a newly opened star system runs short of supplies. The players are a prospecting crew supplying it, next to other crews, stations and outposts.
- **The mystery:** a first expedition went missing. Following its trail reveals bit by bit what the players don't know at the start: the storms come from the system's star, which is becoming unstable, and they keep growing stronger. The rare ores only form where its radiation is strongest.
- **The ending:** the colony has to leave the system before a big flare, which needs something built from the rarest ores near the star: a flare shield for the colony's convoy, to slingshot around the star and out (the players' heat shield is its small prototype). So the campaign moves inward, planet by planet. The six acts, the expedition's fate, the people and the post-game: see [`planned/story.md`](planned/story.md).
- **How it's told:** NPCs and their quests drive the main story, in conversations aboard that the players start themselves. During runs, NPCs speak over the radio only when it matters out there (the storm warning, quest updates, story moments), each call from the one it concerns, so the radio never becomes noise; everything else they say aboard. Players in co-op may talk over it (e.g. on voice chat), so nothing important is only said over the radio: radio lines stay short and can be listened to again aboard (a comms log). The expedition's logs, found as handcrafted pieces on the planets, are optional lore for players who want to dig deeper; the main story never depends on reading them. Cutscenes are unlikely (costly); key moments can use the camera transitions that exist anyway (e.g. the ship taking off and landing).
- **One star system** for the campaign: six planets from the outside in, one per signature ore (the fourth a gas giant whose two moons carry its ore), four moons, stations and outposts; built so more systems can follow later (see [Missions](#6-missions), Planets, moons and regions).
- **Length:** about 40 h to beat the game; play can go on afterwards (post-game, e.g. higher difficulties with rarer ores, open).

### Architecture implications

- **Multiplayer-ready from day one:** game state changes go through host-authoritative code paths, solo = local host. Co-op is enabled later without rewriting gameplay.
- **Camera abstraction:** desktop uses an isometric camera; VR puts the player's head where the desktop camera is, looking down onto the character from the same direction, free to turn and tilt (planned, see [`planned/vr-view.md`](planned/vr-view.md)). Same gameplay, swappable camera rig. The rig also supports camera transitions (see below).
- **Input abstraction:** all gameplay reads Godot input actions only, so keyboard/mouse, gamepad and VR controllers map onto the same actions.

### Localization and voice

- **Languages:** English and German first, more later. All player-facing text goes through translations from the start, also the text in the world (3D menus, labels, the ship's signs, the station's room names painted on its floors). German runs about a third longer than English, so panels and labels leave room for it. The game follows the system language; the settings terminal in cabin 1 picks another one and saves it (resetting keeps it). How it works: [`TECHNICAL.md`](TECHNICAL.md#localization).
- **Translations by contributors:** the project is on GitHub, so new languages and fixes come in as pull requests (one translation file per language, readable in a diff; the CLA applies).
- **Subtitles for every spoken line**, in every language, shown in the world, not on the screen: a speech bubble above the speaking NPC, radio lines next to the player. They work the same in VR. Names in them (people, the star and its planets, moons, stations and regions, also by a short name like "Ostra", weapons, ores and alloys) stand out in pale gold, so players notice what they can ask about or look up; cards keep them plain. Alien strains aren't highlighted: their names are common words (German "Heiler", "Wächter").
- **Voice-over:** generated ahead of time with a free AI text-to-speech model running locally (Qwen3-TTS, picked by ear, see [`TECHNICAL.md`](TECHNICAL.md#4-asset-pipeline)), one audio file per line and voice language, with voices designed from a written description of each character, never cloned from real people. Every line of a character is cloned from one recording of their voice (the anchor), so they always sound like themselves; each voice language has its own anchor, made from the same description. Voiced so far: Brannik, Nell, Kade, Selin, Tamsin, Okoro, Ilse, Dace, Wren and Hollis, in English and German, every line; Fenna every line in English (so in German too, until her German voice comes). The NPC speaks each line from their head as its bubble shows; the prompt is spoken when a talk starts without the greeting. **Skipping is always allowed:** the voice never holds a player back, so moving on (`interact`, "Continue") cuts the line off at once and the next one starts, and leaving the topic or the talk stops it; players who read faster than the voice speaks go at their own pace, and nobody waits for a line to finish. A line without a recording just shows its bubble. **Talks can continue by themselves** ("Dialogue: Continue by itself" in the settings, off by default, each player's own; values **TBD (playtest)**): a voiced line moves on 1 s after its voice ends, a line without a voice after its reading time (2 s plus 0.06 s per letter of the shown text, so a five-row line stays about 17 s), and after a topic's last line the list comes back. Meanwhile an amber bar fills along the bottom of the card's "Continue"; pressing it still moves on at once, and "Back" leaves the topic. Colonists' remarks aren't affected (they stay until the player walks off), and radio lines always go on by themselves. **The voice language** is its own setting next to the texts' ("Voices": English, Deutsch or Off): until one is picked, the voices follow the texts' language when it has voices, else they speak English; a line missing in the picked language is spoken in English. Still to come (see [`planned/voice.md`](planned/voice.md)): the rest of the cast, and radio lines through a radio filter. Steam asks to disclose AI-generated content on the store page.
- **AI-driven NPCs** (idea for later, an optional experiment): a local language model writing NPC dialogue on the fly. Open: it shares the GPU with rendering (VR needs 90 FPS), it must stay consistent with the quests, it needs content guardrails (Steam asks for them with live-generated content), and its lines would need live voice. Scripted quest dialogue stays the backbone.

## 3. Perspective & art direction

- 3D, isometric camera.
- **The core loop is played in the isometric view** (desktop) / **from the same viewpoint with a free head** (VR). Camera transitions away from it are allowed for specific moments, e.g. moving closer to a terminal for a 3D menu, the ship taking off and landing, or the ramp opening at mission start. They always return to the main view. In VR, transitions must stay comfortable (e.g. fades or moving the viewpoint, never turning the player's head); details **TBD (playtest)**.
- **Desktop camera:** fixed angle looking along the map's diagonal, following the player. Projection (orthographic vs. perspective), angle, distance and follow speed are **TBD (playtest)**; they live in `game/camera/iso_camera_settings.tres` (current: perspective, chosen over orthographic after a first comparison).
- **Controls (twin-stick):** movement is relative to the screen ("up" walks up the screen, whatever the camera angle). The player faces the aim direction: the point on the ground under the mouse cursor, or the right stick. Without aim input (gamepad, stick released) the player faces the movement direction. Whichever of mouse and right stick was used last wins.
- **Target lock:** an alien under the mouse cursor (within 4% of the screen height, let go beyond 7%), or the nearest one within 12° of the right stick and 25 m, gets locked on; without an alien there, a nest does the same way (its body on screen counts), so its defenders are locked first: corner brackets in the player's color frame it on the ground, the player turns to it and shots fly straight at it, so aliens up or down a slope can be hit too (`game/player/player_settings.tres`, **TBD (playtest)**). Without a lock, shots fly level. The mouse aims at the ground under the cursor, also on hills and rock tops; the cursor's ray passes through walls and rock faces (also the ship's walls cut away for the camera), so it always finds the floor or a top.
- **In-game cursor** (`AimReticle`): while the local player aims with the mouse, the hardware cursor is hidden and a reticle in the player's color (a ring with four ticks and a dot, 1.5 m wide) is projected onto the ground or a rock's top where the mouse really points, following slopes (fading out on steep ones, so it doesn't smear down walls) and leaving players and aliens, what they carry, holograms, effects, text and markings like the suit ring out. So it shows the true aim spot, and Tremor's and Wick's landing ring sits right on it. With something locked on, it stays under the cursor, dimmed, while the lock brackets mark the target (`follows_lock` moves it under the target instead, to try). The hardware cursor comes back while a menu or station is open, the game is paused, the player is down, the mouse is over a developer tool or points at no ground. A stick or a VR hand aims by a direction: no reticle (**TBD (playtest)**).
- Readability over detail: ore types, aliens and miners must be recognizable at a glance from the isometric distance (and in VR).
- **Seen behind cliffs:** what matters stays visible behind cliffs, plants and walls as a see-through silhouette (`OccludedSilhouette`, `assets/shaders/occluded_silhouette.gdshader`): players in orange (each co-op player in their own color with M7), aliens in red, miners in pale cyan, ore crystals faintly in their ore's color and unopened crates in amber, but only within 30 m of a player, so finding them stays exploring (**TBD (playtest)**). It's only a depth test, so it works from any camera (VR's head tracking too).
- **Seeing into the passes:** the generated ground keeps the rock on the camera's side low instead of cutting anything away at runtime (see [Missions](#6-missions), Generated outside world): the camera sees over it into every area and pass.

### Art direction

Found by rating four rounds of concept images (generated locally, not part of the repo); the art pass (M5) refines it per asset.

- **Realistic, dark sci-fi:** PBR materials (painted metal, rubber, glass) with wear: scratches, rust, grime and dust. Real proportions; toy-like, chunky, pastel and cartoon looks are out.
- **Rim light for readability:** players, aliens and machines get a strong rim light (fresnel on their materials), so their silhouettes stay readable on dark ground and from the isometric distance. It's the main tool that keeps the realistic look from turning into mush.
- **Muted world, glowing accents:** ground, rocks, plants and hulls stay desaturated (greys, browns, olive). Saturated color is kept for what the player has to read: glowing ore in its type color, alien eyes and glands, lamps, the hose, the suit's collar band and other status lights. These emissive accents also carry most of the diegetic UI.
- **Human tech vs. the hive:** human gear (ship, miners, suits, weapons) is industrial and weathered; the aliens and their nests are biopunk and organic (chitin, sinew, wet surfaces, glowing glands), see [Alien look](#alien-look-and-behavior-direction-for-the-art-pass).
- **Per asset** (from the concept rounds):
  - **Miner:** a big machine, about twice the player's height, with the glowing glass column and the lamp on top. First model in the game (`assets/miners/miner_glow.glb`): a column on four mechanical legs with two equipment boxes, 3.6 m tall, legs standing around the ore crystals; its body (collision) has a 1.5 m radius, **TBD (playtest)**. When placed, it turns its front toward the camera's view (25° past straight on), so the fill column is never hidden behind its equipment boxes.
  - **Player:** a bulky, dark armored suit; the collar band and the ore canisters on the back glow. In the game (`assets/player/player.glb`, generated from a concept image, see TECHNICAL, Generated models): a rounded helmet with a dark visor and a cyan lamp on each side, a thick collar with the light band, chunky pauldrons, plated arms and legs with round knee pads, heavy boots, a backpack with a glowing panel and two lamps on top, the cargo tubes standing up its back; 1.89 m. Animated: idle, running in four directions relative to where the player faces (blended by the velocity, played faster or slower with the speed), the upper body holding the weapon while armed as the weapon is held (relaxed arms aboard the ship, where the weapon is holstered), and falling face down on death. The ranged weapons are raised to aim; Riven is held one-handed (the blade low in front, the left arm up as a guard), Dray two-handed (the haft across the body, the head up by the right shoulder), Jag and Auger two-handed at the hip (the left hand on a side handle), Clout's gauntlet raised in front of the chest (the left hand open as a guard), Mantle's plate on the left forearm across the chest (the right fist behind it). The melee attacks move the arms (upper body only, the legs keep walking): Riven's slash across the front from the right to the left and the next one back, Dray's overhead blow, Auger's thrust, Clout's straight punch, Mantle's shove forward with the shoulder; each stroke takes the attack's time (quicker with the swing speed) and lands right as the attack hits. A braced tool leans in and the arms shake with its motor (Jag cutting, Auger's bit spinning), and while Auger drills, the arms stay out at the thrust's end; while Clout charges, the arm draws back by the shoulder with the body turned away, trembling. The weapon's own model swings a little on top.
  - **Ore nodes:** crystal clusters in many shapes and sizes, also growing sideways out of rock walls; each ore type has its own crystal shape besides its color. Modeled per ore in `assets/ores/<ore>.glb` (shapes: see [Resources](#9-resources)): two ground variants (on a cracked rock pad of the deposit's size, crystals at most 1 m tall so they fit between the miner's legs) and one wall cluster for dressing cliffs later. The crystals glow in the ore's color and its glow pattern (see [Resources](#9-resources)), strong enough to bloom from the camera distance without blowing out to white.
  - **Ground:** textured per biome with three tileable sets made from real geometry in Blender: the open ground (*Dust flats*: sandy regolith with patches of cracked crust and sparse pebbles; *Overgrowth*: dark damp soil under mats of moss, with roots, stones and pale fungus buds), scree (loose stones at the foot of cliffs and in patches) and rock on the cliffs (*Dust flats*: sandstone strata; *Overgrowth*: dark blocky rock with moss in the joints). Small stones are scattered on top as 3D models, so the ground has real silhouettes up close. Details in [`TECHNICAL.md`](TECHNICAL.md#4-asset-pipeline) (Terrain textures).
  - **Rocks:** jagged and cracked, never smooth. Modeled in `assets/rocks/rocks.glb` (boulders, rubble, outcrops, and cliff chunks for dressing cliffs later), the plants and debris of both biomes in `assets/plants/plants.glb`.
  - **Ship:** stands on landing skids (no wheels); the ramp is at the rear between the thrusters.
  - **Weapons:** glowing parts show their state (the rifle's ammo counter, the power core in the hammer's head). Harl is modeled as that rifle (`assets/weapons/harl.glb`), its six counter segments show the ammo (see [Ammo and reloading](#ammo-and-reloading)). Brask (glowing shell strip on its side, one shell per round), Dray (glowing core in its head), Pinner (a compact pistol, the battery cell under its barrel glowing in six segments), Grindle (six barrels that turn as they spin up, the belt drum on its left side with ten segments on its face) Quill (two rails with their coils, its rounds as cells along its right side), Tremor (a stubby wide tube with a hazard band at the mouth, its four rounds as cells on its right side) Wick (a squat box with a short snout and a hopper on top, its charges as cells on the hopper), Scald (a hazard-yellow fuel tank under a pipe ending in a wide nozzle), Kith (a sleek body ending in three emitter rings around a glowing core, cooling fins on top) Zenith (a long lance, a thin barrel through five cooling rings to a glowing tip), Fulgen (a compact body, a coil of four rings around a glowing rod and two prongs with glowing tips, its magazine as ten cells on its right side), Nadir (a squat, heavy body with a wide emitter ring around a dark core rimmed in gold, its battery's wells as cells on its right side), Riven (a long, broad flat blade, over a meter, with a glowing amber edge behind a hazard-yellow guard), Auger (a motor block with a hazard band and a side handle, a short shaft and a big stepped cone of a bit like a step drill, a glowing spiral running up it; the bit spins while the attack button is held, at full speed with a thrust and while drilling the faster the more the drill ramped up, and winds down once it's let go) and Jag (a motor block with a big round blade on an arm, its teeth glowing amber under a hazard-yellow guard, spinning while it saws) are greybox meshes so far; the heat weapons' fuel shows as eight cells on their side, and their nozzle, fins or rings glow red to orange-white with their heat.
  - **Swarmer:** `assets/aliens/swarmer/swarmer.glb`, generated from a concept image (see TECHNICAL, Generated models): a lean runner on four long clawed legs, back about 0.75 m high (about the player's hip), nose to tail tip 1.65 m with a whip tail; a flat skull like a wolf's or a crocodile's with a pale bony face, a toothed jaw and glowing yellow-green eyes, dark crimson chitin over sinew with a row of spines along the back, and two pale bone scythes rising behind the head and sweeping back over the body (1.25 m up). Its legs step on their own over the ground, in a trot (the diagonal pairs together); the body breathes while it waits, runs with the head low and the tail streaming, rears back and snaps forward with the jaw open and the blades swinging forward over the head when it attacks, and collapses onto its side when it dies.
  - **Mite** (`assets/aliens/mite/mite.glb`): a knee-high tick, 0.32 m to the top of its back and 0.55 m long: a swollen, round abdomen under chitin plates with glowing seams and blisters on its back (what makes a pack readable from above on dark ground), a small head shield with hooked mandibles and two glowing eyes, six short legs splayed out sideways with the knees high. It twitches while it waits, scuttles, lunges with the mandibles snapping and flips onto its back when it dies.
  - **Spitter** (`assets/aliens/spitter/spitter.glb`): a beetle, 0.72 m to the top of its domed carapace and 1.35 m long, with a swollen acid sac on its rear back that rises to 1 m (the hive's strongest glow, pinched into lobes by dark chitin ribs), wing cases split down the middle, a blunt armored head with hooked mandibles around a puckered mouth, six sturdy legs. The sac breathes while it waits; to spit it rears its front up, squeezes the sac and throws its head forward and up; it dies on its belly with the sac sagging.
  - **Burrower** (`assets/aliens/burrower/burrower.glb`): a flat, wide digger, 0.46 m high and 1.65 m long, with a broad spade of a head plate (its pale lip wider than the body), small glowing eyes under its rim, pincers below it, heavy segmented plates over a flat back with a faint glow in the seams between them, a short tail stump and six short, thick digging legs, the front pair with big spade claws. The head plate sweeps the ground while it waits; it bites by jerking the plate up and forward and rolls onto its side when it dies.
  - **Warden** (`assets/aliens/warden/warden.glb`): the biggest so far, a hunched, front-heavy guard, 1.45 m at its hump and 2 m long, walking on its knuckles: long, thick front legs ending in heavy chitin clubs, shorter hind legs, a wide hood of plates fanning around a low head with small glowing eyes, layered armor over the chest and shoulders and a cluster of glowing glands in a gap of the chest armor. It scans with its head while it waits, the hood twitching; for its slam it rears up on its hind legs with the clubs raised and the hood flared (the telegraph), then brings them down; it dies crashing forward onto its chest.
  - **Gnawer** (`assets/aliens/gnawer/gnawer.glb`): a low, fast raider built around its jaw, 0.52 m high and 1.35 m long, the head a third of it: broad bone crushing plates with blunt cusps and two fangs, short thick mandibles at the corners, a tall crest, big jaw muscles with a striped glowing gland along each, heavy plates over a sloping back, four short legs, no blades. Its jaw grinds while it waits; it lunges and clamps when it bites and rolls onto its side when it dies.
  - **Alien nest:** a mound of chitin plates and sinew with tendrils spreading over a slime layer and a glowing mouth on top (`assets/aliens/nest/nest.glb`); the mouth and the glands pulse with the hive's escalation; two sets of glowing cracks in its plates show as it's worn down.
- **Cost and performance:** realistic PBR is the most expensive look to build and to render. Texture sizes and LODs are planned per asset type in the art pass, with the VR budget (90 FPS, two views) in mind; **TBD (profiling in M5/M6)**.
- **Colors checked together:** the palette viewer (see [`TECHNICAL.md`](TECHNICAL.md#engine-settings)) shows every color the game uses side by side, normal and for the three common color blindnesses, with the pairs that look nearly the same; new colors (alien strains, weapons, alloys) are checked there against the ones they're seen with before they're used.
- Planned: each body's own light (weak and cool far out, harsh inwards), how dark it may get, the storm's light steps, and four player colors paired with numbers (see [`planned/art.md`](planned/art.md)); the sky per body and a far ring of land beyond the map (see [`planned/vr-view.md`](planned/vr-view.md)).

### UI: in the world, not on the screen

- **No 2D HUD** where it can be avoided. Game information is shown in the world or on the player (diegetic UI). This also keeps desktop and VR the same: a screen-space HUD doesn't work in VR anyway.
- **Where things show:** what a player reads in a fight on the suit ring around their feet (health, ammo, spare magazines, the dash, where hits come from, the pointers), carried resources on the player's back (and ore arriving above them), a miner's fill level on the miner itself (and with its health and hose range on its ring on the ground), mission time above the door and by the storm building up; exact amounts aboard (the silos in the cargo bay). Nothing needs to be looked up during a run. The suit's own lights (the collar band mirroring the health, the weapon's counter) stay as looks and for teammates. Three layers: critical things (low health, an empty or reloading weapon, a hit) are always readable and big, situational ones (spare magazines, the dash) show when they change, details (exact amounts, MK levels) stay aboard. Every kind of marking on the ground has its own shape: the player's ring solid arcs, the target lock corner brackets, pointers arrows on the ring's edge, giving ammo a green band around the ring, a miner's hose range a thin dashed circle (alien telegraphs come as red dashed circles and lines with the aliens, see [`planned/aliens.md`](planned/aliens.md)). Planned: the other weapons' feeds on the ammo arc and the tracked quest's pointer (see [`planned/player-display.md`](planned/player-display.md)).
- **The suit ring** (`SuitRing`, values **TBD (playtest)**): a ring the suit projects on the ground around the player (1.36 m radius, wider than the body; its lines keep at least 2 px on screen). Its halves stay fixed to the screen, not to where the player faces, and both arcs fill from the bottom up, so low values sit on the camera's side where the body never hides them; the top is left open where the body stands.
  - **Left arc, health:** green, amber below half, red and blinking below a quarter (it dims between blinks but never goes dark, so its length still reads), flashing white on damage (under steady damage like the storm's, a pulse every half second). A short red mark on the outer edge points to where a hit came from and fades within a second (up to four at once; the storm's damage comes from nowhere and has none).
  - **Right arc, ammo** (only with a ranged weapon drawn): the magazine in its ammo color (Harl, Quill, Zenith and Fulgen warm white, Brask and Tremor orange, Grindle and Scald amber, Pinner, Wick, Kith and Nadir gold), as a segment per round up to 16 rounds (Brask's shells, Pinner's and Nadir's battery, Quill's, Tremor's and Wick's rounds), else one bar; amber and blinking when nearly empty (a sixth or less), the whole arc blinking red when it's empty and nothing pays a reload. A magazine reload fills it up again with the timing window marked white (red after a miss); with single rounds the round being loaded brightens, white within the window. A drained battery (Pinner) fills it up the same way, without a window. While Quill charges, the charge fills the arc, white once it's full. A heat weapon's heat is a thin band just outside the ammo arc, filling from the bottom (amber, turning red as it nears full, blinking while it vents); a vent fills the ammo arc like a reload, with its window. It flashes white when a timed press finishes the reload and red when a reload can't start.
  - **Ticks inside the ammo arc:** the spare magazines, one per spare a run starts with (a partly used one dimmer, used ones only an outline); they show on a reload, when a spare is used or refilled and on switching weapons, then fade.
  - **Inner thin ring:** the dash, warm white, filling up again while it recharges, gone once it's ready.
  - **Outer band:** fills green while the player hands a teammate ammo.
  - **Ticks under the ring's bottom:** the run's six miners in the miner color, left to right: the crafted ones going along (bright, by number), then standard-issue ones (dimmer); placed ones only an outline. They show for 3 s when one is placed or picked up, while the player places or picks one up, and while they stand next to a miner, and always with "Always show". Not in the top gap: the body hides that from the camera.
  - **Quiet when nothing goes on:** each arc dims to a faint outline 3 s after its last change (a hit, healing, firing, reloading, switching weapons), with its filled share still readable; health below half and a nearly empty magazine keep it lit. Aboard (the weapon holstered) the outline hides too, so only what changes shows. It hides while the player uses a menu and once dead.
  - **Pointers on the edge:** arrows just outside the hit marks: the way back to the ship in its status color (once the door is more than 12 m away, blinking in the last minute, the countdown beyond its tip, pushed out by the text's width so it never covers the ring, and keeping its size on screen when the camera moves in), and the ore scanner's smaller arrow in the ore's color.
  - **On the ground:** it tilts with the ground under the player and is drawn over the ground, grass and low plants, but cliffs and props hide it like the body (the silhouette shows the player then).
  - **Settings** (on the pause panel and the settings terminal): its size (small, medium, large: 0.8, 1 and 1.25 times) and "Always show" (a checkbox), which keeps the arcs, the spare ticks and the miners' ticks lit instead of dimming. While the pause panel is open the ring stays shown and lit beside the card, so a new size shows right away. Health never relies on color alone: it blinks below a quarter, and the arc's length shows the share.
  - **Teammates' rings** show only the health arc inside a thin outline in their player color (1 orange, 2 sky blue, 3 yellow, 4 reddish purple), with no ammo, ticks, dash, hit marks or pointers, so four players don't cover the ground. Only the player a machine plays reads its input; until co-op (M7), the debug panel adds teammates that stand still.
- **Target lock on the ground:** four corner brackets in the player's color frame the locked-on alien or nest, sized to its body and kept square to the screen like the ring; drawn over the ground the same way.
- **Ore arriving:** while a hose delivers, a line above the player shows each arriving ore's badge with the amount ("+180", several ores side by side), counting up from the first unit; once the ore stops coming (slower than 5 units a second) it rises and fades over 2 s. A slow trickle (a miner filling while the player stands by) shows once it adds up to 10 units; one that stops before that, or that a hose starts over (the next miner), is forgotten. Spending ore (ammo) shows nothing.
- **The object is the menu.** Where something is chosen or bought, the player uses the thing itself, not a screen next to it: the weapon on a workbench, their own suit in a fitting frame, a miner on its maintenance pad, the star map on the holo table, the skill graph. The camera frames it, its parts become selectable (a weapon's barrel for its damage, its magazine, a miner's drill), and a small **holo card** beside the selected part shows what it is, its level pips (the same pips as on the weapons and the helmet), what the next level does, its cost and the action. This holds any number of weapons, upgrades and skills (there may be 50–100 skills), gives the rooms things to stand at (they fill with assets and NPCs later), and German only has to fit one kind of card. Flat **terminals** stay for system things only: the settings in cabin 1 and the comms station on the bridge.
- **Using one** (values **TBD (playtest)**): a player walks up and uses it (`interact`) from any side they can reach: within reach of the object itself (about 1.2 to 1.6 m from its edge), not just from a spot in front of it. They stay where they are (no stepping to a fixed spot), stand still facing what's selected, and their camera moves in to frame it (keeping the isometric angle); nobody standing in front of it hides it. Leaving moves the camera back. One player uses it at a time.
  - **Mouse:** the cursor points at the parts and the card's buttons, like a 2D menu.
  - **Gamepad and keyboard:** movement (left stick, WASD, also the d-pad and arrow keys) moves the highlight to the nearest part (on a screen: button) in that direction (the nearest within about 60° of it, so the near row comes before the one behind it; where several stations share a room, like the cargo bay's silos, furnaces and ingot rack, across all of them, never into another room; on the weapon racks along their grid: left and right along a line, up and down to the nearest weapon on the line above or below), `interact` presses the card's action; `tab_prev`/`tab_next` (LB/RB, Q/R; E is `interact`) jump between groups (the next row of weapons, the next skill cluster); `pause` (Escape, Menu) or B steps back and closes.
  - **VR (later):** a controller points like the mouse does.
  - Cards and screens lean back to face the camera and glow in the dark rooms (cyan text and frames, the highlight in amber like the floor markings). **Text size:** body text is at least about 2% of the framed view's height (about 22 px at 1080p). Every card and screen is tested to fit in every language (German runs about a third longer).
- **Starting up:** while the engine starts, the boot splash shows the game's emblem (a glowing cyan ore crystal in a ring of storm clouds with amber lightning) and its name on black; it turns into the **boot screen**, the same picture in the world, where a thin cyan ring around the emblem fills while the game loads. Once it's full, the title view fades in from black (the boot screen's last frame stays until the title's first one is drawn). It's the only loading screen (see [Spaceship](#5-spaceship-hub)); it shows no text to translate.
- **A title, not a main menu:** with one save there's nothing to choose. The game opens on a **title view in the world**: the ship drifting in space, seen from outside (its hull closed, the view drifting slowly around it), the game's name as a hologram above it and "Press any button" below. Any button (a key, a mouse or gamepad button; moving the mouse or a stick doesn't count) ends it, and the camera glides into the ship to the player in cabin 1. After a while without input (greybox: 30 s, **TBD (playtest)**) the credits scroll there instead of the prompt; any input brings the prompt back.
- **The pause panel:** `pause` (Escape, Menu/Options) with no menu open shows a holo card beside the player's suit ring, facing the camera, which moves in like at a station, framing the player, the whole ring (its marks, pointers and the ship's countdown) and the card; the card never covers the ring and keeps its size on screen, so its text stays readable. The player stands still. It holds **Resume**, **Settings** and **Quit**; `pause` again or "Resume" closes it, and on the other pages `pause` steps back, focusing the button that opened the page. Since the card keeps to the bottom of its screen, a page's "Back" sits where the first page's "Quit" appears: after a page changes, a mouse click only counts once the pointer has moved a little. **Solo, the world stops** while it's open (aliens, the timer, the storm, the ship's flight); it also opens by itself when the game's window loses focus. **In co-op the game goes on**: the card's title says "Menu" instead of "Paused" with the note "The game goes on", and the player can still be attacked, like at any station.
- **Settings in one place, reachable everywhere:** the same settings show on the pause panel's settings page and on the settings terminal in cabin 1: the language, the voice language ("Voices", see [Localization and voice](#localization-and-voice)), whether talks continue by themselves ("Dialogue", same section), the suit ring's size and "Always show" and **Log files**: "Open folder" shows the game's log files in the system's file manager, to attach to a bug report. The sound volumes are a page of their own, **Sound** (see [Sound](#sound)), opened from the settings like the credits. The terminal also keeps the one dangerous thing, **Reset progress** (see [Death & persistence](#11-death--persistence)). Both have a **Credits** page: who made the game, the tools, and the third-party licenses (Godot's own and those of the components it's built from); the authors of recorded sounds join them once the first recording that needs a credit is used (see [Sound](#sound)).
- **Quitting:** "Quit" on the pause panel works like the bed: aboard it saves and quits to the desktop at once, fading to black; during a run it asks first, since quitting loses the run (one confirm, "Cancel" focused). The bed in one's own cabin (cabin N for player N) stays as the in-world way: a station whose card offers "Sleep", which saves the progress one last time, fades to black (the camera stays on the bed) and quits. No confirmation, since nothing is lost aboard; during a run the card warns that sleeping loses it (what changes during a run is only saved once it's over). Other players' beds can't be used. **Inviting friends** (with co-op, M7): at a comms station on the bridge, which opens the platform's invite (the Steam overlay is the platform's, not a HUD); friends come aboard through the cargo bay. The comms station comes with co-op, together with the comms log (radio lines to listen to again). Planned: leaving and ending a co-op session from the pause panel, joining a friend from the title view, and both in VR (see [`planned/menu-pause.md`](planned/menu-pause.md)).
- **Exceptions:** developer tools (debug overlay for tuning) are allowed in 2D.

### Sound

Weighty and real, no cartoon or chiptune: human gear clanks, hisses and hums (industrial, worn), weapons punchy but not arcade. The menus are silent for now (their first sounds were dropped after listening; they come back with their own round). All levels **TBD (playtest)**.

- **Made by our own scripts:** every sound is synthesized by Python scripts in the repo (oscillators, noise, envelopes, filters, layered), deterministic, so it can be rebuilt or tuned any time and is entirely the project's own. Recordings (CC0, or CC-BY with a credit; never NC, ND or SA) come in only where synthesis isn't convincing, processed by the same scripts; none are used yet. **All of them are placeholders**, a first proof of the sound: a better one replaces a sound's files under the same names, and nothing in the game changes. New sounds come in 2–3 **takes**, picked by ear on a local listening page; only the picked one stays.
- **Variation:** sounds that repeat have 2–6 variants and a small random pitch (±3–5 %), so shots never sound like a loop.
- **Loops** (the drill, the hose, the repair, the storm, a furnace's roar) play seamlessly as long as what makes them goes on, from where it happens (the storm all around).
- **Where the ear is:** the game is heard from the local player (not from the camera far above), turned like the camera, so left and right match the screen and the falloff fits what's on screen. Sounds in the world come from where they happen (the gun, the player) and stop while the game is paused; flat sounds (a volume slider's sample) go on. In co-op each player hears from their own player; VR (M6) puts the ears at the head.
- **When it gets busy:** each sound has a limit of how many play at once (shots 4–6, cues 1); when more start, the farthest are dropped. **Critical cues** for the local player always play and duck the effects and ambience by 6 dB while they do: the timing window's tick, a hit taken, the low-health pulse, the storm warning. A teammate's timing cues and pulse aren't heard.
- **Playable muted:** nothing is told only by sound; everything a sound says is also shown in the world (the suit ring, the light, the cards).
- **Volumes:** buses Master → Effects, Ambience, Voice (the NPCs' voices) → Radio (crew calls, through a band-pass filter and a little distortion, so the same voice sounds like a radio; it follows "Voices" until it gets its own slider with the comms log), later Interface with the menus' sounds and Music; a slider each (10 % steps) on the settings' Sound page ("Volume", "Effects", "Ambience", "Voices"), saved with the other settings; moving "Volume" or "Effects" plays a shot as a sample, "Ambience" the storm, "Voices" Brannik asking "How can I help you?" in the voice language (so does picking a voice language).
- **The sounds so far:**
  - **Weapons:** Harl's, Brask's, Pinner's, Grindle's and Quill's shots, Dray's swing and its hit (only when it strikes something), Riven's slash, its heavy third slash and its hit, Auger's thrust, its hit and its drill (a loop rising with it), Jag's saw (a loop following its blade) and its bite into what it touches, Clout's punch (the pistons' bang), its heavy hit, its charge (a straining servo whine rising with it) and the latch when it's full, a thrown alien landing and slamming into rock, Mantle's bash and its hit, its field's quiet hum (a loop while it stands), a blow or shot stopped (deeper for a heavy one), the field breaking and coming back, Grindle's barrels spinning (a loop, its pitch following the spin), Quill's charge (a hum rising with it) and a ping when it's full, Tremor's hollow launch thunk, a blast (Tremor's shells, Wick's charges), Wick's toss, a charge armed (two low beeps) and tripped (fast rising beeps), Scald's roaring flame, Kith's soft, warm beam and Zenith's searing drone (loops while they fire), an overheat's ping and alarm, a vent's hiss, a fuel cell seated, Fulgen's crack of lightning, Nadir's deep whomp, a gravity well's throbbing hum (a loop while it's open) and its burst, Pinner's battery full again after running dry, Nadir's soft chime for each well recharged, the empty click (a reload that can't start, a drained battery), the reload starting, a magazine seated, each shell going in; for the local player the window's tick and whether the timing press hit or missed.
  - **Player:** a hit taken (a thud, the armor plate clanking, sparks), a slow pulse below a quarter of the health (a heartbeat every 1.1 s), the dash (a rising whoosh), and subtle **steps**: one every 2 m walked on the ground (in time with the run clips' legs; none while dashing or in the air), a boot on gravel outside, on the metal deck aboard (the ship's floors and its ramp), crunching on glass shards (the Glass plains), squelching in mud (the Overgrowth). Teammates' steps carry only a few meters. Death has no sound yet (the synthesized takes didn't work).
  - **Aliens** (the hive wet, clicking and chittering; every strain in its own voice, bigger ones lower, all with the swarmer's sounds for now): a chitter when one notices a player and every 3–7 s while it chases (not under the ground or while hidden), its bite or slash landing, hits in its flesh (a wet thwack) or on its armor (a hollow knock), its death. Every heavy attack's **telegraph** sounds with its wind-up, rising into the strike: the pounce (a hissing rattle), the charge (a snort and a growl), the slam (a deep groan), the spit (acid gurgling), the bloater swelling, the shrieker drawing breath and then its **scream**, the stalker's hook creaking, the darter's wings buzzing, the burrower rumbling under the ground, the Ravager's hide tearing open.
  - **Nests:** a deep, wet throb with each pulse of the glow, faster and louder as the hive escalates; a wet pop for each alien hatching (not the guards it starts with); hits in its flesh; a groaning swell for a burst's warning; the collapse (a crunch, the mound caving in, gas hissing out, its hum dying).
  - **Mining:** a miner set down (a thud, the clamps, a hiss), its servos while it's placed or picked up (a whining motor and whirring gears, rising in pitch as it turns real, falling as it fades; quiet while it waits for its player), the latches snapping shut when it's picked up, its drill while it mines (a motor humming, the bit grinding), the hose while ore flows (a dull rush, a pump, grains rattling), a soft two-tone chime when it's full, an alarm when it breaks down (its motor stalling with a bang, a warning buzz), the repair while a player mends it (a ratchet, a wrench on steel, a torch).

## 5. Spaceship (hub)

- Multiple rooms with NPCs. The ship is big: the whole hub is its interior.
- **No loading screens** (but the boot screen at the start, see [UI](#ui-in-the-world-not-on-the-screen)): the ship and the planet outside are one world. Players walk from the hub down the ramp straight into the mission. That's also why the weather outside matters: while the ship flies and lands, a storm around it hides the next location being loaded, so it acts as the loading screen.
- **Landing gear:** the landed ship stands on six legs along its sides (the hull 1.5 m above the ground, **TBD (playtest)**); nobody walks under it. The legs pull up into their housings in space and in flight, and come down in the touchdown's last 2.5 s. The ramp is 10 m wide and 5 m long and reaches the ground at 30°.
- **The ramp always leads out to the right** (screen right): players walk from the inside to the right into the mission, and come back from the right.
- **Ramp button:** the ramp is closed by default. A button on the inner wall next to the door lowers it, once a mission is picked at the holo table; this also locks the mission and resets its timer. Pressing it again before anyone stepped out raises the ramp again. Once everyone is back aboard, the ramp goes up and the mission is done: the next run needs a newly picked mission.
- **No weapons inside the ship** (nor in a station): weapons are holstered aboard, except in the shooting range, where players try them without a mission. They only work inside the range (not in its doorway), and a force field in the doorway stops every shot, so nothing leaves the range. Targets stand at its far end. Reloads there are free (the ship's stock), so trying weapons and practicing the timing reload costs nothing. Idea for later: the targets show damage numbers on hit.
- **Camera inside:** while a player is aboard, the roof and the walls facing the camera are cut away (they still cast shadows), so the camera can see inside. Inner walls between the camera and the corridor keep a low stub, so the rooms stay outlined. On the lower deck, the whole upper deck is cut away too; on the upper deck, the lower deck is hidden under its floor. Some rooms frame themselves: in the shooting range the camera looks at the whole room instead of the player, so the targets stay on screen from the firing line.
- **Two decks**, nose on the left, tail with the ramp on the right (like a cargo plane's rear ramp). A corridor runs along the middle of each deck; lifts join the decks at both ends, one by the bridge and one in the double-height cargo bay, so a run is a loop: ramp → cargo bay → lower deck (upgrades, armory) → lift → bridge (pick a mission) → upper deck → cargo bay lift → ramp.
  - **Upper deck (command and living):** cockpit at the nose, the bridge next to it, then the player cabins, the crew cabins and the mess hall.
  - **Lower deck (work):** the specimen lab in the nose (empty so far: its tanks for the strains found and the statistics station are planned, see [`planned/ship-interior.md`](planned/ship-interior.md)), medical bay (respawn point, four med beds) and engine room behind it, the shooting range, the armory (loadout) and one upgrade room per vendor (weapons, armor, skills, miners), each with its station (see [Upgrade stations](#upgrade-stations)).
- **Armory:** four lockers along the back wall, one per player (numbered, with a strip in the player's color; planned colors: see [`planned/art.md`](planned/art.md)), where they pick their loadout (see [UI](#ui-in-the-world-not-on-the-screen)). In each, under an amber "Loadout" sign, are two cradles, slots 1 and 2, holding the weapons that go along on the next mission (the card says so too). Every weapon of the game hangs once on the **weapon wall**, a free-standing wall in the middle of the armory, in rows by origin (colony arms on top, then the converted mining tools, then the expedition's prototypes; each row named at its start, in the order of the campaign; an origin without weapons yet gets no row, and the rows grow with the weapons), the ones the player doesn't own yet see-through ("Locked"), which hints at what's still to find; under each weapon in their loadout its slot's number shows in the player's color. Using their locker, the player selects a weapon in a cradle or on the wall, and the camera follows it there and back; on the wall the card floats above the wall over the weapon, so it hides no other weapon (its card: name, how it plays, ranged or melee, the MK pips of its parts, a button per slot). With the keyboard or a gamepad the highlight moves over the wall as a grid, up from its top row into the cradles and down from there back to the wall; `tab_prev`/`tab_next` jump to the first weapon of the previous or next row. The selected slot (its cradle's strip lights up, its number turns amber) is the cradle selected last, or an empty one, so the next weapon fills it; `interact` (or the card's slot buttons, or the keys 1 and 2 straight away) puts the weapon into that slot (if it's in the other slot, the two swap) or takes it out again; a slot may stay empty, but one weapon always goes along (the last one can't be taken out). Only the player a locker belongs to can open it; lockers of players who aren't in the session stay shut. The loadout is locked from the ramp going down until the run is won or lost: the doors stay shut meanwhile. The wall shows the weapons of the player using a locker, else of the local player. Using the wall itself (from either side, anywhere along it) opens the player's own locker with the weapon of the top row nearest to them selected. Greybox: a dark panel standing on the floor, weapons 1.3 m apart, rows 0.6 m apart (**TBD (playtest)**).
- **Lifts:** a platform for up to four players in a shaft beside the corridor. Stepping on it sends it to the other deck (greybox: after 0.6 s, 2 s ride, **TBD (playtest)**); riders who came along have to step off before it leaves again. Waiting at the gate on the other deck calls it. Gates close the shaft on every deck the platform isn't at.
- **Walking the ship:** the bridge sits at the nose, far from the ramp. Setting course in the cockpit starts the flight, and players walk aft and gear up while the ship flies and lands, so the walk isn't dead time (flight time **TBD (playtest)**).
- **Bridge:** missions are selected here. A holo table in the middle is the navigator (see Mission selection below); its hologram of the mission location (a small copy of the location, the ship's spot highlighted) doesn't turn: it lies the way the ship will land, the ramp side to the right like aboard, so what lies beside the ship in the hologram lies beside it outside. Players gather around it to see where they're going. Planned beside it: the quest board (see [`planned/quests.md`](planned/quests.md)). The **comms station** on the bridge invites friends (see [UI](#ui-in-the-world-not-on-the-screen)).

## 6. Missions

- A mission is defined by: **location**, **timer/duration**, **difficulty**, and the quests that can be done there.
- Target duration: about 15–30 min per mission; exact timers **TBD (playtest)**.
- Planets farther in are more dangerous and have rarer resources.
- **Generated outside world:** the world outside the ship is generated for every run from a seed and a **biome** (`game/locations/generated_location.tscn`, biomes in `game/locations/biomes/`). The picked mission sets the biome, the seed, the size (by the region's length) and (by its difficulty) the number of nests; the world the ship stands in before the first mission is picked has a random biome. Greybox, all values **TBD (playtest)**:
  - **Layout:** open **areas** joined by **passes**, with the biome's **barrier** everywhere else (`LocationLayout`, a graph laid out per seed; see What makes a biome below). The passes form a tree with a few **loops** (about one per five areas, more in the Overgrowth), so there are ways around and **dead ends**; they never cross, and every area can be reached. The landing yard in front of the ramp is the first area; the ship stands on a flat landing site.
  - **Size:** a standard mission has 11 areas, a region's length scales it (`VoyageSettings.location_areas`); the areas spread over a square that grows with their number (11 areas: about 420 m with the rock around it; the farthest area 250–450 m along the passes, 40–75 s on foot). Every outline wobbles, so no area is a circle and no pass a tube.
  - **Kinds of areas** (where they lie in the graph decides): the farthest dead ends are **ore pockets** (small, with the rarest ore), the next ones **nest hollows** (sunken, with two nests); where three or more passes meet is a **crossroads**; in the far half some areas are **plateaus** (a mesa with a level top and a natural rock ramp up its side) and **craters** (a bowl with a rim); the rest are **basins**. Pockets and hollows grow with the layout (about one each per eight areas), plateaus and craters too (one each per ten). Areas can hold a point of interest or a set piece (see below); the landing yard and the ore pockets stay free of the generic ones.
  - **Points of interest and set pieces** (map parts, `game/locations/parts/`, greybox until the art pass): a part fills one area, standing on ground flattened under it, with its props (which block players, aliens and shots) in the middle and a walkable ring around them, so it never blocks a pass. It turns its front towards the way in. Its markers say where its ore nodes (the region's commonest, a middle or its rarest ore), nests, crates and quest sites go; ore and nests go there first, the usual placement fills in the rest. Each part says which kinds of areas it fits; its entry in a pool or in a region's set pieces (`PartPlacement`) says where it goes (near the ship, in the middle, far out, in a dead end, or anywhere) and how often it's picked. About one generic point of interest per four areas, picked from the biome's pool (`LocationBiome.part_pool`); a region's **set pieces** (`Region.set_pieces`) are always placed, in the biggest area that fits (a crater set piece turns a basin into a crater if there is no crater). First parts: a **wreck** (a broken hull half sunk in the ground, a crate), an **abandoned outpost** (prefab modules and a landing pad, a crate), a **rich vein** (a wall of crystals in a dead end, the rarest ore in front of it), a **meteor crater** (a meteorite in a crater, ore around it; later the hook for the late deposit) and an **expedition camp** (a survival pod and a blinking beacon, a quest site; only looks for now). Every region has one set piece, e.g. Redscar Flats the outpost, Hollow Mire the expedition camp, Cinder Steps a big crater, Pale Basin a wreck, Glare Fields a rich vein (`game/world/star_system.tres`). Points of interest show in the landmark color in the location hologram, each with a thicker beam so it reads at the hologram's scale (crates don't show, so finding them stays a discovery). Generic points of interest come up about once per four areas, the least used ones first and each at most twice per location; a dead end for a rich vein lies in the far half (rarest ore next to the ship would skip the way out). The ground under a part blends back over 3–8 m, the shortest that keeps every step as it was (a crater's bowl needs the long one); a part's props may stand where a pass's lane runs into the middle of an area, as few as possible, since the walkable ring keeps the way through open.
  - **Crates** (in parts only, never scattered): `interact` opens one (a press, no hold), its lid swings open and what it holds flies to the player; an unopened crate has an amber light band around it that goes dark once it's open, and shows behind props and cliffs within 30 m like ore. Once per run. What it holds is decided, not rolled: a fragment an accepted quest put in this region (see [Quests](#quests)) if one is still to find, otherwise a **supply cache** that refills the opener's spare magazines (every ranged weapon of the loadout; an opened crate is spent even when they were full). No ore.
  - **What makes a biome** (data on `LocationBiome`; the same graph generator serves every biome, a new biome picks from these):
    - its **barrier**, what keeps players out between the areas: **walls** (raised rock nobody can climb, see Ground below; `LocationWalls`), **gaps** (`LocationGaps`): ground lowered below the floor beside the open ground (its width varying, 9–15 m in the Frost fields, 9 m deep), beyond which the ground comes back up to the floor's level as land nobody reaches, up to the rim, or **gaps and walls**: gaps beside the open ground with the rock rising behind them (two areas close together have only the gap between them), or **growth and walls**: a band of growth beside the open ground at its level (laid out like the gaps, without the drop) with low rock behind it. Gaps come as **bands** along the edge (crevasses, lava channels) or as round **pits** bitten into the barrier (sinkholes, the rock between them), and may be filled (lava, brine: a flat surface over the gaps, their bottom level then). Players and aliens see and shoot across a gap but walk around to the next pass (the navigation mesh goes around it); gaps never hide anything from the camera. A gap's edge has a raised **lip** (0.4 m, in the lip tint: snow, salt crust, cooled lava) with **invisible walls** behind it, on their own physics layer (`PhysicsLayers.WALKING_ONLY`) that stops players and aliens but not shots, sight lines or the aim, so nobody falls in or gets stuck (a growth band's walls stop shots and sight too, on the world's layer); a **fence** may stand on those walls (a mesh drawn many times along them, only looks: the Glass plains' shard fences). The rock rises in flat **layers** (mesas), in long, sharp **ridges** or in **slabs** (cells about 22 m across, each a step below, at or above the open ground nearest to it). With **terraces** (`terrace_step`) every area lies on a slab of its own level: the landing yard at 0, the others from one step below to two above, each at most a step from the areas its passes lead to and never below the landing yard next to it (a loop that would climb more is left out). Heightfield only: no caves or overhangs.
    - its **passes**: **winding** ways through the rock, or **bridges**: short, straight crossings (at most 22 m between two areas in the Frost fields, loops up to about 30 m), narrow (10–13 m of open ground, a few m walkable between the lips) and in the lip tint, raised in some biomes (causeways, with a ramp at each end); the areas then lie close together (placed beside each other, a bridge's length apart), and a shrunk ore pocket moves towards its bridge so it stays short. With gaps and walls, a bridge always crosses a gap (it's widened beside the bridge), never the rock. Between areas at different levels a bridge is a **ramp** (rising 0.3 m per m from the lower area's level to where the pass leaves the higher one, measured along the pass so the slope holds however the outlines wobble; it reaches into the lower area when the two lie close). Or **slot canyons**: narrow, S-shaped ways (they bend to one side, then the other), the rock beside them higher than beside the areas. Or **trails**: S-shaped like slot canyons (without the higher rock), with a trodden **path** down the middle (3 m, darker packed soil, without scree; nothing grows on it). With many loops most areas are crossroads, so a biome's own kinds take crossroads too when there are too few basins (the ones with the fewest passes first).
    - **its own kinds of areas**, on top of the shared ones (about one each per ten areas).
    - its plants and rocks (`ScatterType`), which can be limited to some kinds of areas (`area_kinds`: the shard gardens' spires, the fungal groves' stalks), grow only on a growth band (`on_barrier`) and keep room to other blocking things (`spacing`, so aliens find their way between a grove's stalks).
    - at most **one hazard**: mild, readable and avoidable, never in the landing yard, on a pass's lane or under ore, nests or map parts, and it hinders aliens too, so luring aliens into it is a tactic (`GroundHazard` zones; whoever stands in one is told through their `GroundContact`; damaging ones hurt players and aliens standing in them).
  - **Frost fields** (built in greybox; the textures, the snow bridges' meshes and the weather come with the art pass): gaps (**crevasses**) around wide ice sheets, walls only at the location's edge, **snow bridges** between the sheets; wide sightlines, choke points at the bridges. Its own kinds: the **ice shelf** (the biggest basins: flat, with up to three jagged **cracks**, 3–5 m wide, running in from beyond its edge between the passes coming in and ending before any lane) and the **frozen lake** (an area of at least 22 m radius, flat, with a round lake 0.5 m below its shore in the widest gap between its passes, shrunk until it keeps off the lanes; dropped, the area a basin, if that leaves less than 7 m). Its hazard is **slick ice** on the frozen lakes: feet grip at 15 % (the acceleration of players and aliens), so they slide a little when they start, turn and stop; the ice is tinted pale blue.
  - **Salt pans** (built in greybox): the most open biome, for range fights. Low walls (4 m) with round **sinkholes** (5–9 m across, 7 m deep, dark brine at their bottom) bitten into them along the open ground's edge; long raised **causeways** (up to 34 m, 1 m above the pans, in the salt crust's tint) between the areas, with brine on both sides. Its own kinds: the **sinkhole field** (an area dotted with round pits, 3–5.5 m in radius, at least 7 m apart and off its middle, the ones close to a lane left out: a maze in the open; with fewer than three left, a basin) and the **brine flat** (the biggest basins: flat, smooth and empty, no plants or rocks). No hazard.
  - **Dust flats** (built; its own textures and plants, see Biomes below): the baseline, close corners and cover. High layered **mesas** (8 m cliffs, rising in 5 m layers) around the areas, **slot canyons** between them (9–11 m wide, room for aliens' paths at the pinches, S-shaped, the rock beside them 5 m higher than beside the areas, fading in over 4–16 m from the areas). Its own kinds: the **butte field** (an area of at least 22 m radius with 2–4 **buttes**, rock pillars 3–6 m in radius with a flat top 5 m above the ground, at least 8 m apart and 6 m off its middle, the ones close to a lane left out: cover; with fewer than two left, a basin) and the **dry wash** (one of the biggest basins, at least 26 m in radius: stretched along the passes coming in, about twice as long as wide, with a sandy **bed** winding along it: 8 m wide, 1.5 m below its banks, sloping over 3 m, smooth, lighter and without scree, no plants or rocks on it). No hazard.
  - **Glass plains** (built in greybox; the glass meshes and textures come with the art pass): layered, shooting down from above. Every area is a **terrace** on its own level (3 m steps); at the open ground's edge the land drops a step (5–8 m wide) with a **shard fence** along the edge (glass spikes on the invisible walls: they block walking, not shots), beyond it **slabs** at mixed levels (a step below, level or a step above). Straight **ramps** (at most 20 m between two areas, 12–15 m wide) between the terraces, fenced on both sides. Its own kinds: the **terrace stairs** (an area of at least 28 m radius whose passes leave a gap of at least 160° free: two square slabs at its back, the lower one a step up with a ramp up its side, the upper one two steps up on its far side, with a ramp up across the lower slab's front) and the **shard garden** (an area of at least 20 m radius with clusters of glass **spires** that block walking and shots: cover; they grow nowhere else). Its hazard is the **shards**: 3–5 patches in every shard garden, 2–3.5 m in radius, with splinters lying on them in a pale glass tint; they hurt every player and alien standing on them 4 damage per second (every 0.5 s). The crunch underfoot comes with the sound effects (step 9 B2).
  - **Ash fields** (built in greybox): **lava channels** (6–10 m wide) beside the open ground, glowing lava 3 m below the floor, with high, sharp **ridges** behind them; narrow **crust crossings** (at most 20 m) in cooled-lava dark between the areas; choke points and timing. Its own kinds: the **lava island** (its ore pockets: lava all around, at least 16 m of it, reached by its one crossing, at least 16 m long) and the **vent field** (an area of at least 20 m radius with 5–8 vents). Its hazard is the **vents**: each rests 6–12 s, then glows (a pulsing disc over its reach) and rumbles (it shakes) for 1.5 s and bursts with a plume, 15 damage to every player and alien within 3 m. Their reach is painted scorched on the ground, so they're seen from afar; they stand apart, on flattened ground, off the lanes. The rumble's sound comes with the sound effects (step 9 B2).
  - **Overgrowth** (built in greybox; the giant stalks and roots get their own meshes with the art pass): a maze, ambushes. **Growth walls**: a band of giant fungal stalks and roots (5–8 m wide) beside the open ground, at its level, with low rock (4 m cliffs, 3 m layers) behind it; its walls stop walking, shots and sight; tall tubes and caps grow on the band (up to about 8 m), only low roots on the camera's side. **Trails** through the growth (13–15 m of open ground, 7–9 m walkable between the lips, room for aliens' paths), many loops (about 0.6 per area); its areas are 2 m bigger than they'd be with walls (18–36 m in radius), since the lips take a strip of their edge. Its own kinds: the **mire** (an area of at least 24 m radius with 3–5 round **mud pools**, 3.5–7 m in radius, at least 4 m apart and 4 m off its middle, inside the area's outline with their shore and off the lanes; level, 0.4 m below the ground around them, smooth, with a dark, wet sheen; with fewer than three, a basin) and the **fungal grove** (an area of at least 20 m radius with giant stalks and mushrooms, 2–3 times the plants' size, at least 4 m apart: cover, short sightlines; they grow nowhere else). Its hazard is the **mud** in the mires' pools: players and aliens walk at 60 % of their speed in it (`GroundHazard.speed`); the dash isn't slowed, it's the way out. The squelch comes with the sound effects (step 9 B2).
  - **Ground:** gentle swells on the open ground; the rock rises in a steep cliff (8 m in the Dust flats, 7 m in the Overgrowth, over 4–5 m) and higher in flat layers behind it. **On the camera's side** of the open ground (the desktop camera never turns, and the mission tells the location where it looks) the rock starts as a 3.5 m ledge and rises behind it in terraces no steeper than the camera looks down, so it never hides the floor beyond its foot; the cliffs the camera looks at keep their height. Only low plants and rocks (up to 1 m) grow on that rock. The first step onto the rock is at least 3 m everywhere. Nobody gets stuck: every spot players can walk to leads back to the ship (a drop off a mesa lands in its basin), and nothing can be climbed onto the rock. Steep ground shows rock, scree collects at the foot of cliffs and in patches, the rock's top is a bit lighter (see [Art direction](#art-direction), Ground). Invisible walls stand at the edges, behind a higher rim of rock.
  - Plants and rocks per biome, scattered in patches. Big ones (trees, boulders) block players, aliens and shots; small ones (bushes, grass) are only looks and fade out in the distance. A lane along every pass (up to the middle of the areas it joins) stays free of blocking ones, ore nodes and nests. Ground and plant colors stay muted so ores and aliens stand out.
  - **Ore and nests by the way there:** ore fields of 1–3 nodes of one type. The first two lie 25–55 m from the ramp, so they're found right away, and have the region's commonest ore; every ore pocket has the rarest; the other fields spread over the basins, crossroads, plateaus and craters, rarer the farther they lie along the passes. Nests (about 0.9 per area, scaled by the difficulty) lie at least 80 m from the landing site along the passes (a bit closer through the rock), two in every nest hollow first, then spread over the other areas, the ones every way out goes through last. Ore fields and nests only appear where players can walk to from the ship.
  - **Biomes**, on a gradient from the cold outside to the scorched inside (each planet has 2–3): *Frost fields* (outer planets and moons), *Dust flats* (outer and middle), *Overgrowth* (middle), *Salt pans* (middle and moons), *Ash fields* (volcanic, inner), *Glass plains* (ground vitrified by the star's flares, innermost). *Dust flats* (sandy with a cracked crust, high plateaus with layered sandstone cliffs, boulders, rock outcrops, rubble, small stones, thorny spire plants, dry shrubs, pebbles and bones) and *Overgrowth* (dark soil under olive moss, gentler hills, fleshy tube plants, mushrooms, puffball fungi, fronds, a few boulders and stones) have their own look. The other four are greybox so far, with their own layout and ground values, the existing ground textures tinted and the existing rocks: *Frost fields* (crevasses and snow bridges, see above) only rocks on gentle ground, *Salt pans* (sinkholes and causeways, see above) wide flat areas, pebbles and bones, *Ash fields* (lava channels, ridges and vents, see above) dark, rough ground, *Glass plains* (terraces, fences and shard gardens, see above) flat and sparse, with glass slivers (greybox prisms). The Overgrowth's growth walls, mires and groves: see above.
  - Generating takes about 1–1.5 s on a worker thread, started when the mission is picked at the holo table; putting the world under the ship costs a short hitch (about 0.2 s) in the clouds during the descent, and its navigation mesh bakes in the background (see [Spaceship](#5-spaceship-hub), Ship states).
  - The tests use the small hand-built test location (`game/locations/test_location.tscn`) instead: fixed spots, quick to set up. The location viewer (`game/debug/location_viewer.tscn`, see [`TECHNICAL.md`](TECHNICAL.md#engine-settings)) draws generated layouts from above, with the barrier, the hazard and the map parts. The seed sweeps check every biome: every area, pass and bridge can be walked, nobody gets stuck, nothing climbs the rock, no walkable spot lies next to a gap, the hazard keeps off the lanes, the landing yard and the sites, each biome's own kinds of areas turn up, a sinkhole field's pits are gaps and a lava island has lava all around but for its crossing; the Salt pans' causeways rise above the pans, the Ash fields' rock forms ridges, the Dust flats' slot canyons are deep, their buttes rise and their washes' beds lie low, and the Glass plains' areas lie on their levels, a step apart at most, with the stairs' top two steps up. A built Overgrowth location's growth walls stop shots, its mud pools lie level and slow, its grove stalks grow only in the groves.
- **Planets, moons and regions:** each planet and moon has fixed **regions** (like countries), each with its biome and its handcrafted set pieces (landmarks, quest sites, the lost expedition's sites). A visit generates a new section of the region every time (a new seed), with the region's set pieces placed in it. So story missions use the same generated worlds with handcrafted pieces placed in them.
  - **Six planets** from the outside in, one per signature ore, with 3–4 regions each and at least one hard one; the first planet's three regions go from easy to hard for getting started. The fourth is a **gas giant** with no ground to land on: its two moons share its signature ore, so that step of the campaign is a pair of moons instead of a globe. About 25 regions in all (with about 120 runs in a 40 h campaign, about 5 visits each).
  - **Moons** are small (1–2 regions, shorter timers) and have a biome their planet doesn't, so the ores of other biomes are found early; they have no signature ore of their own (except the gas giant's).
  - **Tiers:** each body has a tier (1 for the outermost planet, one more per planet inwards; a moon has its planet's). It shows on the navigator's card; a region's danger (easy, normal, hard) works within it. The heat shield needs that MK level to reach the body, and the body's highest MK level is its tier + 2 (`CelestialBody.max_mk`, see Upgrade stations in [Progression](#12-progression--upgrades)).
    - **Farther in is harder** (`game/mission/mission_settings.tres`, Tiers, and `voyage_settings.tres` for the nests; all **TBD (playtest)**): per tier above 1, aliens have +30 % health and do +20 % damage, and a location has +10 % nests. That keeps pace with one damage and one armor MK level per planet (+25 % each), so each new planet feels a bit harder until the players have upgraded. Each tier also brings two new strains (built up to tier 2, see [Combat](#10-combat--aliens)).
    - **The star's influence** (the star is getting unstable, see [Story](#story-and-setting-draft)): per tier above 1, the storm's warning starts 10 s earlier (60 s on Torvane, 110 s on Zairen) and its damage grows 25 % faster (2 → 4.5 HP/s per second); the starting damage and the timer stay. With more armor inwards, the time to get aboard stays about the same, but the warning gets longer and the last seconds harsher. No other hazards from the star; the biomes have their own (see What makes a biome above).
  - **Opening planets:** the next planet inwards opens with the ship's **heat shield** at the drive core in the engine room, one MK level per planet, paid mostly with the current planet's signature ore, so better ores open the way. MK1 is the bare core (Torvane and Lisk); MK2 opens Vehra, MK3 Cassor and Mirr, MK4 Gaulm's moons Orrel and Iska, MK5 Skelde, MK6 Zairen: a body is open when its tier is at most the shield's MK level (moons open with their planet), and every region of a reached body is open. The story wraps it later but never adds a second gate.
    - **Costs** (all **TBD (playtest)**), ores only, never alloys: MK2 300 Meridine and 400 Vyrel, MK3 300 Emerin and 400 Halcyne, MK4 300 Indral and 400 Ressa, MK5 300 Selyx and 400 Duskine, MK6 300 Noctile and 500 Duskine (about 3–4 good runs of the signature ore, less than a full silo).
    - **Paid from the hauls:** the host makes the next plate the ship's project; from then on every run that comes back pays into it first, with exactly the ores it still needs, before the rest goes into every player's hold (see [Co-op progression](#co-op-progression)). Example: the plate needs 300 Meridine; a run brings home 200, the plate takes all 200; the next run brings 150, the plate takes the last 100 and the hold gets 50. Ore the plate took still counts as found. The host can also pay what's missing from their own hold any time, as far as it has it. The plate is installed as soon as it's paid (at once, not timed, so opening a planet never waits), the project ends with it, and the next planet's regions offer missions right away. Only the host works on it (the ship and the open bodies are the host's save); a guest sees the card with a hint.
    - **The navigator** shows bodies out of reach dimmed, with "?" for their signature ore; their card, and their regions', say what they need ("Needs heat shield MK2 (engine room)", with the plate's ores as badges).
    - **Each plate also raises the highest MK level** of the upgrades by one: MK3 with the bare core (Torvane), MK4 with Vehra, up to MK8 with Zairen, so the upgrades grow with the campaign instead of all being bought on the first planet (see Upgrade stations in [Progression](#12-progression--upgrades)).
  - **Flights between bodies** cost nothing (no fuel).
- **Stations and outposts** are fully handcrafted maps with NPCs and quests, and no enemies (see [Spaceship](#5-spaceship-hub), Stations and outposts).

### Quests

- NPCs hand out quests: the main (story) quest and several side quests can be active at once, e.g. destroy a nest, recover something, extract an amount of one ore, find an expedition log.
- A quest is tied to a planet, location or kind of mission, so it can't be done in just any run.
- **Quests can't be missed:** a run that fails one or skips it leaves it open, and it's available again in a later run.
- Optional bonus goals pay off for staying longer (see below).
- Rewards: ores, weapon blueprints, and reputation with the NPC.
- **Planned in detail** (quests as data, eight kinds of goals, blueprint fragments in crates, requests, the main story's acts, the quest board on the bridge, co-op): see [`planned/quests.md`](planned/quests.md).
- **Teaching the mechanics:** crew NPCs explain mechanics when they become relevant, in their conversations and first quests, not in tutorial pop-ups (no 2D HUD). For example, whoever runs the armory explains that the suit makes ammo from carried ore, that the spare magazines come from the ship's stock and aren't rebuilt during a run (so ore is only spent on rounds that are fired), what the dark cells on the backpack mean, and the timing reload.

### Reasons to move and to stay (risk vs. reward)

Standing at one spot until the timer runs low must not pay off.

- **Nodes run dry:** each holds a limited amount, a few miner fills, so the players have to move on (see [Miners](#7-miners)).
- **A mining site draws aliens:** each working miner raises its nearest nests' raider rate (planned, see [`planned/aliens.md`](planned/aliens.md)).
- **Richer and rarer ore** lies farther from the ship; planned: rich fields between the nests of every nest hollow (see [`planned/risk-reward.md`](planned/risk-reward.md)).
- **A late deposit** (planned): a meteor bringing the region's rarest ore late in the run, far out and guarded (see [`planned/risk-reward.md`](planned/risk-reward.md)).
- **Quest goals** elsewhere on the map, and optional bonus goals.
- **No carry limit:** the risk is dying with everything on the back (see [Hose transfer](#8-hose-transfer)). Planned: a cargo hatch at the ramp to bank ore without ending the mission (see [`planned/risk-reward.md`](planned/risk-reward.md)).
- Not planned: the storm closing in from the edges before the timer runs out.
- **Ideas for later** (from the first playtest of the passes, where finding the way back in time became part of the run):
  - More things to place from the miners' room besides miners and the sentries: e.g. beacons that help find the way back to the ship.
  - **Barriers** in a location (e.g. a rockfall or a wall of alien growth closing a pass) that only an unlocked tool or upgrade removes, so the far, richer parts of some regions (their signature ore) open up with progress. Never the only way to an ore a quest needs before that unlock.

### Timer and return

- Greybox: a mission has a 15 min timer (finding ore along the passes takes a while) (`game/mission/mission_settings.tres`), **TBD (playtest)**.
- The mission is **over when every player is back aboard**, in time or after the timer ran out. Dead players don't count: once everyone alive is aboard and at least one player made it, the mission ends (see [Death & persistence](#11-death--persistence)).
- The time shows in the world: a countdown hangs above the door (running outwards from it, over the ramp, so it doesn't cover the silos' displays), in a status color that is green, turns amber after half the time, red in the warning time (the last minute on a tier 1 body, longer farther in, see Tiers in [Missions](#6-missions); **TBD (playtest)**) and stays red once the storm hits.
- Outside, when the door is farther away (greybox: 12 m, **TBD (playtest)**), an arrow on the suit ring's edge points to it in the status color (blinking in the last minute), with the countdown beyond its tip, so the way back, the urgency and the exact time are visible while the ship is off-screen.

### Timer expiry

- When the timer runs out, the storm becomes lethal: everyone outside the ship takes damage that grows every second. Greybox: 2 HP/s, +2 HP/s per second on a tier 1 body (faster farther in, see Tiers in [Missions](#6-missions)), so an unhurt player (100 HP) lasts about 9 s (`game/mission/mission_settings.tres`, **TBD (playtest)**). Aboard is safe. There's no grace period beyond the rising damage: the countdown and the warning say enough.
- The storm shows in the world: in the warning time the light dims and a reddish-brown haze builds up in the distance; when the timer runs out it gets thick within a few seconds. Inside the ship the haze fades out (it's an inner room), only the dim light outside still shows the storm. It stays while the players are aboard and clears for the next run.

## 7. Miners

- Placed by the player on ore / mineral / resource nodes.
- One miner per node. The player places it on the nearest free node in range (`place_miner`); a ring on the ground marks that node.
- **Placing and picking up take time** (`MinerHandling` on the player, greybox 3 s and 5 s in `MinerSettings`, **TBD (playtest)**), so collecting the miners at the end of a run is a decision against the clock. Placing takes the node at once and projects the miner onto it as a hologram (cyan, see through, scan lines drifting up, like a miner being built in the miners' bay) that turns into the real miner from the bottom up, a bright line at the edge, and lands with the thud; picking up is the reverse, the real miner turning back into the hologram from the top down until it's gone, ending with a latch. Its number plate, fill and hose show only once it's real. The player moves, dashes and fights freely meanwhile: it only goes on while they stay within the miner's hose range (the dashed circle of its ring); out of it, it waits (the hologram dims, the ring's progress band and circle blink) and goes on when they come back. One at a time: while one goes on, nothing else is placed or picked up; placing or picking up another while it waits gives it up (nothing lost: a miner being placed goes back to the fleet, one being picked up turns real again where it stands). Taking damage doesn't cancel it, dying does. Meanwhile it doesn't mine, its hose stays off and nobody else can pick it up.
- Fill slowly over time; the player needs to place several for a good haul. Target: an empty miner takes about 2–5 min to fill (so a few fills per mission); exact values **TBD (playtest)**.
- **Stop when full.** Capacity and speed can be upgraded (see [Upgrade stations](#upgrade-stations)) / better miner types.
- **Nodes run dry** (values **TBD (playtest)**): each node holds a limited amount (`OreNode.FILLS`), counted in fills of a standard-issue miner's tank (180 units): common nodes 2 fills, mid 1.5, signature 1 (rarer ore, smaller deposits). Its miner takes what it mines out of the node, so a bigger tank empties a node in fewer trips but never makes it bigger. The node shows it: its crystals shrink into the rock one after another as it empties, the smallest first (the loose chips around the pad go at once), each holding its share by its volume, so a node being mined visibly wears down; a dry node is only its rock. A miner on a node that runs dry stops like a full one (with the same chime), but its lamp blinks amber instead of glowing steady: "move me". A dry node takes no miner (no placing ring) and the ore scanner skips it; the navigator's hologram still shows every node (a preview before landing).
- Fill level is shown on the miner itself: a glass column fills up in the ore color, and a lamp on top lights up when it's full. Aliens attack it from its surface: their reach counts from the edge of its body, not its center.
- **Its ring** (`MinerRing`, values **TBD (playtest)**): every miner projects a ring on the ground, always shown, like a player's suit ring: the health arc on the screen's left (green, amber below half, red below a quarter, empty with a blinking red outline while broken) and the tank's fill arc on its right in the ore color (white when full), both filling from the bottom up just outside its legs (0.25 m beyond its body), the top left open where the miner stands; a thin dashed circle at its hose range (5 m, more with the hose upgrade), solid while the hose is connected; while it's placed or picked up, a thin band inside the arcs going around clockwise from the top with the progress in the hologram's color.
- Art pass: the miner model gets a small display showing the fill counter (amount / capacity) and a timer (time until full).
- **Attacked by aliens** (raiders, see [Combat & aliens](#10-combat--aliens)): a miner has health (greybox 100); the frame flashes white when hit and turns rusty with damage. At zero it **breaks down**: it stops mining and the hose disconnects, so its ore can't be taken out until it's repaired, but it keeps the ore. The frame blinks red while broken.
- **Repair:** a player standing next to a miner (greybox 2.5 m) repairs it automatically, 25 health/s; a broken miner works again only once it's repaired completely (4 s from broken). Damaged miners that still work get repaired too. Values **TBD (playtest)**.
- Defense options like shields and sentry turrets are possible later.
- **Crafted miners** (values **TBD (playtest)**): a player's miners are built in the ship and kept (`MinerFleet` on the player, saved with them), up to six, each in its cradle in the miners' room with its number ("M-01" to "M-06"; the number belongs to the cradle) and its own parts and MK levels: drill, ore tank, hose and **plating** (health +25 % per level, 100 → 275 HP at MK8). Every player starts with M-01. On the planet each crafted miner wears its number plate on a side box above a strip in its owner's player color. Damage only counts during a run: a crafted miner comes back aboard at full health, free; one picked up and placed again in the same run keeps its damage.
- **Miners per run:** each player takes six miners into a run: their crafted ones that go along, **standard-issue** ones filling up the rest, so nobody is ever without miners. Placing takes the miner still to place that mines that node's ore fastest (crafted ones first by number on a tie; a standard-issue one is never faster), so the best drill goes onto the hardest ore by itself, without a choosing step. Standard-issue miners are the ship's: free, refilled for every run, MK1 in everything, mining at 75 % of the fill rate, with a plain grey plate, never lost. Once a player's six are placed, `place_miner` places nothing more until one is picked up.
- **Picking up:** `interact` next to a miner (within 2.5 m, the repair range; an amber ring around its foot shows which one) picks it up (see the time it takes above): what's still in its tank goes onto the player's back, the miner back to its owner's count to place again. Any player can pick up any miner (a teammate rescues a dead player's miner; it stays its owner's), broken ones too.
- **Lost:** a crafted miner still standing on the planet when the run ends (everyone alive is aboard and the ramp goes up, or everyone died) is lost there, together with a level of its parts under way; its cradle empties and its card says where ("Lost on Torvane, Redscar Flats") until a new one is built there.
- **Rarer ores mine slower** (per ore, greybox: mid ores 85 %, signature ores 70 % of the fill rate, **TBD (playtest)**).
- **Harder ores need better drills,** so miners have their own progression (all **TBD (playtest)**): each ore has a hardness (`OreType.hardness`: common ores 0, mid ores 1, Meridine and Emerin 2, Indral and Selyx 3, Noctile and Aethrine 4), and a drill's MK level sets how hard an ore it mines at full speed, set by hand per level (`Upgrade.hardness` for MK1, `UpgradeLevel.hardness`): MK1–2 up to 1, MK3–4 up to 2, MK5–6 up to 3, MK7–8 up to 4. So each planet's signature ore mines at full speed with the level that planet's heat shield plate allows (Torvane MK3: Meridine, Cassor MK5: Indral, Skelde MK7: Noctile), once it's bought. A drill too soft still mines it at a quarter of the speed (`MinerSettings.soft_drill_share`, on top of the ore's own rate, the drill's level and the standard-issue share), so no ore is ever blocked, only slow: a standard-issue miner (MK1) on Meridine mines at about 13 % of the base rate, a crafted one with drill MK3 at about 105 %. The drill turns while it mines; on an ore too hard for it, it turns at that share and its loop plays lower and wavering, strained. No lamp or blink (the amber blink is for nodes running dry).

## 8. Hose transfer

- When the player is within a miner's radius, a hose automatically connects the miner to the player and transfers resources.
- Player can move and shoot while connected.
- Leaving the radius disconnects the hose and stops transfer; re-entering reconnects.
- Each miner has its own hose, so several miners in range transfer at once. In co-op a miner connects to the nearest player in range.
- The hose sags between the miner and the player's back, rests on the ground when it's long, and has the ore color.
- Radius and transfer speed are miner values (upgradable with the miner): greybox 5 m and 30 units/s, so a full miner (180 units) empties in 6 s. **TBD (playtest)**.
- **No carry limit:** everything is carried until returning to the ship; a soft limit (a full load slowing the player down) was dropped, since it would fight the "one more trip" decision. The risk is dying: everything carried is lost.
- Carried resources show on the player's back as one stack of ore poured into glass vessels, like layered sand: the rarest ore at the bottom, the commonest on top, each ore a band in its glow. The stack fills the backpack's two tubes first (left, then right), then canisters strapped on beside the backpack (two stacked per side, a third farther out; at most six, beyond that they all show full), so a big haul makes the silhouette bulkier, also for teammates. Ammo takes the commonest ore first, so it drains the top of the stack and rare ore stays safe at the bottom. Greybox: a tube and a canister each hold one full miner (180 units), **TBD (playtest)**. Exact amounts show aboard, at the silos once the ore is in the hold.

## 9. Resources

- **14 ore types** in three bands, with invented, mineral-like names (each starting with its own letter, so they read apart in lists; listed in `game/ores/all_ores.tres`, commonest first):
  - **Common** (found in several biomes, on many planets): Kyrite (cyan, long hexagonal prisms, steady), Olvane (green, chunky blocks, steady), Pallin (pale ice blue, frost needle sprays, slow pulse), Tessar (teal, stacked plates, wave).
  - **Mid** (fewer biomes, farther out): Vyrel (violet, blade shards from broken geodes, slow pulse), Duskine (deep blue, stretched double pyramids, sparkle), Halcyne (sky blue, basalt-like column bundles, flicker), Ressa (pink, round grape clusters, breathing).
  - **Signature** (one per planet, outermost to innermost; the rarest near the star): Meridine (magenta, twisted spires, heartbeat), Emerin (emerald, crossed double-pointed spindles, sparkle), Indral (indigo, faceted gem balls, fast pulse), Selyx (pale lavender, fiber tufts, wave), Noctile (dark body with mint veins, broken-open nodules, breathing), Aethrine (iridescent, a crown of gem prisms, shifting).
  - The number comes from the plan of about six planets (some with moons): four common and four mid ores for the biomes, a signature ore per planet. More planets bring more signature ores; the catalog, the hold, the cards and the tests work for any number.
- **Each ore is identifiable at a glance** by its color, its crystal shape and its glow pattern, so telling them apart never depends on color alone. The patterns: **steady**; **slow pulse** (about 0.4 Hz, between about 55% and full); **fast pulse** (about 2.5 Hz, shallower); **flicker** (irregular like a candle or a loose contact, with occasional dips); **wave** (bands of light rising up the crystals); **sparkle** (single facets glint at random times); **breathing** (slow in and out, about 0.25 Hz, with a brighter core); **heartbeat** (a double beat about once a second, then rest); **shifting** (the hue slowly turns, about once in 10 s, and changes with the viewing angle, iridescent). One ore has a **dark body** instead: near-black glossy crystals with glowing veins in its color, the veins in its glow pattern. Neighboring deposits don't glow in sync. Patterns stay calm and readable from the camera: nothing brightens more than 3 times a second (photosensitivity). Rates and depths are **TBD (playtest)**. The same glow shows on everything filled with the ore (the miner's column, the hose, the canisters) and on its badge on holo cards (its icon in its color, glowing in its pattern, with its name).
- **Spread over the planets:** biomes carry the common and mid ores, each ore in 2–3 biomes (*Frost fields*: Kyrite, Pallin, Halcyne; *Dust flats*: Kyrite, Pallin, Vyrel, Halcyne; *Overgrowth*: Olvane, Tessar, Duskine, Ressa; *Salt pans*: Olvane, Tessar, Vyrel, Ressa; *Ash fields*: Kyrite, Tessar, Duskine, Halcyne; *Glass plains*: Olvane, Pallin, Vyrel, Ressa; not every biome is on every planet), each planet adds its signature ore, so a region has 3–6 types (see [Missions](#6-missions)) and a single run about 3–5. Rarer ores lie farther from the ship, make more ammo and mine slower. Prepared for later: harder ores needing better drills (see [Miners](#7-miners)).
- **Refinery:** combines 2–3 ores into **alloys** in real time, so early ores stay useful; refining passes while playing, during runs and while the game is closed ("come back tomorrow") and never blocks the story. All values **TBD (playtest)**.
  - **8 alloys** in four tiers of two, each from a common ore as bulk, a mid ore and from tier 2 on the signature ore, every common and mid ore going into two of them and every signature ore into one (listed in `game/refinery/all_alloys.tres`, by tier). Names are invented like the ores', each with its own first letter; the signature alloys echo their signature ore. A bar takes 10 of the common ore, 5 of the mid ore and 3 of the signature ore, and 1, 3, 6 or 10 min by tier.

    |---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | 1 | **Cobrel** | Kyrite | Vyrel | – | copper orange, steady |
    | 1 | **Gantrite** | Olvane | Halcyne | – | pale gold, slow pulse |
    | 2 | **Merisk** | Pallin | Duskine | Meridine | rose red, shifting |

  - **Recognizable like the ores:** each alloy has its color and glow pattern (the ores own the cold hues), and its badge is an ingot (not a crystal) with 1–4 stamped ticks for its tier, so costs and lists show alloys the same way as ores.
  - **In the ship** (greybox until the cargo bay's art pass): in the cargo bay's front corner by the door to the lower deck, four furnaces stand in a row with the ingot rack beside them, doors and shelves facing the camera, with a way past the left furnace along the wall; the furnaces' flues join a manifold, and a pipe runs to it over the bay from each silo cluster's pump hub. The ship starts without a furnace; the quests unlock them one after another, up to four (see [`planned/quests.md`](planned/quests.md); until then the debug overlay does). A locked furnace is a cold, dark shell whose card only says "This furnace is cold."; an unlocked one has a lit lamp over its door.
  - **A furnace** refines one alloy at a time, in any amount the hold pays for, paid when started; two alloys at once need two furnaces. The object is the menu: `interact` at it opens it as a station; its card picks the recipe (`tab_prev`/`tab_next` or its arrows, by tier) and the number of bars (buttons −10, −5, −1 and +1, +5, +10 either side of the number, or `zoom_out`/`zoom_in` by one; from 1 up to what the hold pays for; starts at 1); the directions only move on to the next station, never change what's picked; shows the cost against the hold (red where it's short) and the time it takes, and "Start". The bars finish one by one on the wall clock and go into the hold as they finish (checked every second and on loading; a clock set back never takes a finished bar away, the next one just waits). While it runs, its door glows in the alloy's color, filling from the bottom with the batch, a light flickers in front of it, and the plate below names the alloy and the bars done; its card shows the bars done, how far the bar in the furnace is and the time left, and "Stop" (pressed twice: the finished bars stay, the ore of every unfinished one goes back to the hold). No adding to a running batch. Nothing starts or stops while a mission is underway, like buying.
  - **Recipes are discovered:** a recipe shows once every ore in it has been in the hold (like the silos' "?"); until then the card shows "Unknown alloy" with the ores of it found so far and a "?", so nothing still to find is given away.
  - **The ingot rack** is a station like the silos: a shelf per alloy, a row per tier from the top, the bars stacking up on it in the alloy's color (up to 24 shown) with a plate naming it and the exact count; unknown alloys' shelves are dark with "?". `tab_prev`/`tab_next` step through the shelves, the directions on to the furnaces and the silos; the card shows the alloy, its tier, how many bars there are, its recipe per bar and what it's used for ("Used for: Upgrades MK6, Kith": the miners' build, the upgrade levels and the weapons whose cost has it; nothing while none does, like Gantrite until the station's requests). The bars are kept in the player's hold like the ores.
  - **Each player's own:** the number of furnaces is the ship's (the host's save); the batches are each player's, refined from their own hold into their own, in their own save (see [Co-op progression](#co-op-progression)). The furnaces and the rack show the batches and bars of the player using them, else of the player the camera follows.
  - **What alloys are for:** building crafted miners (Cobrel, see [Miners](#7-miners)), part of every upgrade's cost from MK4 on (see [Upgrade stations](#upgrade-stations)) and building the six prototype weapons; the heat shield stays ores only, so opening planets never waits for a furnace. Until the quests unlock the furnaces, MK4 and up and the prototypes need the debug overlay's furnaces and "Fill rack".
  - Planned (see [`planned/refinery.md`](planned/refinery.md)): the station's requests, the engineer explaining the refinery, more or faster furnaces as skills.
- **Ores are the only currency:** everything is bought with ores and the alloys refined from them (the higher costs); there are no experience levels.

## 10. Combat & aliens

- Aliens outside the ship try to kill the player; player fights back with weapons.
- **Weapons:** a fixed set of unique weapons, ranged and melee, each with its own in-game name (untranslated, like the ores and places) and a plain class word under it on the cards (translated: "Harl · Assault rifle"); all 18 (see [`planned/weapons.md`](planned/weapons.md)). New ones come as blueprints from quests and the story and are built at the workbench (see [Upgrade stations](#upgrade-stations)). Ranged weapons use **ammo** made from carried ore (see [Ammo and reloading](#ammo-and-reloading)). The player can't carry all of them: before a mission they pick a **loadout** of a few weapons in the ship and play the mission with it.
- **Enemies:** only aliens for now, one species in several strains and roles: eleven hunting strains, a guard, a raider and the apex, the Ravager (more guards and raiders may come, see [`planned/aliens.md`](planned/aliens.md)). A **strain** is the body (how it moves, what it takes, its attacks), a **role** what it goes for: **guards** stay by their nest, **hunters** go for players, **raiders** for miners. Every strain plays one role, so the body tells the role: the warden guards, the gnawer raids, the swarmer, the mite, the spitter, the burrower, the brute, the shrieker, the bloater, the tender, the darter, the stalker and the skimmer hunt. A group of aliens that hatches together is a **pack** (hunters only). The Ravager isn't hatched by nests: it roams some locations on its own.
- **No drops:** aliens drop nothing; ore only comes from mining. Killing is about safety, not loot, so the miners stay central.
- **Attacking:** one attack button; the equipped weapon decides what it does (shoot, swing). Melee stays on it: a separate melee button would make the melee weapons pointless.
- **Loadout slots take any weapon type**, so a melee-only loadout is a real choice. Melee is never required to get on in a run (nothing that only a melee weapon can do).
- **Starting weapons:** a new player owns Harl (ranged) and Dray (melee), and both are in the loadout. The others come as blueprints through the game (from the quests, step 16; until then the debug overlay's "Give blueprints"); the first one to find is Brask; Pinner, Grindle, Quill, Tremor, Wick, Scald, Kith, Zenith, Fulgen, Nadir, Riven, Auger, Jag, Clout and Mantle are blueprints too. Owned weapons and the loadout belong to each player (see [Co-op progression](#co-op-progression)).
- **Composed of Resources** (`game/weapons/`): a weapon (`WeaponType`, one folder per weapon in `game/weapons/types/`) is its **feed** (its ammo: magazines, single rounds, a battery or a fuel cell that heats up; none for melee weapons, but a shield's field charge, which isn't ammo), its **trigger** (how holding the attack button attacks: at its rate while held, one attack per press, barrels or a blade spinning up first, a charge let go, or a press to thrust and holding on to drill), its **delivery** (how an attack reaches its targets: projectiles, which can pierce, a shell lobbed in an arc that blasts where it lands, a charge tossed onto the ground, a cone of flame, a beam, lightning jumping from alien to alien, an orb opening a gravity well, a swing (also a chain of slashes ending in a heavy one, a saw's cuts and a shield's bash), a thrust through a line that can drill into what it hit, or a punch at the nearest alien), its **effects** (what a hit does: damage with stagger and push, scaled by a partial charge, setting aliens burning, slowing them, throwing them, healing teammates and repairing miners), a shield's **guard** (its field), its upgrade **parts**, its origin (colony arms, converted mining tools, expedition prototypes), its draw time and a greybox scene, so most new weapons are data. `all_weapons.tres` lists every weapon; the tests go over all of them.
- **First weapons** (values **TBD (playtest)**): **Harl** (assault rifle, colony) fires fast single shots (8 a second) that reach far; **Brask** (shotgun, colony) fires a fan of pellets (7 over 30°) that only reaches 12 m; **Dray** (hammer, converted tool) swings at everything within 2.5 m in a 120° arc in front of the player (also several aliens at once), unless something solid is in the way, about 1.5 times a second; it comes down as a two-handed overhead blow (from over the right shoulder, slammed down in front, the face of its upright head leading).

- The hive escalates over a run (see the nests below), and aliens are stronger on bodies farther in (see Tiers in [Missions](#6-missions)); working miners draw raiders, and the end of a run brings the hive's frenzy (see the nests below).

### Ammo and reloading

Values in `game/weapons/*_settings.tres` and `game/player/player_settings.tres`, all **TBD (playtest)**.

- **Magazines:** each ranged weapon fed with ore holds a magazine (greybox: Harl 24 rounds, Brask 6 shells, Grindle a belt of 120, Quill 5, Tremor 4, Wick 6 charges, Fulgen 30 arcs; the heat weapons' fuel cells Scald 80, Kith 100, Zenith 60); a shot uses one round (a heat weapon's attack too, ten a second). Pinner and Nadir run on a battery instead (see Pinner and Nadir above). A magazine upgrade holds more (see [Upgrade stations](#upgrade-stations)). Melee weapons need no ammo, so they're the fallback when the ore runs out; nothing in a run needs ammo.
- **Spare magazines first:** each ranged weapon in the loadout starts a run with 2 spare magazines (`PlayerSettings.spare_magazines`; Brask: 2 loads of shells), so with the loaded one that's 3. Reloads take them first, for free; a Harl reload with a spare still throws away the rounds left in the old magazine. More spares are a skill.
- **Then made from carried ore:** once the spares are used, a reload pays ore from the player's cargo for each round it loads, so the carried ore is the reserve. Any ore type works, so it never depends on what a location has; the commonest one is used first (lowest tier: Kyrite, then Olvane, ...), so rare ore is spent last, and rarer ores make more ammo per unit (greybox: one Kyrite unit makes 4 Harl rounds or 1 Brask shell; Olvane and Tessar 1.25×, mid ores 1.5×, signature ores 2×). Aboard (also in the shooting range) reloads are free, from the ship's stock.
- **Full for every run:** each run starts with full magazines and spares, and so does respawning in the medical bay (health carries over, see [Death & persistence](#11-death--persistence)). Without ore, those three magazines are all there is until the miners deliver, so mining early or taking a melee weapon still matters.
- **Reloading** (`reload`): by hand at any time, and by itself when the magazine runs empty (also when pulling the trigger on an empty weapon once ore is carried again). Without ore to pay for it, nothing happens and the gauge blinks red. The player walks slower while reloading (greybox: 75 % speed).
  - **Harl, Grindle, Quill, Tremor, Wick, Fulgen, Scald, Kith, Zenith (magazine, a fuel cell):** a reload throws away the rounds left in the magazine and loads a full one (Harl 1.6 s, Grindle 3.5 s, Quill 2 s, Tremor 2.6 s, Wick 2.2 s, Fulgen, Scald and Kith 2 s, Zenith 2.4 s; a hot heat weapon vents instead, see Heat weapons), paying for all of it; with too little ore it loads what the ore pays, and it doesn't start when that would be no more than what's loaded. It can't fire meanwhile.
  - **Brask (single shells):** loads one shell after another (0.5 s each) until it's full or the ore runs out; the loaded shells stay. Pulling the trigger stops the reload and fires what's loaded.
  - **Switching weapons** cancels a reload: Harl's magazine stays empty (it was thrown away), Brask keeps the shells it loaded. The rounds of a holstered weapon stay as they are for the run.
- **Timing reload:** pressing `reload` again during a reload is a timing press. Within the window (Harl: 55–70 % of the reload; Brask: 40–90 % of each shell, so it opens late enough to react to the next shell after a hit) the reload (the shell) finishes right away; outside it, it takes longer (Harl +0.5 s, Brask +0.3 s per shell), and there's only one press per reload (shell). Not pressing is normal speed, so it's an optional skill, never required. Brask's shells timed in a row load quicker (5 % each, up to 10 %); a miss or a shell without a press breaks the chain. The window opening ticks (a sound cue, needed for VR and accessibility, see [Sound](#sound)), and a press sounds whether it hit.
- **Shown in the world:** on the weapon, Harl's six counter segments each light up for a sixth of the magazine (Pinner's six cell segments, Grindle's ten on its drum and Fulgen's ten cells likewise), and Brask's strip has a glowing shell per round (Quill's, Tremor's and Wick's cells too, and a cell per well on Nadir; the heat weapons' eight cells each a share of the fuel); the last segment (shell) blinks amber when it's nearly empty, all of them blink red when it's empty and nothing pays a reload. During a reload the counter fills up like a bar with the window's segment white (red after a miss); the shell being loaded brightens, white within the window. Since the weapon is small at camera distance, the suit ring's ammo arc shows the same (see [UI](#ui-in-the-world-not-on-the-screen)), and it flashes red when a reload can't start (not with a full magazine: then `reload` just does nothing).
- **Handing a teammate ammo** (co-op support role): holding `interact` next to a teammate (within 2 m, for 0.8 s, with no terminal or button in reach) gives them a magazine for their equipped weapon, paid from the giver's ore, by the same rules as a reload (a magazine replaces theirs, shells top it up). The suit ring's outer band fills green meanwhile. Works with local players now; over the network with co-op (M7).
- **Spares shown on the ring:** the equipped weapon's spare magazines are ticks inside the suit ring's ammo arc, in the weapon's ammo color; a partly used one (Brask's shells) is dimmer, used ones are only an outline. They show on a reload and when a spare is used, then fade.
- **Skills** (see [Progression](#12-progression--upgrades)): more spare magazines, keeping the rounds left in a magazine when reloading, reloading at full walking speed (relevant once aliens come in faster and slower variants), planned: the suit rebuilding spare magazines from carried ore during a run (see [`planned/skills.md`](planned/skills.md)). Maybe a reload speed or a wider window as an upgrade part, if playtests ask for it.

When it starts an attack or takes a hit it shows fully for 3 s. Killed, it drops to the ground.
- **Ravager** (`game/aliens/strains/ravager/`, the apex): a huge armored monster on six pillar legs with a crest of horns and brood pods on its back, 1000 HP (scaled by tier like the rest), 3 armor everywhere, almost impossible to stagger or push. Its steps shake the view of players within 40 m, the more the closer, and throw up dust, so it's felt before it's seen. It sheds three swarmers from its pods when a player is within 20 m (every 20 s, only under the cap), charges along a lane like the brute's (7–18 m, 45, a 2.2 m wide lane, dazed for 2.5 s when it runs into something) and slams close up (it rears up for 1 s, then 40 within 3 m of the spot, shaking the ground hard). - **Buffed aliens show it:** while a scream boosts an alien or a tender's link holds it, it's 15 % bigger and outlined in the hive's yellow-green, so a buffed pack stands out from afar.
- **Telegraphs:** every heavy attack has a wind-up readable from the isometric camera (the body crouches or rears up, the eyes and glands brighten, 0.4–0.8 s) and a sound of its own (see [Sound](#sound)). No hit-stop (co-op and VR don't take it well).
- **Hits:** each hit (a projectile, a swing) flashes the alien white, throws glowing ichor in the hive's yellow-green from its flesh or grey sparks and chips off armor (readable without numbers), pushes it back a little (Harl 0.5, a Brask pellet 0.8, Dray 6 m/s, fading within a fraction of a second; heavy strains less) and jolts its body.
  - **Armor:** a flat reduction per hit, but at least 25 % of the damage always gets through, so no alien is immune; many small hits suffer, heavy hits don't. A strain can be armored only on its front (the warden, the brute). A tender's link adds armor on every side. Burning ground (lava, vents) ignores armor. The swarmer has none.
  - **Stagger:** each hit fills a stagger meter by weapon (Harl 7, a Brask pellet 4, Dray 40); past the strain's threshold (swarmer 25) it's staggered for 0.5 s: it rears up, stops and its attack is broken off (also a pounce's wind-up). A stagger empties the meter, hits while staggered don't fill it, and the meter drains within a second. So a swarmer staggers after about six quick Harl hits, one close Brask blast or one Dray swing.
- **Limit:** at most 60 aliens live at once (40 in VR), small strains counting as a share (a mite a quarter, a darter half; the Ravager doesn't count); nests wait while it's reached. Guards take at most half of it, so on big maps with many nests the hunters and raiders still come (nests whose guards don't fit stand unguarded). The debug overlay's hatch shortcuts ignore it.
- **Greybox nests** (`game/aliens/nest/alien_nest.tscn`, values in `alien_nest_settings.tres`, all **TBD (playtest)**): dark crimson mounds on a slimy patch with a glowing mouth. When the outside world appears, a warden hatches beside each nest and guards it. Once the mission starts, each nest also sends out **hunters** in packs: aliens that go for the nearest player outside however far away and don't give up because of distance (only by the player going aboard or dying). The hive escalates with the mission's progress, from the start until the storm hits: a nest hatches a pack every 60 s at first and every 20 s at the end, while fewer of its hunters are out than 1 at first and 5 at the end (counted by their share, so one pack may go over it); once they're down below that, the next pack comes a full interval later. A pack hatches one alien after another, a quarter of a second apart, and aliens waiting to hatch count towards the cap. The glow in the mouth pulses faster as the hive escalates.
- **The frenzy:** from the storm's warning on (and during the storm), the hive frenzies: its aliens are 15 % faster and their eyes glow brighter, nests send hunters at their end values (a pack every 20 s, up to 5 out) and their mouths pulse at the fastest rate. It pushes the way back without new rules.
- **Destroying a nest** (values in `alien_nest_settings.tres`, all **TBD (playtest)**): every weapon hurts a nest, ranged and melee (a swing reaches it at its edge), so a melee-only loadout clears nests too. It has 600 health on a tier 1 body (more farther in, like the aliens, and +50 % per player beyond the first) and armor like the aliens' (1 off every hit, at least a quarter gets through), so heavy hits are better, never required. A hit calls its guards: they go for the attacker from any distance while that player is within their leash. **It fights back:** at two thirds and one third of its health its mouth swells and glows white for 1 s, then it bursts, spewing 3 hunters of its packs' strains one after another (under the cap); a hit taking it past both at once still brings both bursts, one after the other. **Its state, without a health bar:** after each burst glowing cracks open in its plates and its glow flickers lower. **Destroyed**, it collapses inwards, the glow goes dark and the slime dries grey over a few seconds; the dark husk stays as a landmark for the run, but hatches nothing more: its hunters and raiders stop coming (the aliens it already hatched stay), and every other nest sends its next pack right away, a short surge. With every nest gone, nothing hatches any more, only what's still out there; the storm still ends the run. Nothing drops: it pays off in safety, fewer hunters and raiders, and later quest progress (the "clear nests" goal). Every visit generates a new section, so the nests are back. The damage readout shows numbers on it like on aliens. Nests stay something to find: the location hologram doesn't show them and they have no silhouette behind cliffs, alive or destroyed.
- **A scream's answer:** when a shrieker screams, the nest nearest to it sends its next pack right away, boosted like the shrieker's (at most every 15 s per nest, and only under the cap); its mouth flares and a ring sweeps out from it, so the answer can be seen.
- **Packs and what hatches where** (`game/aliens/hive/hive.tres`, all **TBD (playtest)**): hunters come in packs from a list of recipes, e.g. two swarmers, eight mites, a swarmer with six mites, two swarmers and a spitter, a burrower with six mites, a swarmer, a burrower and a spitter; each nest picks one by weight among those whose strains all hatch there. Which strains hatch depends on the body's tier: the first planet's from the start, older tiers' from the start, the body's own new strains from halfway through a run (so they appear late in a run on the first planet that brings them, from the start on the next one), and hard regions add one strain of the next tier from three quarters of the run on (the location keeps the same one), so the next planet isn't a wall. Each biome favors some strains (burrowers in the Dust flats and Salt pans, darters in the Ash fields and Glass plains, skimmers in the Glass plains, stalkers in the Overgrowth): packs and raiders with them come twice as often, never only them, so no biome needs a particular weapon. Tier 5 brings packs of five darters, three darters with two swarmers, two stalkers, a stalker with six mites, four darters with a shrieker, a stalker with a tender and two swarmers, a brute with four darters, and two stalkers with a bloater; tier 6 brings the skimmer (two, three or four of them, two with three darters, one with a brute and two swarmers, two with a stalker) besides all the others mixed, and the Ravager more often.
- **Raiders:** nests also send out raiders (gnawers), one by one. A raider goes for the nearest working miner however far away, attacking it until it breaks down and then moving on to the next one. Once no miner works, it goes back and guards its nest. They only hatch while at least one miner works, the first one a full interval after that, and escalate like hunters with their own values: one every 60 s → 25 s, at most 1 → 2 per nest at once (**TBD (playtest)**). Defending miners means shooting raiders before they get there, or repairing behind them. **Miners draw them:** each working miner makes its nearest nest send raiders 10 % more often (20 % on a signature ore), so a big mining site pays off but gets busy.
- **Pathfinding:** aliens walk around rocks and nests along a navigation mesh that the outside world bakes when it appears (miners placed later aren't part of it, aliens steer around them instead), and steer around each other, so swarms spread around their target instead of stacking.

### Alien look and behavior (direction for the art pass)

- **A hive of bio-organic beasts**, not soldiers or machines: insect- and reptile-like creatures with chitin plates over sinewy flesh, claws, mandibles and teeth, several legs, no tech, no clothes. They are one species in many strains, all sharing the same body language and colors, so any alien reads as "one of them" at a glance.
- **Swarms:** most aliens are small and come in numbers; they scuttle low over the ground, fast and erratic. Danger comes from many at once more than from a single one.
- **Strains:** the planned roster is in [`planned/aliens.md`](planned/aliens.md). Looks from the concept rounds:
  - **Swarmer:** a lean, dog-sized runner on four clawed legs with a long toothed jaw and scythe-like bone blades on its back. Modeled close to the concept (see Art direction above).
  - **Brute:** a four-legged rhino-beetle with heavy layered armor plates and horns. The plates are chitin and must not read as riveted metal. Modeled: four layered plates over the front ending in a ragged edge, a long upward nose horn under a forward thoracic horn, a sinewy back with four glowing slits.
  - **Shrieker, bloater, tender:** modeled too: the shrieker a lean four-legged runner with a long ridged neck and a glowing throat sac that swells as it screams; the bloater a fat grub on short stubby legs (nothing spider-like, for players with arachnophobia) whose back is a veined bladder with glowing veins, popping to a husk when it bursts; the tender a low six-legged body with gland clusters on its back and three tendrils ending in glowing bulbs.
  - **Darter, stalker, skimmer, Ravager:** modeled too: the darter a slender wasp-like flier with two pairs of see-through wings, glowing bands across its abdomen and a glowing sting; the stalker a tall, hunched biped on digitigrade legs with two huge scything talons raised over its shoulders, smaller grasping claws, feeding tendrils around its mouth and darker mottled chitin with thin glowing seams; the skimmer a broad, plated ray with thick fleshy wings, a row of glowing spots along each wing's trailing edge, a long ringed whip tail with a glowing barb and a big glowing throat sac; the Ravager a walking fortress about 5 m long on six thick pillar legs, armored all over, with a crest of horns and glowing brood pods on its back.
  - **Spitter:** a beetle body with a swollen, glowing acid sac on its back; the acid glows in flight. Modeled close to the concept, like the mite and the burrower (see Art direction above).
- **Nests:** they live in organic nests / hives grown into the ground, from which they spawn. The ground around a nest can be covered by a living, slimy layer, which also marks their territory. Look: a low, wide mound of chitin and sinew with a glowing opening on top and tendrils spreading over the slime.
- **Escalation and attraction:** the longer a mission runs and the more the players mine, the more aliens come and the stronger the strains get; miners draw them in (see Combat above).
- **Readability:** silhouettes with clear head, legs and claws, glowing eyes or glands that show where they face, colors that don't clash with the ore colors and stand out on the ground: every strain glows in the swarmer's yellow-green (acid included), which no ore uses; stalkers have darker chitin. Death should read clearly too (collapse, splatter).

## 11. Death & persistence

- Health shows on the suit ring's left arc (see [UI](#ui-in-the-world-not-on-the-screen)) and, for teammates, on a light band around the collar: green, amber below half, red and blinking below a quarter, flashing white on damage. Greybox: 100 HP, **TBD (playtest)**.
- On death: the body drops where it stands, **everything carried this run is lost**, and the player respawns in the medical bay with full health.
- When every player is dead, the mission is lost: the countdown turns grey and says LOST, and after a few seconds (greybox: 3 s, **TBD (playtest)**) everyone respawns aboard and the ramp goes up. The next run starts with a newly picked mission.
- Co-op: the mission ends when every player is either back aboard or dead, as long as at least one made it back. The dead lose what they carried and respawn aboard when it ends (planned with co-op: going down first and being revived by a teammate standing close, and respawning after 30 s instead of waiting, see [`planned/coop-revive.md`](planned/coop-revive.md)).
- When every player dies, the miners on the planet are lost too (see [Miners](#7-miners)).
- **Not too punishing:** losses should hurt, but not set players back for long.
- Planned skills soften it (see [`planned/skills.md`](planned/skills.md)): the **rescue drone** revives a player where they fell once per run, in solo and in co-op, so playing alone isn't unfair without anyone to revive you; the **hardened canister** keeps part of the carried ore on death.
- Back aboard (before the storm gets you): carried resources go into the hold and are spent at the upgrade stations. Every player has their own hold, in their own save (also when they play on someone else's ship), and every run's whole haul goes into each player's hold (see [Co-op progression](#co-op-progression)). The hold keeps growing over runs. It shows in the cargo bay as silo clusters (greybox until the cargo bay's art pass): an industrial silo per ore of the game in a fixed spot, ordered by rarity, with a window strip where the ore glows up to its fill level (full at 3000, 1500 and 600 units for common, mid and signature ores, **TBD (playtest)**) and above it, at the top of the silo's body, a display with the exact amount in big digits in the ore's color (the ore's name small below), readable from the normal camera; it sits high enough to show over the silo in front of it, and amounts of more than four digits get smaller to fit. Each band is a cluster around a pump hub with pipes to the silos' tops: the common and mid clusters (2 × 2 big silos each, about 2 m and 1.6 m wide, 5 m and 4 m tall) behind the main lane, the commons by the ramp; the signature cluster (2 × 3 smaller silos) in front of the lane, set back from the lane and the ramp so the way in from the ramp stays open. A cluster's far row is shifted half a step sideways, so from the isometric camera no silo hides another's window. The main lane (at least 4 m), the way between the clusters, the way to the lift, the ramp button and the floor signs (moved to fit) stay clear; players can't walk through the silos. Rarer ores get smaller silos. An ore never brought aboard shows as a dark silo with a grey "?" and no name (it stays known once found, even when spent), so the hold doesn't give away what's still to find. The silos are used directly: `interact` next to one opens the hold as a station with it selected; the camera follows the selected silo, and the card shows the ore's name, its band, how much the hold has, the open regions where it's found (in more than four, the planets and moons instead, so the card keeps its size with every planet open) and, for an ore harder than a first drill mines at full speed, the drill level that does ("Full speed with drill MK3."). `tab_prev`/`tab_next` step through the silos in reading order (rows from the back, each from left to right) the directions to the nearest silo, furnace or rack shelf on screen. The ramp's countdown hides meanwhile, since it hangs over the silos by the ramp. The silos show the hold of the player using them, else the one of the player the camera follows (each player sees their own). The refinery stands in the cargo bay's front corner by the door to the lower deck (see [Resources](#9-resources)); the cargo bay's floor sign moved in front of it.
- **Health carries over** between runs (and is saved with the player): a player keeps what they came back with. The four **med beds** in the medical bay heal, for free: `interact` at a bed (its foot or its sides) lies the player down on it (the weapon put away), and health fills up at a rate that heals from nothing to full in 3 s (**TBD (playtest)**); its card shows the health filling up ("Healing: 62 / 100", a bar, then "Full health") and "Get up". Any player can use any bed, one player per bed. The first time the player stands at the ramp button hurt (with a mission picked), a bubble above it says "Not at full health: the medical bay is at the nose." (once per player, saved with the player: in co-op every player sees it once, whoever's ship it is). Dying still means respawning in the medical bay with full health. The medic (Ilse) explains it, standing between the beds.
- **Saving:** automatic, no save button. Progress (the player's hold, the heat shield and where the ship is, the owned weapons, the loadout, the upgrade levels) is saved whenever it changes aboard; what changes during a mission is saved once it's over, so quitting mid-mission loses that run like dying (the game starts aboard in space again). There is one save. **Reset progress** on the settings terminal wipes it: it asks three times, each time saying that all progress is lost for good, and each confirm button waits a moment (greybox: 1 s, **TBD (playtest)**) so quick repeated presses can't get through. Only while the player is alone: with others in the session the button is disabled with a hint, and everyone keeps their progress. The settings (language) and the statistics stay.
- **Statistics:** the game keeps statistics of what each player does on their own machine (kills per weapon and strain, damage dealt and taken with its causes, deaths, reloads and their timing presses, ore collected, hauled and lost, miners, unlocks, upgrades and skills, runs and their outcomes, time aboard and outside), over all sessions and as a log per session, for playtesting and tuning (see [`TECHNICAL.md`](TECHNICAL.md#statistics)). They're local files; nothing is sent anywhere. Planned: a live view for development, a statistics station aboard and achievements on top (see [`planned/stats.md`](planned/stats.md)).

## 12. Progression / upgrades

Bought in the ship with ores, the higher levels also with alloys from the refinery (no experience levels, no player classes):

- **Weapons:** each one upgraded on its own, part by part, in MK levels (MK1–MK8, one more with each opened planet); planned: damage, capacity and a part of its own per weapon (see [`planned/weapons.md`](planned/weapons.md)).
- **Armor:** the suit, in MK levels.
- **Skills:** bought with ore like the upgrades, each player their own. Each cluster is a small tree growing out from its center: a skill opens once the one before it is bought (no gates between clusters). Most are a single unlock, some have ranks (shown as the same pips as the MK levels); each rank takes real time like the upgrade levels (see below). Skills deeper in a tree cost rarer ores: the first one of a cluster commons only, the next ones a mid ore too, from the fourth on a signature ore (`Skill.depth`; amounts **TBD (playtest)**). Everything can be maxed out completely, so the choice is only what to get first, and there's no respec. A run's role comes from the loadout (e.g. a healer loadout), not from a class.
  - **The first skills** (values **TBD (playtest)**), in four clusters:
    - **Mobility:** **dash** (`dash`: Space, gamepad B; about 4 m in the direction the player moves, else where they aim; a 2 s cooldown, no invulnerability; the suit ring's thin inner ring fills up again during the cooldown), then **reload on the move** (reloading at full walking speed).
    - **Survival:** **reinforced suit** (3 ranks, +20 health each, before the armor's multiplier), then **regeneration** (health comes back at 5 HP/s once the player took no damage for 5 s; the collar band pulses slowly meanwhile; it doesn't replace the medical bay).
    - **Ammo:** **spare magazines** (3 ranks, one more spare magazine per ranged weapon each, one more tick on the suit ring), then **careful reload** (a magazine reload keeps the rounds left in it and only tops it up).
    - **Scanning:** **ore scanner** (a small arrow on the suit ring's edge in the ore's color points to the nearest ore node within 40 m that isn't dry, also one with a miner on it, like the arrow back to the ship), then **damage readout** (numbers on hit aliens and nests; Brask's pellets add up into one).
  - **Planned:** the rescue drone, the hardened canister, rebuilding spares and the miner radar next, then about 60 skills in six clusters (Mining and Support added) (see [`planned/skills.md`](planned/skills.md)).
- **Miners:** capacity, speed, drill types, defenses (shields, sentry turrets).
- **New planets:** better ores open the way to the next planet: the engine room's heat shield, paid with the current planet's signature ore (see Opening planets in [Missions](#6-missions)). Each opened planet also raises the highest MK level by one (see Upgrade stations below).

### Upgrade stations

- One station per upgrade room on the lower deck, each the object itself (see [UI](#ui-in-the-world-not-on-the-screen)):
  - **Weapons:** a rack on the back wall right of the door, behind the workbench, with every weapon of the game in rows by origin like the armory's weapon wall (the ones not owned yet greyed out, "Locked"), three weapons to a line (a longer row wraps onto the next line; more lines push the rack up, so it starts just above the bench); picking one lays it on the workbench (with the keyboard or a gamepad: up from the bench onto the rack, along its grid, and its card's "Lay on the bench", the card beside the rack so it hides no weapon; `tab_prev`/`tab_next` lay the first weapon of the previous or next row on the bench), where its parts are the upgrades. A weapon not owned yet is itself a part on the bench: with its blueprint, its card builds it once for its ore from the hold (a fixed cost per weapon, from the planet the weapon belongs to; greybox: Brask 240 Kyrite and 80 Vyrel, Pinner 120 Kyrite and 40 Meridine, Grindle 300 Kyrite and 120 Emerin, Quill 320 Kyrite and 140 Indral, Wick 260 Kyrite and 100 Emerin, Tremor 360 Kyrite and 160 Selyx, Kith 300 Kyrite and 180 Indral, Scald 380 Kyrite and 180 Noctile, Zenith 500 Kyrite and 300 Aethrine, Fulgen 400 Kyrite and 180 Selyx, Nadir 420 Kyrite and 200 Noctile, **TBD (playtest)**; the six prototypes also take bars of the alloy of their own signature ore: Clout 6 Verdan, Kith 8 Indite, Fulgen 10 Sylith, Mantle 10 Sylith, Nadir 12 Noxar, Zenith 15 Auralt, the colony arms and converted tools ores only), without it says the blueprint is missing. The camera follows the selected weapon on a big rack; with a weapon on the bench it moves halfway towards the weapon's place on the rack, but only to the right, so the card right of the bench stays on the screen.
  - **Armor:** a fitting frame the player steps into; the camera frames their own suit, its parts are the upgrades (so far the whole suit, later helmet, chest, legs), and the real helmet pips light up when bought.
  - **Miners:** the miners' bay: six cradles along the back wall, three on each side of the door (M-01 on the left), and the maintenance pad in front of them. A built miner stands small in its cradle; picking it (a click, the directions, or `tab_prev`/`tab_next` through the cradles) puts it on the pad (its cradle shows empty meanwhile), where its drill, ore tank, hose reel and plating (its side boxes) are the upgrades, bought into that miner's own levels (the drill's card also shows the badges of the hardest ores it mines at full speed, "Full speed up to:", and the ores its next level adds, "MK3 also:", of the ores found so far; with none of those found yet "MK5 mines harder ores too."), and its number plate on the floor in front of the pad is the miner itself: its card shows its number, its parts' MK pips (a level under way filling its pip), how many standard-issue miners fill up the next run, and "Leave aboard" / "Take along" (going along by default); while one of its levels is under way it warns that the level is lost with it. An empty cradle shows a faint outline and "Build": its card (floating below the row) shows what building a miner costs (greybox 300 Kyrite, 120 Vyrel and 20 Cobrel, the same for every cradle and for one built again) next to the hold and how long it takes (greybox 3 min, on the wall clock like an upgrade level; the outline fills from the bottom meanwhile, the card shows the time left and "Stop", pressed twice, for what was paid back), and where the cradle's last miner was lost. The bay shows the miners of the player using it, else the local player's. Like everything else, building, stopping and leaving aboard are locked during a mission.
  - **Skills:** a holo graph of all skills, projected above an emitter in the skills room and facing the camera, styled like the star map: each cluster is a constellation with its name, the skills its glowing points, the links between them lit once bought. Bought skills glow fully, ones that can be bought right now pulse, ones that open later stay dim. The mouse pans (dragging) and zooms (wheel) smoothly; on a gamepad or keyboard the highlight jumps to the nearest skill in that direction (the view follows it) and `tab_prev`/`tab_next` jump between clusters. The card scales with the zoom, so it keeps its size on screen, and it sits where it covers no other skill (so the mouse can still point at them) and stays in view.
- The holo card of a selected upgrade shows the MK level as pips, how much the next level improves (compared to MK1), the next level's cost (ores, from MK4 on an alloy's ingot badge after them) and an "Upgrade" button, plus what the hold has of each. Costs the hold can't pay show in red and can't be bought; upgrades of weapons the player doesn't own yet are greyed out ("Locked"). Levels beyond the ship's reach show as faint pips, and at the highest level the ship allows the card says which plate brings the next one ("MK4 comes with heat shield MK2 (engine room).") while still showing its cost. Like the loadout, buying is locked while a mission is underway. A bought level runs for a while first (see below). The card floats beside the selected part, an amber leader line points from it to the part, and the part gets an amber outline.
- **Greybox** (all **TBD (playtest)**): the weapons rack and workbench, the fitting frame and the maintenance pad are primitives (the pad carries the miner model at three quarters of its size); the player stays where they stand (the card and the highlighted part draw on top, so they don't hide them).
- **Up to the ship's reach:** the highest MK level that can be bought is that of the bodies the ship's heat shield reaches (tier + 2): MK3 while only Torvane is open, one more with each plate, MK8 with Zairen. In co-op the same number is each body's cap (the tier sync, see [Co-op progression](#co-op-progression)).
- **Paid from the player's own hold, by one rule** (`game/progression/upgrades/mk_costs.tres`, the same for every upgrade): each MK level costs a common and a mid ore, from MK3 on also the signature ore of the planet that brought the level, so each planet's signature ore buys that planet's MK level and the way onward (the heat shield), and from MK4 on bars of the alloy of the previous planet's signature ore, so it can be refined before the level opens (one furnace makes MK4's 4 Merisk in 12 min, the level takes 15; MK8's 12 Noxar in 2 h, the level 6 h). Auralt stays for Zenith and the station's requests. Higher levels ask for rarer commons and mid ores and more of each. Each upgrade scales the amounts (`Upgrade.cost_scale`: weapon damage and the drill 1, the armor 1.25, magazines, the ore tank and the hose 0.75), the ores rounded to tens, the bars to whole ones (at least one); the miners' parts too. - **Upgrades and skills take real time.** A level or a skill rank is paid when started and then runs on the wall clock, also during runs and while the game is closed; it takes effect when it's done (`TimedJob` in `Upgrades.jobs` / `Skills.jobs`, applied by the ship's `ProgressClock` every second while no mission is underway and by the save on loading), so one finishing during a run waits until everyone is back aboard. Each time is set by hand per level and rank (`UpgradeLevel.seconds`, `SkillRank.seconds`), so each can be tuned on its own; the first values follow one ladder, **TBD (playtest)**: MK2 1 min, MK3 5 min, MK4 15 min, MK5 40 min, MK6 1.5 h, MK7 3 h, MK8 6 h, and a skill rank takes the ladder's step depth + rank − 1 (dash 1 min, regeneration 5 min, reinforced suit 1 / 5 / 15 min). One level per part (one rank per skill) at a time, any number of parts and skills at once (also several parts of one weapon), so the hold and the runs are the limit. Before starting, the card says how long the next level or rank takes ("Takes 5:00"). The card of one under way fills its pip from the left, says "MK3 under way" (a skill "Rank 2 under way", or "Learning" with a single rank) and "Time left: 4:12" and offers "Stop" (pressed twice: "Stop for sure"), which gives back what was paid (kept with the job, so retuning the costs doesn't change it; a job saved before that gives back the ores of its cost, a miner's build its whole cost); stopping is locked during a mission like starting. On the weapons and the helmet the pip of a level under way breathes dimly and flashes once when it's done; on the skill graph a ring fills around the skill's point. A soft chime plays at the station selling it when one finishes (for the local player; nothing on the screen). A save keeps each job's level, start, its time as started and what was paid, so retuning doesn't shift running jobs; a clock set back makes a job wait, never undoes it. Building a weapon stays instant. The debug overlay's "Finish jobs" finishes them all (and the miners being built); "Build miners" builds one in every empty cradle at once. Planned: the quest board's list of running jobs (see [`planned/quests.md`](planned/quests.md)).
- **Greybox upgrades** (`game/progression/upgrades/`, a weapon's parts in its folder, MK1–MK8, multipliers **TBD (playtest)**): each weapon has three parts (`WeaponType.parts`, a list, so a weapon can get more later): its damage (+25 % per level, up to +175 % at MK8), its capacity (ranged weapons' magazines: +50 % and +100 % at MK2 and MK3, then +25 % per level up to +225 %: Harl 24 → 36 → 48 → ... → 78 rounds, Brask 6 → 9 → 12 → ... → 20 shells, buying one fills the magazines to the new size; the melee weapons' attack speed: Dray's and Riven's swings, Auger's thrusts, Jag's blade, +10 % per level; Mantle's field charge like a battery, up to twice at MK8) and a part of its own (Harl's fire rate +10 % per level, Brask's pellets one more per level in the same fan, 7 → 14, Dray's arc width 15° more per level, 120° → 225°, and quicker by 15 % per level up to twice as quick at MK8: Pinner's recharge, Grindle's spin-up, Quill's charge time; Tremor's blast wider by up to +60 % at MK8, 3.5 m → 5.6 m; Wick's charges one more out at once per level, 3 → 10; Fulgen's jumps one more per level, 3 → 10; Nadir's well radius up to +50 %, 5 m → 7.5 m; Scald's burn longer and Zenith's heat building slower, up to twice at MK8; Kith's range up to +50 %, 12 m → 18 m; Riven's stagger (every slash's) up to twice at MK8; Auger's drill ramping up to twice as quick; Jag's width like Dray's arc, 100° → 187.5°; Clout's charge time quicker like Quill's, its throw up to +60 % farther with up to +60 % of the hit passed on; Mantle's block angle like Dray's arc, 120° → 225°, the bash as wide); Kith has its heal rate (like damage) in place of damage; Pinner's battery, Nadir's battery (one more well per level, 2 → 9), Grindle's belt, Quill's, Tremor's, Wick's and Fulgen's magazines and the heat weapons' fuel cells are their capacity; the capacity and own parts cost three quarters, the suit armor's health (+25 % per level: 100 → 125 → 150 → ... → 275 HP), and for each crafted miner the drill (fill rate +25 % per level, and harder ores at full speed, see [Miners](#7-miners)), the ore tank (capacity like the magazines), the hose (range and transfer rate +25 % per level) and the plating (health +25 % per level). A finished armor level adds its extra health right away.
- **Each player has their own levels** (like their weapons); each crafted miner has its own.
- **Shown in the world:** small pips, one per MK level, the reached ones glowing, in rows of up to four (MK8: two rows of four, the second filled after the first; a row keeps the length of three pips): on each weapon (behind Harl's sight, on Brask's receiver and barrels, on Dray's head, along the top of Pinner, Grindle, Quill, Tremor, Wick, Scald, Kith, Zenith, Fulgen and Nadir; Pinner's, Wick's, Kith's, Zenith's, Fulgen's and Nadir's smaller, Scald's burn row on its pipe) and on top of the helmet for the armor (the isometric camera always sees it). Each of a weapon's parts has its own row (`DamagePips`, `CapacityPips`, ... in its scene): the magazine's glows in the ammo color, in front of the damage row on top of the weapon, the part of its own further along.

### Co-op progression

- The **host's save** holds the ship (its heat shield and where it is), story, crew and quests. Each player keeps their **own hold, weapons, skills and loadout** and brings them along, and spends their own ore on their own gear; players can inspect each other's gear. No trading for now.
- Everyone gets the full haul (all the ore brought aboard, by anyone) into their own hold; nothing is split, and what a player loses by dying is lost for everyone (it never got aboard). The ship's project (the heat shield's next plate) takes what it still needs from the haul before it's shared, so in co-op everyone pays the same and nobody's hold is touched (see Opening planets in [Missions](#6-missions)); the host pays the rest from their own hold if they choose. Planned: quest deliveries filling from the haul the same way, and the cargo hatch and the silos showing what the ship took (see [`planned/coop-ore.md`](planned/coop-ore.md)).
- **Drop-in:** friends can join a player's session at any time, also while they're alone on their ship; solo and co-op aren't separate modes, solo just means nobody else is in the session. Invites go out from the comms station on the bridge. Progress can only be reset then (see [Death & persistence](#11-death--persistence)).
- Each player places **their own miners**; a lost miner is lost for its owner.
- **Tier sync:** each body has a maximum MK level (its tier + 2, the same number that caps buying, see [Upgrade stations](#upgrade-stations)); planned with co-op: stronger gear counts as that level, so guests with better gear don't carry the host through. Weapon types and unlocked skills stay. Alien numbers and health scale with the player count.
