# UI: in the world, not on the screen

- **No 2D HUD** where it can be avoided. Game information is shown in the world or on the player (diegetic UI). This also keeps desktop and VR the same: a screen-space HUD doesn't work in VR anyway.
- **Where things show:** what a player reads in a fight on the suit ring around their feet (health, ammo, spare magazines, the dash, where hits come from, the pointers), carried resources on the player's back (and ore arriving above them), a miner's fill level on the miner itself (and with its health and hose range on its ring on the ground), mission time above the door and by the storm building up; exact amounts aboard (the silos in the cargo bay). Nothing needs to be looked up during a run. The suit's own lights (the collar band mirroring the health, the weapon's counter) stay as looks and for teammates. Three layers: critical things (low health, an empty or reloading weapon, a hit) are always readable and big, situational ones (spare magazines, the dash) show when they change, details (exact amounts, MK levels) stay aboard. Every kind of marking on the ground has its own shape: the player's ring solid arcs, the target lock corner brackets, pointers arrows on the ring's edge, giving ammo a green band around the ring, a miner's hose range a thin dashed circle (alien telegraphs on the ground, like the spitter's landing ring and the brute's lane, see [Aliens](aliens.md)). Planned: the tracked quest's pointer (see [`planned/player-display.md`](../planned/player-display.md)).
- **The suit ring** (`SuitRing`, values **TBD (playtest)**): a ring the suit projects on the ground around the player (1.36 m radius, wider than the body; its lines keep at least 2 px on screen). Its halves stay fixed to the screen, not to where the player faces, and both arcs fill from the bottom up, so low values sit on the camera's side where the body never hides them; the top is left open where the body stands.
  - **Left arc, health:** green, amber below half, red and blinking below a quarter (it dims between blinks but never goes dark, so its length still reads), flashing white on damage (under steady damage like the storm's, a pulse every half second). A short red mark on the outer edge points to where a hit came from and fades within a second (up to four at once; the storm's damage comes from nowhere and has none).
  - **Right arc, ammo** (only with a ranged weapon drawn): the magazine in its ammo color (Harl, Quill, Zenith and Fulgen warm white, Brask and Tremor orange, Grindle and Scald amber, Pinner, Wick, Kith and Nadir gold), as a segment per round up to 16 rounds (Brask's shells, Pinner's and Nadir's battery, Quill's, Tremor's and Wick's rounds), else one bar; amber and blinking when nearly empty (a sixth or less), the whole arc blinking red when it's empty and nothing pays a reload. A magazine reload fills it up again with the timing window marked white (red after a miss); with single rounds the round being loaded brightens, white within the window. A drained battery (Pinner) fills it up the same way, without a window. While Quill charges, the charge fills the arc, white once it's full. A heat weapon's heat is a thin band just outside the ammo arc, filling from the bottom (amber, turning red as it nears full, blinking while it vents); a vent fills the ammo arc like a reload, with its window. It flashes white when a timed press finishes the reload and red when a reload can't start.
  - **Ticks inside the ammo arc:** the spare magazines, one per spare a run starts with (a partly used one dimmer, used ones only an outline); they show on a reload, when a spare is used or refilled and on switching weapons, then fade.
  - **Tick inside the health arc's bottom:** a ready rescue drone (a skill), pale green, lit with the health arc and dimmer while it's dimmed; blinking while the drone gets the player up, gone once it's used.
  - **Inner thin ring:** the dash, warm white, filling up again while it recharges, gone once it's ready.
  - **Outer band:** fills green while the player hands a teammate ammo.
  - **Ticks under the ring's bottom:** the run's miners (one per open cradle, up to six) in the miner color, left to right: the crafted ones going along (bright, by number), then standard-issue ones (dimmer); placed ones only an outline. They show for 3 s when one is placed or picked up, while the player places or picks one up, and while they stand next to a miner, and always with "Always show". Not in the top gap: the body hides that from the camera.
  - **Quiet when nothing goes on:** each arc dims to a faint outline 3 s after its last change (a hit, healing, firing, reloading, switching weapons), with its filled share still readable; health below half and a nearly empty magazine keep it lit. Aboard (the weapon holstered) the outline hides too, so only what changes shows. It hides while the player uses a menu and once dead.
  - **Pointers on the edge:** arrows just outside the hit marks: the way back to the ship in its status color (once the door is more than 12 m away, blinking in the last minute, the countdown beyond its tip, pushed out by the text's width so it never covers the ring, and keeping its size on screen when the camera moves in), the ore scanner's smaller arrow in the ore's color, and with the miner radar (a skill) a still smaller arrow to each of the player's own miners that needs them (amber when full or dry, blinking red under attack). With more arrows than room (nine), the ship's comes first, then the tracked quest's (planned), miners under attack, the ore scanner's and full or dry miners.
  - **On the ground:** it tilts with the ground under the player and is drawn over the ground, grass and low plants, but cliffs and props hide it like the body (the silhouette shows the player then).
  - **Settings** (on the pause panel and the settings terminal): its size (small, medium, large: 0.8, 1 and 1.25 times) and "Always show" (a checkbox), which keeps the arcs, the spare ticks and the miners' ticks lit instead of dimming. While the pause panel is open the ring stays shown and lit beside the card, so a new size shows right away. Health never relies on color alone: it blinks below a quarter, and the arc's length shows the share.
  - **Teammates' rings** show only the health arc inside a thin outline in their player color (1 orange, 2 sky blue, 3 yellow, 4 reddish purple), with no ammo, ticks, dash, hit marks or pointers, so four players don't cover the ground. Only the player a machine plays reads its input; until co-op (M7), the debug panel adds teammates that stand still.
- **Target lock on the ground:** four corner brackets in the player's color frame the locked-on alien or nest, sized to its body and kept square to the screen like the ring; drawn over the ground the same way.
- **Ore arriving:** while a hose delivers, a line above the player shows each arriving ore's badge with the amount ("+180", several ores side by side), counting up from the first unit; once the ore stops coming (slower than 5 units a second) it rises and fades over 2 s. A slow trickle (a miner filling while the player stands by) shows once it adds up to 10 units; one that stops before that, or that a hose starts over (the next miner), is forgotten. Spending ore (ammo) shows nothing.
- **The object is the menu.** Where something is chosen or bought, the player uses the thing itself, not a screen next to it: the weapon on a workbench, their own suit in a fitting frame, a miner on its maintenance pad, the star map on the holo table, the skill graph. The camera frames it, its parts become selectable (a weapon's barrel for its damage, its magazine, a miner's drill), and a small **holo card** beside the selected part shows what it is, its level pips (the same pips as on the weapons and the helmet), what the next level does, its cost and the action. This holds any number of weapons, upgrades and skills (there may be 50–100 skills), gives the rooms things to stand at (they fill with assets and NPCs later), and German only has to fit one kind of card. Flat **terminals** stay for system things only: the settings in cabin 1 and the comms station on the bridge.
- **Using one** (values **TBD (playtest)**): a player walks up and uses it (`interact`) from any side they can reach: within reach of the object itself (about 1.2 to 1.6 m from its edge), not just from a spot in front of it. They stay where they are (no stepping to a fixed spot), stand still facing what's selected, and their camera moves in to frame it (keeping the isometric angle); nobody standing in front of it hides it. Leaving moves the camera back. One player uses it at a time.
  - **Mouse:** the cursor points at the parts and the card's buttons, like a 2D menu.
  - **Gamepad and keyboard:** movement (left stick, WASD, also the d-pad and arrow keys) moves the highlight to the nearest part (on a screen: button) in that direction (the nearest within about 60° of it, so the near row comes before the one behind it; where several stations share a room, like the cargo bay's silos, furnaces and ingot rack, across all of them, never into another room; on the weapon racks along their grid: left and right along a line, up and down to the nearest weapon on the line above or below), `interact` presses the card's action; `tab_prev`/`tab_next` (LB/RB, Q/R; E is `interact`) jump between groups (the next row of weapons, the next skill cluster); `pause` (Escape, Menu) or B steps back and closes.
  - **VR (later):** a controller points like the mouse does.
  - Cards and screens lean back to face the camera and glow in the dark rooms (cyan text and frames, the highlight in amber like the floor markings). **Text size:** body text is at least about 2% of the framed view's height (about 22 px at 1080p). Every card and screen is tested to fit in every language (German runs about a third longer).
- **Starting up:** while the engine starts, the boot splash shows the game's emblem (a glowing cyan ore crystal in a ring of storm clouds with amber lightning) and its name on black; it turns into the **boot screen**, the same picture in the world, where a thin cyan ring around the emblem fills while the game loads. Once it's full, the title view fades in from black (the boot screen's last frame stays until the title's first one is drawn). It's the only loading screen (see [Spaceship](ship.md)); it shows no text to translate.
- **A title, not a main menu:** with one save there's nothing to choose. The game opens on a **title view in the world**: the ship drifting in space, seen from outside (its hull closed, the view drifting slowly around it), the game's name as a hologram above it and "Press any button" below. Any button (a key, a mouse or gamepad button; moving the mouse or a stick doesn't count) ends it, and the camera glides into the ship to the player in cabin 1. After a while without input (greybox: 30 s, **TBD (playtest)**) the credits scroll there instead of the prompt; any input brings the prompt back.
- **The pause panel:** `pause` (Escape, Menu/Options) with no menu open shows a holo card beside the player's suit ring, facing the camera, which moves in like at a station, framing the player, the whole ring (its marks, pointers and the ship's countdown) and the card; the card never covers the ring and keeps its size on screen, so its text stays readable. The player stands still. It holds **Resume**, **Settings** and **Quit**; `pause` again or "Resume" closes it, and on the other pages `pause` steps back, focusing the button that opened the page. Since the card keeps to the bottom of its screen, a page's "Back" sits where the first page's "Quit" appears: after a page changes, a mouse click only counts once the pointer has moved a little. **Solo, the world stops** while it's open (aliens, the timer, the storm, the ship's flight); it also opens by itself when the game's window loses focus. **In co-op the game goes on**: the card's title says "Menu" instead of "Paused" with the note "The game goes on", and the player can still be attacked, like at any station.
- **Settings in one place, reachable everywhere:** the same settings show on the pause panel's settings page and on the settings terminal in cabin 1: the language, the voice language ("Voices", see [Localization and voice](localization-and-voice.md)), whether talks continue by themselves ("Dialogue", same section), the suit ring's size and "Always show" and **Log files**: "Open folder" shows the game's log files in the system's file manager, to attach to a bug report. The sound volumes are a page of their own, **Sound** (see [Sound](sound.md)), opened from the settings like the credits. The terminal also keeps the one dangerous thing, **Reset progress** (see [Death & persistence](death-and-persistence.md)). Both have a **Credits** page: who made the game, the tools, and the third-party licenses (Godot's own and those of the components it's built from); the authors of recorded sounds join them once the first recording that needs a credit is used (see [Sound](sound.md)).
- **Quitting:** "Quit" on the pause panel works like the bed: aboard it saves and quits to the desktop at once, fading to black; during a run it asks first, since quitting loses the run (one confirm, "Cancel" focused). The bed in one's own cabin (cabin N for player N) stays as the in-world way: a station whose card offers "Sleep", which saves the progress one last time, fades to black (the camera stays on the bed) and quits. No confirmation, since nothing is lost aboard; during a run the card warns that sleeping loses it (what changes during a run is only saved once it's over). Other players' beds can't be used. **Inviting friends** (with co-op, M7): at a comms station on the bridge, which opens the platform's invite (the Steam overlay is the platform's, not a HUD); friends come aboard through the cargo bay. The comms station comes with co-op, together with the comms log (radio lines to listen to again). Planned: leaving and ending a co-op session from the pause panel, joining a friend from the title view, and both in VR (see [`planned/menu-pause.md`](../planned/menu-pause.md)).
- **Exceptions:** developer tools (debug overlay for tuning) are allowed in 2D.
