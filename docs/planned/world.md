# The world (planned)

Decided 2026-10-27: the frame every writing round works in (see [`story.md`](story.md)). Most of it is never said outright, but every line has to fit it. Numbers are what lines can rely on, not exact counts. Still open: the ship's name (see Open below).

## Timeline

- **About 25 years ago** the survey charted the lane to Kesh and built Waypost Ostra as its relay and the prospectors' station: a docking ring and a few modules, a few hundred people, crews like Hollis' coming and going. Wren was born there.
- **About 3 years ago** the colony came: the system was declared worth settling, and haulers brought thousands of settlers (Tamsin flew one), most of them asleep for the months through the lane. Ostra grew into the colony's hub, the settlements on Torvane's surface were founded.
- **About 2 years ago** Calder's science expedition went inward, to Zairen's Aethrine (known from the survey; nobody knew of Kesh's flares when the expedition was planned); it has been silent for about a year.
- **The game starts** about three years after landfall, with the players' crew new to prospecting (Ostra's people greet them as "another prospecting crew").

## Travel

- **Ships are fast inside the system:** a flight between planets takes a few hours in the world (the game shows it in under a minute); lines never give exact times. Every ship can reach any body, the players' ship any one its heat shield allows, and it flies to any open planet, moon or station whenever the players want.
- **Between stars only through the lane**, a jump point at the system's edge (every system's lanes lie at its edge), where big lane ships cross now and then; a crossing takes months. Any ship with a lane drive can cross (the colony's haulers crossed on their way in), but only with a **beacon** at each end to lock onto; without one, ships jump blind and don't come out. A beacon's core is Aethrine, the only crystal that holds a lane's tune.
- **Contact with home** goes with the lane ships, a few times a year; there is no live connection. Kesh's flares are burning out the lane watch's beacon: it weakens in act 2 (Ostra Control mentions a rough lane window) and goes dark in act 3, so no lane ship can come and nobody can leave. Nobody rescues them, without a villain; the lane ship that doesn't come is a story beat. The way out is a new beacon (see [`story.md`](story.md), What the colony builds).

## Who's behind it

- **The charter:** a company far away that charted the lane, holds the system's rights and buys the ores; the colony is its contract. Never seen in person, only in what people say and in the council's reports: distant and counting, not evil. The council still answers to it (the reason it downplays the danger in act 3), and Ilse distrusts it.
- **The survey's report** called Kesh stable for centuries. One old reading, found by Calder, showed signs that the report smoothed over: a line in a late log, no conspiracy and no one to punish; it makes Ostra angry for a moment and Ilse right.
- **Why the ores matter:** the signature ores aren't Kesh's alone: they form wherever a star's radiation is strong, and they're known back home as what lane drives, beacons and shields are built from; Kesh has them rich, the reason the lane was worth opening. Scientists knew from the survey that Zairen has Aethrine, the rarest. NPCs know the ores by name and use. Never more detailed than that.

## The hive and the star

- **Kesh is natural:** a flare star whose cycle the survey didn't catch. The danger is nature, the drama is people.
- **The hive** is native life at an animal's level: no intelligence, no language, no queen giving orders, no plan against humans. It feeds on the ores' radiation and defends what it feeds on; the Ravager is its biggest animal, not a boss with a mind.
- **It comes from near the star:** spores ride the flares' outflow from the inner planets outward, which is why the hive is strongest inside, grows with the storms and spreads outward (act 3's "the hive spreading" means outward, from where the star is worst; the expedition found out, Okoro reads it in a log). From act 4 on, the outer planets' regions get one more nest each (**TBD (playtest)**, a story flag on the voyage settings).
- **Nothing older:** no ruins, no lost civilization, no artifacts. The hive is the only life besides humans; the planets' past is the expedition's.

## People and daily life

- **Technology:** no thinking machines (no AI companions, no androids): autopilots, miners and drones do what they're told, people do the rest. Med beds heal wounds; nothing grows people back.
- **Falling out there:** when a suit's wearer goes down, the suit locks them into a stasis shell, and its beacon calls the ship's retrieval drone; the carried ore is dumped to make them light enough. Ilse patches them up in the medical bay. The planned rescue drone skill is a faster drone, the hardened canister one the dump can't open (see [`skills.md`](skills.md)).
- **Money:** the charter pays in credit off-world, which buys nothing here. Inside the system it's ore, alloys and favors; Kade's depot keeps the colony's account. No coins, no prices.
- **Origins:** mixed, one common working language, names from everywhere. Earth exists far away and is never named; no nations, no flags.
- **Customs** (Nell and Fenna carry them, a handful used across the acts): a toast at the bar to the crews out in the storm, a lamp lit in Ostra's gallery while a crew is out, names scratched into the bar's counter for everyone who didn't come back (Calder's people among them). The lamp and the names show in the world, too.
- **Time:** the colony counts days since landfall; after act 2 (Okoro's cycle) people count the flare cycle instead, and the flare's date (act 4) becomes the countdown everybody knows. In lines only, no calendar on screen.

## The colony

- **About 7,000 people:** Ostra about 2,500 (old station hands and new settlers), the settlements on Torvane's surface about 3,500, the smaller stations a few hundred each, a few dozen prospecting crews (most of 3–10 people, Rook's the biggest). Small enough that everyone knows someone who knows you: Nell knows every crew by name, a crew lost out there is news everywhere, and one ship's hauls matter.
- **Tensions are political and personal,** never a hostile faction to shoot: the station hands against the settlers, the council against the crews who take the risks, the surface settlements feeling forgotten, the people who won't leave (a thread in acts 5–6, a colonist's, maybe the hauler pilot's family).
- **Ostra can't leave:** an orbital station has no drive. The colony leaves its home behind (Wren's birthplace); the smaller stations and outposts are emptied into the convoy first (act 5's work between missions: ferrying people and supplies). Ostra's core has a storm shelter for about a hundred people: those who won't leave stay and ride out the flare, the skeleton crew of the post-game.
- **The convoy:** about 7,000 people in 8–12 big haulers. They gather at the lane watch and, once the new beacon is lit, cross a few at a time over days while it holds against the rising flare; the players' ship crosses last (the ending's camera moment).

## Stations and traffic

- **The smaller stations** (see [`station.md`](station.md)): **Gaulm's skimmer**, a fuel station skimming the gas giant's upper clouds for the drives (the expedition refuelled there, near their base); **a farm station**, hydroponics in orbit around Lisk or Vehra, feeding Ostra (Fenna's supplier); **the lane watch**, the survey's first outpost by the lane at the system's edge, its beacon and traffic control, the oldest place in the system (Hollis' and Calder's old haunt), with its beacon keeper and two or three hands. It opens in act 3, when the beacon fails and the crew flies out to see why; the new beacon is lit there and the convoy gathers there in act 6.
- **Traffic, a little:** haulers between Ostra, the surface, the farm and the skimmer, prospecting crews coming and going, a lane ship now and then. It shows as ships passing far off during flights (their running lights), another ship at Ostra's second clamp (Rook's when he's in, a hauler otherwise) and other crews' landing scars in the regions. Over the radio only **Ostra Control** (a new small voice, one of the old station hands, maybe Wren's father on shift), one short line on docking and one on undocking ("Clamps are closed. Welcome back."), a few variants by act; no chatter during runs.
- **Traffic follows the story:** it thins out as the system turns dangerous (acts 3–4, crews staying home after a storm), grows into the evacuation (acts 5–6, haulers everywhere, ships gathering near Ostra into the convoy) and is gone after the ending: part of Ostra's change per act.

## The ship

- It belongs to the crew together, no captain: an old ship they bought second-hand, which is why it's worn and why it's theirs. It gets a name, painted on the hull and said now and then (Tamsin over the radio, Ostra Control); which one is open.

## Telling it

- Through people (topics, talks, messages), the expedition's logs and Nell's rumors, plus a line on each body's navigator card (what the survey called it). No codex or encyclopedia station: the game's story is listened to, not read.
