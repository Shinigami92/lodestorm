# Miners and the hose

- Placed by the player on ore / mineral / resource nodes.
- One miner per node. The player places it on the nearest free node in range (`place_miner`); a preview of the miner it would place stands on that node (see Choosing which miner below).
- **Placing and picking up take time** (`MinerHandling` on the player, greybox 3 s and 5 s in `MinerSettings`, **TBD (playtest)**), so collecting the miners at the end of a run is a decision against the clock. Placing takes the node at once and projects the miner onto it as a hologram (cyan, see through, scan lines drifting up, like a miner being built in the miners' bay) that turns into the real miner from the bottom up, a bright line at the edge, and lands with the thud; picking up is the reverse, the real miner turning back into the hologram from the top down until it's gone, ending with a latch. Its number plate, fill and hose show only once it's real. The player moves, dashes and fights freely meanwhile: it only goes on while they stay within the miner's hose range (the dashed circle of its ring); out of it, it waits (the hologram dims, the ring's progress band and circle blink) and goes on when they come back. One at a time: while one goes on, nothing else is placed or picked up; placing or picking up another while it waits gives it up (nothing lost: a miner being placed goes back to the fleet, one being picked up turns real again where it stands). Taking damage doesn't cancel it, dying does. Meanwhile it doesn't mine, its hose stays off and nobody else can pick it up.
- Fill slowly over time; the player needs to place several for a good haul. Target: an empty miner takes about 2–5 min to fill (so a few fills per mission); exact values **TBD (playtest)**.
- **Stop when full.** Capacity and speed can be upgraded (see [Upgrade stations](progression.md#upgrade-stations)) / better miner types.
- **Nodes run dry** (values **TBD (playtest)**): each node holds a limited amount (`OreNode.FILLS`), counted in fills of a standard-issue miner's tank (180 units): common nodes 2 fills, mid 1.5, signature 1 (rarer ore, smaller deposits); a node in a nest hollow's rich field holds double (`OreNode.RICH_FACTOR`). Its miner takes what it mines out of the node, so a bigger tank empties a node in fewer trips but never makes it bigger. The node shows it: its crystals shrink into the rock one after another as it empties, the smallest first (the loose chips around the pad go at once), each holding its share by its volume, so a node being mined visibly wears down; a dry node is only its rock. A miner on a node that runs dry stops like a full one (with the same chime), but its lamp blinks amber instead of glowing steady: "move me". A dry node takes no miner (no placing ring) and the ore scanner skips it; the navigator's hologram still shows every node (a preview before landing).
- Fill level is shown on the miner itself: a glass column fills up in the ore color, and a lamp on top lights up when it's full. Aliens attack it from its surface: their reach counts from the edge of its body, not its center.
- **Its ring** (`MinerRing`, values **TBD (playtest)**): every miner projects a ring on the ground, always shown, like a player's suit ring: the health arc on the screen's left (green, amber below half, red below a quarter, empty with a blinking red outline while broken) and the tank's fill arc on its right in the ore color (white when full), both filling from the bottom up just outside its legs (0.25 m beyond its body), the top left open where the miner stands; a thin dashed circle at its hose range (5 m, more with the hose upgrade), solid while the hose is connected; while it's placed or picked up, a thin band inside the arcs going around clockwise from the top with the progress in the hologram's color.
- Art pass: the miner model gets a small display showing the fill counter (amount / capacity) and a timer (time until full).
- **Attacked by aliens** (raiders, see [Combat & aliens](weapons.md)): a miner has health (greybox 100); the frame flashes white when hit and turns rusty with damage. At zero it **breaks down**: it stops mining and the hose disconnects, so its ore can't be taken out until it's repaired, but it keeps the ore. The frame blinks red while broken.
- **Repair:** a player standing next to a miner (greybox 2.5 m) repairs it automatically, 25 health/s; a broken miner works again only once it's repaired completely (4 s from broken). Damaged miners that still work get repaired too. Values **TBD (playtest)**.
- Defense options like shields and sentry turrets are possible later.
- **Crafted miners** (values **TBD (playtest)**): a player's miners are built in the ship and kept (`MinerFleet` on the player, saved with them), up to six, each in its cradle in the miners' room with its number ("M-01" to "M-06"; the number belongs to the cradle) and its own parts and MK levels: drill, ore tank, hose and **plating** (health +25 % per level, 100 → 275 HP at MK8). Every player starts with M-01.
- **The ship's open cradles** (`Cradles` on the ship, the host's save): the ship was stripped and sold off before Brannik bought it, so it starts with two cradles (M-01's and M-02's); the others come back one by one (planned: one per act in acts 1–4 through the main quest, see [`planned/balance.md`](../planned/balance.md); until then the debug overlay's "Open cradle"). A miner is built only in an open cradle. A missing one is a stripped spot in the bay's row: two bare floor rails, three cables cut off at the back, capped and taped, a faded ring on the floor where its base stood and its number plate, dimmed; its card says it was sold off before the ship was bought and offers nothing to build. On the planet each crafted miner wears its number plate on a side box above a strip in its owner's player color. Damage only counts during a run: a crafted miner comes back aboard at full health, free; one picked up and placed again in the same run keeps its damage.
- **Miners per run:** each player takes one miner per open cradle into a run (two at the start, six with every cradle): their crafted ones that go along (the first ones by number, the others stay aboard), **standard-issue** ones filling up the rest, so nobody is ever without miners. Which one goes onto a node is the player's choice (see below). Standard-issue miners are the ship's: free, refilled for every run, MK1 in everything, mining at 75 % of the fill rate, with a plain grey plate, never lost. Once a player's miners for the run are placed, `place_miner` places nothing more until one is picked up. Planned: a team's shared budget in co-op (see [`planned/balance.md`](../planned/balance.md)).
- **Choosing which miner to place** (`MinerChoice`, `MinerPicker` on the player, values **TBD (playtest)**): with two to six miners whose drills, tanks and plating differ, which miner goes where is a decision.
  - **The default:** placing takes the miner with the softest drill that still mines the node's ore at full speed (a standard-issue one before a crafted one with the same drill level, crafted ones by number), so the better drills stay free for harder ore without the player doing anything; without one at full speed, the fastest (crafted ones first by number on a tie).
  - **The preview** (`MinerPreview`): while a player is in range of a free node and has a miner left to place, a faint hologram of the miner `place_miner` would place stands on the nearest free node: fainter than one being placed, slowly breathing, no scan lines. It fades in on the node (again when the nearest free node changes) and out when the player walks off, has none left or places or picks up a miner nearby; `place_miner` turns it into the building hologram at once. It wears the miner's number plate (a plain grey one for standard-issue) above its owner's color strip and a label on its side box with its parts ("Drill MK5 · Tank MK3"); its color says how its drill fits the ore: cyan at full speed, amber when it's too soft. A thin circle on the ground around it is lit clockwise from the top up to a tick for the time it would take to fill on this node (or until the node runs dry), the full circle 10 min (`MinerPreview.DIAL_TIME`). Only its own player sees it (the local player's; in co-op each player sees only their own).
  - **Switching:** `miner_prev` and `miner_next` (Z and X, the d-pad's left and right; `tab_prev`/`tab_next` share Q/R and the bumpers with `switch_weapon` and `reload` during a run) step the preview through the miners still to place (the crafted ones by number, then a standard-issue one), wrapping around, while there's a node to place on. The choice sticks until it's placed, also on another node; the next preview starts on the default again. In VR the binding comes with M6's controller mapping.
  - In the art pass, drill heads and tanks look different per MK level.
- **Picking up:** `interact` next to a miner (within 2.5 m, the repair range; an amber ring around its foot shows which one) picks it up (see the time it takes above): what's still in its tank goes onto the player's back, the miner back to its owner's count to place again. Any player can pick up any miner (a teammate rescues a dead player's miner; it stays its owner's), broken ones too.
- **Lost:** a crafted miner still standing on the planet when the run ends (everyone alive is aboard and the ramp goes up, or everyone died) is lost there, together with a level of its parts under way; its cradle empties and its card says where ("Lost on Torvane, Redscar Flats") until a new one is built there.
- **Rarer ores mine slower** (per ore, greybox: mid ores 85 %, signature ores 70 % of the fill rate, **TBD (playtest)**).
- **Harder ores need better drills,** so miners have their own progression (all **TBD (playtest)**): each ore has a hardness (`OreType.hardness`: common ores 0, mid ores 1, Meridine and Emerin 2, Indral and Selyx 3, Noctile and Aethrine 4), and a drill's MK level sets how hard an ore it mines at full speed, set by hand per level (`Upgrade.hardness` for MK1, `UpgradeLevel.hardness`): MK1–2 up to 1, MK3–4 up to 2, MK5–6 up to 3, MK7–8 up to 4. So each planet's signature ore mines at full speed with the level that planet's heat shield plate allows (Torvane MK3: Meridine, Cassor MK5: Indral, Skelde MK7: Noctile), once it's bought. A drill too soft still mines it at a quarter of the speed (`MinerSettings.soft_drill_share`, on top of the ore's own rate, the drill's level and the standard-issue share), so no ore is ever blocked, only slow: a standard-issue miner (MK1) on Meridine mines at about 13 % of the base rate, a crafted one with drill MK3 at about 105 %. The drill turns while it mines; on an ore too hard for it, it turns at that share and its loop plays lower and wavering, strained. No lamp or blink (the amber blink is for nodes running dry).

## Hose transfer

- When the player is within a miner's radius, a hose automatically connects the miner to the player and transfers resources.
- Player can move and shoot while connected.
- Leaving the radius disconnects the hose and stops transfer; re-entering reconnects.
- Each miner has its own hose, so several miners in range transfer at once. In co-op a miner connects to the nearest player in range.
- The hose sags between the miner and the player's back, rests on the ground when it's long, and has the ore color.
- Radius and transfer speed are miner values (upgradable with the miner): greybox 5 m and 30 units/s, so a full miner (180 units) empties in 6 s. **TBD (playtest)**.
- **No carry limit:** everything is carried until returning to the ship; a soft limit (a full load slowing the player down) was dropped, since it would fight the "one more trip" decision. The risk is dying: everything carried is lost.
- Carried resources show on the player's back as one stack of ore poured into glass vessels, like layered sand: the rarest ore at the bottom, the commonest on top, each ore a band in its glow. The stack fills the backpack's two tubes first (left, then right), then canisters strapped on beside the backpack (two stacked per side, a third farther out; at most six, beyond that they all show full), so a big haul makes the silhouette bulkier, also for teammates. Ammo takes the commonest ore first, so it drains the top of the stack and rare ore stays safe at the bottom. Greybox: a tube and a canister each hold one full miner (180 units), **TBD (playtest)**. Exact amounts show aboard, at the silos once the ore is in the hold.
