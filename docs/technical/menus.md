# 3D menus

Menus are things in the world (see [`design/ui.md`](../design/ui.md)), each a `WorldMenu` (`game/ui/world_menu.gd`): one player uses it at a time (`open`/`close`, `Player.menu`), and while they do, the camera frames it (`view_height`, `view_focus`). There are two kinds: screens (`WorldPanel`, on terminals) and stations (`Station`, the object is the menu).

**Screens:** a `WorldPanel` (`game/ui/world_panel.tscn`) renders a normal Control scene (`menu`) in a SubViewport onto an unshaded quad. A `Terminal` (`game/ui/terminal.tscn`, an `Interactable`) is a console with such a screen; set its `menu` to the Control scene. The settings terminal in cabin 1 shows `game/ui/settings_menu.tscn` (the only terminal left: system things only).

- **Items:** `ItemType` (`game/items/item_type.gd`) is the base of everything a hold keeps (`OreType`, `AlloyType`): its name, color, glow pattern (`ItemType.Glow`), dark body and badge icon (`get_badge_icon`: an alloy without an icon draws an ingot with its tier's ticks, `IngotIcon`), so holds, cards and tests handle every kind the same way. `PlayerHold` keeps a player's units per item (`ores()` gives the ores for the cards, whose costs are ore costs until alloys join them).
- **Item badges:** `ItemBadge` (`game/items/item_badge.gd`) shows an item on a card: its `ItemType.icon` (a white crystal silhouette with grey facets on transparent) through `assets/shaders/ore_badge.gdshader`, which tints the greys in the ore's color and plays its glow pattern (from `ore_glow.gdshaderinc`; sparkle per grey level, i.e. per facet; shifting turns the hue; a dark body is a dark icon with a colored rim and facet edges), and optionally its translated name. An item without an icon gets a four-facet crystal drawn in code (`ItemBadge.fallback_icon`). The navigator, flight and cost (`ItemCost`, `game/ui/item_cost.gd`) rows list ores as badges with their names in `HFlowContainer`s, so they wrap; the cards are sized for six ores with the longest names (`tests/stations/test_menu_fit.gd`).
- **Building a menu:** a Control scene filling its viewport, laid out for `size * pixels_per_meter` (960 × 600 for the default 1.2 × 0.75 m screen), with `assets/ui/terminal_theme.tres` (fonts 30 px, titles 46 px with the `Title` variation; a focus style that stands out, since gamepads navigate by focus). A station's holo card uses the theme's card variations instead of overrides of its own: `Card` on its root (the panel), `CardTitle` (34 px), `CardText` (26 px), `CardNote` (26 px, dimmed: side notes, row names) and `CardStatus` (26 px, amber: where things stand); text that changes its color in code takes it from `CardPalette` (`game/ui/card_palette.gd`, the same colors; `tests/ui/test_card_palette.gd` checks both and that every card keeps to them). Texts go through translations like any scene (see Localization). A control closes its menu with `WorldPanel.showing(self).close()`.
- **Using:** `open(player)` sets `Player.menu`; the player then ignores movement, aiming and actions (an action still held when the menu opens or closes waits for its release, so the closing press doesn't act in the world). The `IsoCameraRig` moves to the menu (or its `view_focus`, e.g. a station's object and its card) and zooms until it sees `view_height` m (1.6 for a terminal, a station sets its own, **TBD (playtest)**; a `Terminal` passes both on to its panel). The screen material switches to no depth test while used and renders after the player's silhouette and rim light (`ON_TOP_PRIORITY`), so a player standing in front doesn't hide it.
- **Input:** while open, the panel takes the events in `_input`. Mouse events become a ray from the camera; `pixel_at(origin, direction)` turns it into a menu pixel, and the event is pushed into the SubViewport (`push_input(event, true)`: layout coordinates). A VR controller will use `pixel_at` the same way. Other events are translated into GUI actions (`NAVIGATION`: `move_*` and `ui_*` directions, `interact` and `ui_accept`), so the menus use Godot's focus navigation; a held stick presses once, held keys repeat. `pause` and `ui_cancel` close.
- **Sharpness:** the SubViewport keeps its layout size as `size_2d_override` (stretched) and renders at about the pixels the screen covers on the display, in half-octave steps (`_update_resolution`): zoomed in on a 4K display that's 2× the layout, from the isometric distance 1/8 (viewport textures have no mipmaps, so a big texture would shimmer). Fonts re-rasterize at that size (viewport oversampling), so text stays crisp.
- **Testing:** push input into the root viewport with `push_input(event, true)`: without it the window rescales the position (a headless window is tiny). Headless, the window is too small to test the render resolution; check it with a windowed probe.

**Stations** (`game/stations/station.gd`, each station in its own folder, e.g. `game/stations/navigator/`): an object whose parts are the menu. The scene's root is the `Station` (or a subclass); children:

- `Use`: an `Interactable` (with an optional `Highlight`) that opens it; disabled while used. It sits at the object's center: `interact_range` counts from its `footprint` (a rectangle on the floor, e.g. a bench or a bed) or, without one, from its center (round objects: their radius plus the reach), so players use it from any side.
- `view_focus` and `view_height` frame the object and the card. The player using it stays where they stood when they used it and looks at the selected part (`get_look_target`).
- `StationPart`s (`game/stations/station_part.gd`): the selectable parts. `item` is what the part stands for (an `Upgrade` at an upgrade station), `pick_radius` how close the mouse cursor must be (screen distance to its center), `card_offset` where the card floats (right/up on screen, in m), `outline` the meshes outlined while hovered or selected (a grown, front-culled material in `material_overlay`). `get_parts` lists the ones the highlight moves between (all visible parts by default), `get_pickable_parts` the ones the mouse can click.
- `Card`: a `WorldPanel` whose menu is a `StationCard` (`show_part`, `press_primary`). It isn't opened itself: the station passes mouse events to it (`WorldPanel.point`), turns it to face the camera every frame, glides it to the selected part and draws it on top (`set_on_top`), plus an amber leader line (an `ImmediateMesh` ribbon) from its edge to the part.

Input while used: mouse motion hovers the nearest pickable part, a click selects it (`_picked`, e.g. the weapon station lays a rack weapon on the bench); directions (`move_*`, `ui_*`) select the next part in that screen direction (`step` asks `_neighbor`: by default `_nearest_on_screen` of `room_parts`: the nearest part within `CONE` (60°) of the direction on screen, else the most nearly straight on (along the direction counting more than across); the locker and the weapon station move along their racks' grid instead, since a rack seen in perspective while the camera glides tilts its lines on screen). For stations sharing a `room` (`&"cargo_bay"`: `OreHold`, the furnaces, the `IngotRack`), `room_parts` are the parts of every usable station of the room not used by someone else, and stepping onto another one's part hands the user over (`hand_over`: close, select, open; `Station.of(part)` finds a part's station); `tab_prev`/`tab_next` call `_cycle` (the locker and the weapon station jump a row, the silos and the rack step through their own parts, a furnace through its recipes); a station's own keys go to `_shortcut` (the locker's `slot_1`/`slot_2`, keys 1 and 2); `interact`/`ui_accept` call the card's `press_primary`; `pause`/`ui_cancel` close.

What every station shares, so a subclass doesn't look it up itself: `mission` (found in the scene tree) and `ship` (the nearest one, looked up until it's there: ships join their group after their rooms are ready), both settable for tests; `is_locked` (while a mission is underway, for the stations that set `locks_while_underway` in `_init`: upgrades, skills, lockers, furnaces, the heat shield); `viewer` (whose state it shows: the user, else the ship's `get_viewing_player`, the player the cutaway follows, else the first local player). A two-press "Stop" is a `ConfirmPress` (`game/stations/confirm_press.gd`).

- **Upgrade stations:** `UpgradeStation` (`game/stations/upgrade/upgrade_station.gd`) with an `UpgradeCard` (`game/stations/upgrade/upgrade_card.tscn`): `can_buy`/`buy` pay from the hold of the player using it (`Player.hold`), locked while a mission is underway. `get_levels` is the `Upgrades` it buys into (the user's; the miners' bay: the miner on its pad). The armor (`armor_station.tscn`, a fitting frame, the player inside) places its part by hand. The miners' bay (`MinerBayStation`, `game/stations/miner_bay/miner_bay.tscn`) has the pad's parts by hand (the miner itself at its floor plate, drill, glass tank, cable and side boxes outlined) and builds its six `MinerCradle`s in code along `Cradles` (each a `StationPart` with a stowed miner at a third of its size and its plate; one being built or empty drawn with `miner_ghost.gdshader`, a see-through outline denser below `fill_top`); its `MinerCard` (an `UpgradeCard` with the parts' pip rows) shows the pad's miner, an empty cradle (build cost as items, `ItemCost.show_items`) or one being built, and a cradle's card floats towards the camera below the row (`_card_position`). Its card is bigger in the world for its wider view (`view_height` 6.6, the same text size on screen). `WeaponStation` (`weapon_station.tscn`, right of the weapons room's door) hangs `weapons` (an `Arsenal`, every weapon of the game) on its `Rack`, a `WeaponRack` (three columns, growing up), and lays the picked weapon on the bench. Its rack weapons aren't in `get_parts` but `_neighbor` reaches them (up from the bench's top part, down from the rack's bottom line back); selected, the `UpgradeCard` shows them with "Lay on the bench" (`show_pick`, `picking`), placed beside the rack (`_beside_rack`) with the camera between weapon and card. `_cycle` lays the first weapon of the next row on the bench; else `get_view_center` shifts halfway along the rack towards the bench weapon's place; its parts sit at the weapon scene's `MkPips` (damage) and `MagazinePips` (magazine). The weapons shown are their scenes with processing off (`WeaponDisplay`); weapons the player doesn't own are see-through (`GeometryInstance3D.transparency`).
- **Weapon racks:** `WeaponRack` (`game/stations/weapon_rack.gd`) hangs an `Arsenal` in rows by `WeaponType.origin` (enum order, top to bottom; within a row the arsenal's order; an origin without weapons gets no row), centered on its origin with the rows going down (`spacing`, `row_spacing`, `label_space` above each row for its name; `columns` wraps a long row onto more lines; `grow_up` puts the bottom line at its origin instead of the top one), a `Label3D` naming each row (`origin_name`, re-set on `NOTIFICATION_TRANSLATION_CHANGED`), and resizes its `panel` (and `collision`) to cover them (`panel_to_floor` for a wall standing on the floor); `show_for(player)` shows MK levels and owned weapons, `get_order` is the hanging order, `get_top` the panel's top edge, `get_half_width` half its width; `neighbor(part, direction)` and `row_start(part, step)` move a highlight over it as a grid (skipping hidden weapons). `WeaponWall` (`game/stations/loadout/weapon_wall.tscn`, in the middle of the armory) is one that shows its `viewer` (the player using a locker whose `wall` it is, else the first local player) and their loadout's slot numbers under the weapons (`get_tag`).
- **Lockers:** `LoadoutStation` (`game/stations/loadout/loadout_locker.tscn`, four in the armory, `number` 1–4, each with the armory's `wall`) with a `LoadoutCard`. Its parts are a part per cradle (`Cradle1`/`Cradle2`, `get_cradle_part`, a display of the weapon in that slot, hidden while empty, selected first on opening) and the wall's weapons (`get_part`); a selected weapon in a cradle stays selected when it moves (the other cradle, or the wall). It relays the cradles whenever the loadout or the MK levels change. While a wall weapon is selected (`is_on_wall`), `_card_position` puts the card above the wall over it (`CARD_GAP`), and the camera frames the weapon and the card (`get_view_center`, `wall_view_height`, also checked by the readable text rule). `_neighbor` moves along the wall's grid and between it and the cradles, `_cycle` jumps a row. Selecting a cradle selects its slot, and an empty slot becomes `selected_slot` whenever the loadout changes (framed amber on the card, not focused: grabbing the focus every frame cancels a mouse press on the other button), `toggle(weapon, slot)` changes the loadout (`Loadout.assign`) but never takes out the last weapon. Locker `number` belongs to the `number`th node in the players group (`get_owner_player`, `can_use`): the order players join in, until co-op gives them stable seats. The `Door` shows while a mission is underway or nobody owns the locker. `WeaponDisplay` (`game/weapons/weapon_display.gd`) makes the displayed weapons for the racks, bench and cradles, centers them on their holder (weapon models start at their grip) and shows the ones not owned see-through.
- **Navigator and flight console:** `NavigatorStation` (`game/stations/navigator/navigator.tscn`, at the bridge's holo table) has one continuous `zoom` (0: star system, 1: a body, 2: a landing site) that eases towards `zoom_target`; `set_zoom_target` stops short of what can't be reached (`UNREACHABLE_LIMIT`: the star, stations, locked bodies and regions) and shows the region's landing site (`Voyage.preview`) when the target reaches 2; choosing it is separate (`choose_destination` → `Voyage.choose`, which sets `Voyage.destination`; `Voyage.confirm` flies to the destination). It keeps its views and zoom when closed (the table goes on showing them) and builds the star system as soon as the mission is there, so the table shows it from the start; its `Use` reaches around the whole table. Its `level` (what the parts are) follows the target. Its `View` child is the `NavigatorHologram` (`navigator_hologram.gd`, which holds the system view's size and the globe's, `display_orbit`, and `get_routes` as typed `Route`s for the tests); it builds `System` (a hologram sphere per planet and moon of `Voyage.settings.system`, a ring-and-hub shape per station, moons around the body they orbit, an orbit ring per body, the star in the middle as a part whose item is the `StarSystem`; all its hologram materials fade together) and `Globe` (a sphere with `assets/shaders/region_globe.gdshader`: each surface point belongs to the nearest region center within `region_reach`, borders where regions meet and at their edge; selected and hovered regions are passed in as uniforms). `_show_zoom` (`NavigatorHologram.show_zoom`) scales each view around its anchor (`_zoom_into`: the body, the region's surface point) while moving the anchor to the center, and crossfades into the next view over the last quarter of each step; at the landing site it grows in the `HoloTable`'s location hologram (`set_hologram_scale`, `LANDING_SCALE` so the card beside it leaves it free). Picking on the globe is a ray against the sphere (`_part_at` → `NavigatorHologram.globe_part_at`), the card sits at a fixed spot beside the hologram (`_card_position`), `pause`/`ui_cancel` step back (`_back`), and the wheel arrives through `_shortcut` (new `Station` hooks); `zoom_in`/`zoom_out` (wheel, triggers, +/-) zoom gradually. `FlightStation` (`game/stations/flight/flight_console.tscn`, on the cockpit console) has the launch lever as its part; `launch` calls `Voyage.confirm`, and the lever's `Handle` tilts back during the flight. Both cards (`NavigatorCard`, `FlightCard`) refresh every frame from the mission's state. The world is data (`game/world/`: `StarSystem` → `CelestialBody` → `Region`, the greybox system in `game/world/star_system.tres`, referenced by `VoyageSettings.system`); `Voyage.roll_offers` makes one `MissionBrief` per open region (`StarSystem.open_regions`, `MissionBrief.for_region`: its biome, danger and length) with a new seed after every run, and `Voyage.offer_for(region)` finds it.
- **Skill graph:** `SkillStation` (`game/stations/skill_graph/skill_graph.tscn`, in the skills room) with a `SkillCard` (`game/stations/skill_graph/skill_card.tscn`). The skills are data (`game/progression/skills/`): a `Skill` (name, description, `effect`, `ranks` of `SkillRank`s with cost and value, the skill it `requires`, its `graph_position` in the cluster), `SkillCluster`s (name, color, `center` on the graph) in one `SkillTree` (`game/progression/skills/tree/skill_tree.tres`; each skill its own file, since the save refers to each by its UID), and each player's learned ranks in `Skills` (`Player.skills`; `value(effect)` adds up what the learned skills with that effect do). `Graph` (above the emitter) turns to face the camera every frame and holds a point per skill (a hologram sphere as its `StationPart`, rank pips below ranked ones), links from the required skill, the cluster names and a dark backdrop with a frame. The backdrop draws after the room's see-through things and the hologram after the backdrop (`render_priority`); while used, the backdrop also skips the depth test, since the floor markings below the tilted graph are nearer the camera than it. `can_learn`/`learn` pay the next rank from the hold (locked during a mission, like the upgrades). The view: `Graph/Focus` is the `view_focus`, moved by `set_pan` (kept within `graph_bounds`), and `set_zoom` scales `view_height` (down to `MIN_ZOOM`) around a graph point; `_card_scale` (a new `Station` hook) scales the card with the zoom, so it keeps its size on screen and passes the text size check at any zoom. Mouse: a press away from the card that moves more than `DRAG_THRESHOLD` pans (`_shortcut`, with a ray against the graph's plane), the wheel zooms around the cursor; `zoom_in`/`zoom_out` held zoom around the selected skill; `select` pans to keep the selected skill in view (`_follow`); `_cycle` jumps to the next cluster's root and centers on the cluster. The card goes to `card_spot`: beside the selected skill, trying inwards then outwards, level then half a card down or up, then pushed further sideways, and taking the first spot that covers no other skill and stays in `view_rect` (else the one covering fewest). Closing resets zoom and pan.
- **Bed:** `BedStation` (`game/stations/bed/bed.tscn`, one per player cabin over the cabin's bed prop, `number` 1–4, the beds against the near wall turned around so the use spot is at the foot) with a `BedCard`. `sleep` saves (`SaveGame.save_progress`, not while a mission is underway), hides the card (`Station.show_card`), fades a full-screen black `ColorRect` in (`FADE_TIME`; a transition, not a HUD) with the camera still on the bed, then closes and quits (`quits`, off in tests); the saving, fading and quitting are `ScreenFade.quit_game` (`game/ui/screen_fade.gd`), shared with the pause panel. Ownership uses `Station.nth_player`, shared with the lockers.
- **Heat shield:** `HeatShieldStation` (`game/stations/heat_shield/drive_core.tscn`, in the engine room) with a `HeatShieldCard`. The shield is data and state: `ShieldPlate`s (their cost) in `game/progression/heat_shield/plates.tres` (`ShieldPlates`), and the ship's `HeatShield` (`Ship.heat_shield`: `mk`, `project`, `paid`; `reaches(body)`, `missing`, `take_from` pays and installs, `level_changed` when the MK level changes). `Ship.store_haul` (called by `Mission.bank`, which takes the living players' cargo as one haul; a won run banks everyone aboard) lets the project take its part first; `Ship.pay_shield` pays from the hold. Reach replaces a flag per region: `CelestialBody.is_reachable(shield_mk)`, `StarSystem.open_regions(shield_mk)` and `is_open`; `Mission` offers the newly open regions on `level_changed`, and the navigator rebuilds its hologram. The station builds its plates in code (a closed ring mesh per plate, so the selection outline is a border; the fill is an arc over the camera's side, `FRONT_ARC`), each plate's `StationPart.item` its `ShieldPlate`; `is_host` is `multiplayer.is_server()` until co-op. `ShipFlight.fly(to_body)` takes the longer departure (`FlightSettings.travel_time`) when `Voyage.confirm` crosses to another body (`Voyage.ship_body`), with a sphere in the body's color growing behind the hull.
- **Hold:** `OreHold` (`game/stations/hold/ore_hold.gd`, `game/stations/hold/ore_hold.tscn`, in the cargo bay, its origin in the middle of the main lane) builds an `OreTank` (a `StationPart`: a greybox silo built in code, with a collision body and its own `use_point`) per ore of `OreCatalog`, in a cluster per band around a pump hub (sizes in `TANKS`, silos per row in `ROW_LENGTH`; the far row shifted by `window_shift`). The clusters behind the lane line up leftwards from `back_right_edge`, `WALK_WIDTH` apart; the signature cluster ends at `front_right_edge`. `get_clusters` lists them (the tests check the lane, the ways between and to the lift, the ramp button and the windows). It's a station without a `Use` child (`Station.use_point` is optional): a silo's `use_point` selects it and opens the hold. `_cycle` steps through `get_reading_order` (rows from the back, left to right). `get_view_center` follows the selected silo, and `Ship.countdown_hidden` hides the ramp's countdown while it's used. It shows a `PlayerHold` (`show_hold`): the user's while it's used, else the viewer's (`Ship.viewer`, or the first local player), following its `item_changed`; `PlayerHold.found` (saved with the player) keeps found ores known when emptied. `Ship.store_haul` puts each run's haul, after the heat shield's share, into every player's hold.
- **Refinery:** `Refinery` (`game/stations/refinery/refinery.tscn`, in the cargo bay's front corner, its `hold` the `OreHold`) holds four `FurnaceStation`s (`furnace.tscn`, `slot` 0–3) and an `IngotRack` (`ingot_rack.tscn`) in a row, builds the manifold over the flues and a pipe from each of the hold's `Hub`s (deferred, once the silos are built), and every `TICK` puts the bars every player's batches finished by then into their hold (`Refining.update(hold, now)`; `now` is always passed in, so tests run the clock). A furnace is a greybox built in code with its door as its one part; `_cycle` steps the recipe (`step_recipe`), the card's `BAR_BUTTONS` and `zoom_out`/`zoom_in` (`_shortcut`) call `step_bars` (`bars`, read through `get_bars`: 1 up to `get_max_bars`, what the hold pays for), the directions are the `Station`'s, `start`/`stop` pay and refund through `Refining` (`stop` only arms on the first press, `stop_press`), and it shows the `viewer`'s batch. `FurnaceCard` and `RackCard` show it; `RackShelf` stacks up to `SHOWN_BARS` bars in a `MultiMeshInstance3D` (`bar_position`) with a steady emissive material per alloy (`bar_material`; the silos' fill shader is too bright for a full shelf). The rack plays the clink on its shelf from the shown player's `Refining.bars_finished`; the furnace starts and stops its roar loop with the shown batch.
- **Ore exchange:** the rules are `OreExchange` (`game/ores/ore_exchange.gd`, static: `RATE`, `can_put_in`, `can_take_out`, `ores_in`/`ores_out` in catalog order, `affordable`, `trade` paying through `PlayerHold.pay`). `ExchangeStation` (`game/stations/exchange/ore_exchange.tscn`, placed on Ostra's depot counter by `ostra_build.py`, its origin the counter's middle on the deck) builds both hoppers in code as its two parts (`get_in_hopper`, `get_out_hopper`, both with the same card spot over the counter); `_cycle` and the card's arrows call `step_ore` (skipping the other hopper's ore), `COUNT_BUTTONS` and `zoom_out`/`zoom_in` call `step_count` (`count`, read through `get_count`: 1 up to `get_max_count`), a trade keeps `count`; `_opened` (which also sets `count` back to 1) and every trade re-pick valid ores (`_pick_valid`: in the one the hold has most of, out the commonest other one). `ExchangeCard` shows it; the sound is `stations/exchange`.
- **Pause panel:** `PausePanel` (`game/player/pause/pause_panel.tscn`, inheriting `world_panel.tscn`; `Player.pause_panel`, loaded in `_ready` since its scripts refer back to the player's) is a transparent `WorldPanel` that opens itself with `pause` when its `holder` has no menu (`can_open`), not through an `Interactable`. It runs while the game is paused (`PROCESS_MODE_ALWAYS`), keeps beside the player facing the camera (`_place`, with a `Focus` node between the two for the camera) and shows a `PauseCard` (`game/player/pause/pause_card.tscn`) with pages (`Page`: main, settings, credits, the quit question; `back` steps back, see below). Solo (`SaveGame.is_solo`) it sets `SceneTree.paused` while open (`pausing`) and lets go when someone joins or it leaves the tree; `NOTIFICATION_APPLICATION_FOCUS_OUT` opens it while `pause_on_focus_loss` (a static, off in the tests and for probes). `IsoCameraRig` runs while paused too (not shaking), so it frames the panel. `quit` asks first during a run (`loses_run`), then `ScreenFade.quit_game` (`quits`, off in tests). Tests that wait while paused set their own node to `PROCESS_MODE_ALWAYS` (GUT's awaiter is its child) and the map to `PROCESS_MODE_PAUSABLE`.
- **Pages:** a `WorldPanel`'s `pause`/`ui_cancel` call its menu's `back() -> bool` first when it has one and only close when that returns false (the pause card, the settings terminal's credits).
- **Settings and credits:** `SettingsList` (`game/ui/settings_list.tscn`: the language buttons, the voice language buttons (`VoiceLines.LANGUAGES` and "Off", the one spoken now lit), "Continue by itself" (`GameSettings.dialogue_auto_continue`, saved as `dialogue/auto_continue`), the suit ring's size buttons and its "Always show" switch, "Open folder" for the log files (`logs_folder`); `languages`, `current_language`, `LANGUAGE_NAMES`) and `SoundList` (`game/ui/sound_list.tscn`: a volume slider per bus in `Sfx.BUSES`, each playing a sample; the Voice bus `VoiceLines.play_sample`) are the settings components, both in the pause card (the pages `SETTINGS` and `SOUND`) and in the terminal's `SettingsMenu` (which adds "Reset progress" and the sound and credits pages, `show_sound`, `show_credits`). `Credits` (`game/ui/credits.tscn`, a `ScrollContainer`) names the team and the tools (`TEAM`, `TOOLS`) and adds Godot's license (`Engine.get_license_text`), its components' copyrights (`Engine.get_copyright_info`) and their license texts (`Engine.get_license_info`) at `SMALL_FONT_SIZE`; `ui_up`/`ui_down` scroll it by `scroll_step`, `scroll_speed` scrolls it by itself.
- **Boot screen:** `BootScreen` (`game/ui/boot/boot_screen.tscn`) is `run/main_scene`: a camera, the emblem (`assets/ui/boot/emblem.png` on a `Sprite3D`), the name (a `Label3D` like the title view's) and the ring (a quad with `boot_ring.gdshader`: a faint track, an arc filling clockwise from the top with `progress`) on black. It loads `scene_path` (`game/maps/main.tscn`) with `ResourceLoader.load_threaded_request` (without sub-threads: those loaded `player.glb`'s parts twice) and moves the ring towards the loading's progress by `fill_speed`; once both are done it instances the scene, gives it a `ScreenFade.from_black` (which waits `FADE_IN_DELAY` frames, since the first frames compile shaders and would skip the fade) and changes to it (`changes_scene`, off in the tests, which take the scene from `loaded`). Adding the map (its `_ready`s, ~1.5 s) and its first frame (~1.3 s) block the main thread, so the boot screen's last frame stays on the screen meanwhile. Loading on a worker thread leaves about 20 small objects leaked at exit ("ObjectDB instances were leaked"; scripts that preload a `.gdshader` when compiled on the thread, an engine issue). The **boot splash** (`application/boot_splash/image`, `assets/ui/boot/boot_splash.png`, 3840 × 2160 on black, fitted into the window keeping its aspect) is the boot screen rendered with its ring empty by `tools/boot_splash.sh` (`tools/boot_splash.gd`, needs a window); render it again after changing the boot screen's look. The emblem is generated (see Generated images).
- **Title view:** `TitleView` (`game/ui/title/title_view.tscn`, in `game/maps/main.tscn` at the hull's middle) is a `WorldMenu` the first player uses from the start (deferred), so they stand still; it turns to the camera's basis on opening (its children are laid out on the screen's plane: `Name` and `Prompt` `Label3D`s, `Credits` a `WorldPanel` drawn on top with a `title_credits.tscn`), snaps the camera there (`IsoCameraRig.snap`), and sets `Ship.show_outside` (the closed hull) and `Ship.countdown_hidden`. `Focus` drifts slowly (`drift`, `drift_period`). `is_button` (a key, mouse or joypad button press, not an echo) closes it; after `credits_delay` without input the credits scroll (`credits_speed`). `TitleView.shows` (static) is off in the tests (`tests/support/pre_run.gd`) and for probes that want the map right away.
- **Cards fit and read:** `tests/stations/test_menu_fit.gd` shows every upgrade on every station's card, every skill and rank on the skill card, every weapon on the locker card (and opens every terminal) in every language, and `tests/stations/test_speech_fit.gd` every crew member's and colonist's lines in their bubble and every state of their talk card, with the buttons disabled and enabled (the enabled style is wider), and fails when the content needs more than the layout (a card that's too wide spills over both edges of its screen and gets cut off; button rows are `HFlowContainer`s so they wrap instead); it also checks that body text (the theme's 30 px) is at least 2% of the framed view's height (`font size / pixels_per_meter / view_height`). So a card's `pixels_per_meter` follows its station's `view_height` (500 at 2.6–3 m, 400 at 3.6 m).
