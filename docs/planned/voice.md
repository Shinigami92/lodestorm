# Voice-over (planned)

Decided 2026-10-17, built after the art pass (see [`PLAN.md`](../PLAN.md), M5). All values **TBD (playtest)**. What's wanted (see [Localization and voice](../GAME_DESIGN.md#localization-and-voice)): English and German voice-over from a free AI text-to-speech model running locally, lines generated ahead of time, a license that allows selling the game, distinct voices that aren't cloned from real people without their consent; subtitles as speech bubbles, as today. The audio basics (the buses and the volume settings) are built (see [Sound](../GAME_DESIGN.md#sound)); voice adds its Voice and Radio buses.

## The spike (done 2026-10-24)

- **Result:** Qwen3-TTS. Its VoiceDesign model makes a character's anchor from the character's description, its Base model clones every line from that anchor (the cloned lines got 5 of 5 stars for both test characters in English, 4½ and 5 in German). Brannik and Nell (the female test character) have their anchors in both languages; the other candidates and why they lost, the tools and the setup: [`TECHNICAL.md`](../TECHNICAL.md#4-asset-pipeline) (Voice-over).
- **Candidates** (checked 2026-10-24 for the license of their code **and** weights): **Qwen3-TTS** 1.7B (Apache-2.0; 10 languages including German; a VoiceDesign model makes a voice from a text description, a Base model clones from a clip, also across languages), **VoxCPM2** (Apache-2.0; 2B, about 8 GB; 30 languages including German; voice design from a description and cloning, 48 kHz), **Chatterbox** multilingual (MIT; clones from a short clip, German), **Kokoro** (Apache-2.0; small and fast, ready-made voices, no cloning; the baseline), **Piper** (MIT engine, per-voice licenses; the light fallback). Ruled out: non-commercial weights (XTTS-v2, F5, Higgs Audio v3, OmniVoice, Echo-TTS, Voxtral TTS, Breeze TTS 2), Fish Audio Drama 3 (revenue cap and a "Built with Fish Audio" duty in its license, and a 35B model that doesn't fit a 16 GB GPU). Kokoro and Piper weren't rendered: neither clones from an anchor. The winner and the reason are in [`TECHNICAL.md`](../TECHNICAL.md), which later feeds Steam's AI disclosure (M8).
- **Voices without cloning real people:** no recordings of real people (also no public-domain readings: their readers never agreed to being cloned). Voices come from a model's own built-in or designed voices; where a model needs a reference clip, the clip is made by another model's synthetic voice, and each NPC's voice records where it came from (model, voice id, settings) next to its profile.
- **A voice anchor per character and language:** a character stays in their tone (timbre, pace, temper) because every line is cloned from the same **anchor**: a few clips (neutral, plus a strained or urgent one where the character needs it) picked once by ear from voice-design takes, committed with their transcript, the description that made them and the delivery notes (how the character speaks). The description alone is not enough: a new model or version turns the same description into a different voice, while the anchor clips stay the same. Each voice language has its own anchor, made from the same description with "native German speaker" (and so on) and picked by ear to sound like the same person: German cloned from an English anchor kept a heavy English accent with every model. About 15 voices in the end (8 crew, 4 station NPCs, a few spares), clearly apart in pitch, pace and timbre.
- **Writing a description** (what the spike's ratings taught): age, voice (pitch, texture), pace and temper, and the scene ("talking to a crew member across the counter"); "plain and conversational rather than narrating" (without it, voices read like a narrator); "native English speaker with a neutral accent" (without it, some came out with an accent); for female voices, warm but not breathy or seductive. Several seeds per description, picked by ear; voices that fit someone else are kept as spares.

## In the game

- **Languages:** text and voice languages are set apart (decided 2026-10-24): the voice language is its own setting next to the text language, so German text with English voices works, and the other way round. English voices first; a voice language only shows up in the setting once its lines are rendered. A German voice is the same character from the character's German anchor (see above); a line missing in the chosen voice language falls back to English.
- **The pipeline:** a dev tool (`tools/voice.sh`, running the model locally like the Blender scripts; heavy on the GPU) reads every line from the dialogue data and renders one file per line, voice and voice language (Ogg Vorbis) into `assets/voice/<npc>/<language>/`, named by a hash of the text, the anchor and the model with its version, so a changed line gets a new file and stale files are listed for removal. Moving to a new model or version therefore marks every line stale and the tool renders the whole cast again from the unchanged anchors; that's a rerun, and listening to each NPC again, not new voice work. Committed with Git LFS, not run in CI. Lines stay the English originals (the translation keys).
- **Playing it:** when a bubble shows a line, its file plays from the NPC (3D sound); moving on stops it and plays the next; a line without a file just shows the bubble, so missing audio never breaks anything. Radio lines play near the player through a radio filter (band-pass and a little static, an effect on a Radio bus, not baked into the files), so the same voice sounds different on the radio.
- **Settings:** a Voice and a Radio bus next to the earlier ones, their volumes in the settings terminal, "Voice on/off" and the voice language (default: the text language when it has voices, English otherwise).
- **Tests:** every voice file belongs to a current line (no orphans) and plays; a report (not a failure) of lines without audio, since the cast fills up over several steps.
- **AI-driven NPCs** stay a later, separate experiment (they share the GPU with rendering and need guardrails).
